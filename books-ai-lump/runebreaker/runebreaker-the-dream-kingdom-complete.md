### Part 1: Main Theme (The Living Room)

**Date:** 1992-10-30 at 20:15 America/New_York

Ocea tucked her bare feet underneath her faded denim jeans on the soft, worn living room carpet, leaning her shoulder against Tay's arm as the sixteen-bit fanfare of *Super Pixel Plumbers* chimed from the speakers of their curved-glass CRT television. Outside the bay window, an October wind rattled the dry autumn leaves along the street, but inside, the room was warm, lit only by a brass table lamp in the corner and the shifting phosphor glow of the screen.

At eighteen years old, she and Tay were fraternal twins—though Tay never missed an opportunity to remind her that he had entered the world ten minutes first. Despite their senior year of high school pulling them toward adulthood, Friday nights on the living room floor still belonged to the two of them.

"Watch the gap by the moving platform," Tay said, his thumb working the directional pad of Player One's grey plastic controller with practiced ease. Even in a simple cotton t-shirt and jeans, Tay carried a quiet, steady warmth that had anchored Ocea for as long as she could remember. Whenever the world outside got too loud or overwhelming, Tay was the older brother who wrapped her in breathless bear hugs and stayed until the static in her head cleared.

"I see it, I see it," Ocea laughed, pressing the jump button at the exact frame he had taught her earlier that evening. Her little pixel sprite cleared the pit and landed safely beside his on the checkered flagpole platform.

"Look at that," Tay grinned, setting his controller down on the carpet and bumping his shoulder against hers. "Frame-perfect. Told you that jump was all in the rhythm."

Ocea smiled, reaching into the front pocket of her jeans. Her fingers closed around a familiar, jagged piece of kilned clay painted a bright, glossy fire-engine red. They had sculpted the medallion together at summer camp years ago, scoring a zigzag line down the center before the clay dried so they could snap it cleanly into two interlocking halves.

"You still wearing yours?" she asked, holding up the red half so it caught the blue-white glow of the television screen.

Tay reached up to the collar of his t-shirt and tugged on a thin leather cord, pulling out the matching ocean-blue half. He held it edge-to-edge against hers for a second; the jagged ceramic teeth clicked together like a lock finding its key before he tucked it back beneath his shirt against his chest.

"Always," Tay said softly. "Ten minutes older means I'm on permanent lookout duty, remember?"

Ocea leaned her head against his shoulder, soaking in the quiet comfort of the room. Neither of them noticed the faint, violet spark that flickered across the black plastic casing of an unlabeled cartridge resting beside the console.

Halfway across an arched quartz bridge spanning a river of liquid starlight, Ocea stopped short. A jagged fissure of dead, static-filled black space had eaten right through the middle of the stone parapet. Purple runes—identical to the ones that had coiled around Tay in the rift—crawled along the edges of the crack like hungry insects, systematically deleting the color from the crystal and leaving cold, grey cinderblock in its wake.

Ocea crouched beside the fissure, careful not to let her bare toes touch the creeping violet static. When she drew the fire-engine red half of her summer-camp medallion from her pocket, a tiny spark of golden light leaped from the painted clay and struck the nearest purple rune.

Ahead, nestled inside a natural amphitheater of giant, sky-piercing mana crystals that acted as a shield against the creeping purple blight, rose the thatched roofs, spinning waterwheels, and cobblestone lanes of Sanctuary Village.
