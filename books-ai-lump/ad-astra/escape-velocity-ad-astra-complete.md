# Prologue: Escape Velocity (Movements I–III — Earth to the Stars)

## Movement I: The Cage (Industrial Park Road & Subterranean Silo Alpha)

### Industrial Park Road & Subterranean Silo Alpha

**Date:** 1996-07-16 at 21:30 America/New_York

The fluorescent ballast above the mixing desk in Studio A hummed at sixty cycles per second—a cold, relentless B-natural flat that had been drilling into the back of Cassidy O'Connell's skull for four years.

*The walls are cold, the air is thin. The noise outside is closing in.*

She stood with her forehead resting lightly against the cool double-paned glass of the control room window, looking out into the darkened live room of **The Fortress**. Outside the heavy corrugated-steel walls of the warehouse on **Industrial Park Road**, a warm July evening was settling over the southern edge of Blacksburg. Across the two-lane asphalt, behind a chain-link perimeter fence lit by amber high-pressure sodium lamps, the night-shift mechanics at the **Blue Ridge Transit** operations garage were parking the last of the cream-and-maroon **Stalwart** campus shuttle buses after their evening loops around **Commonwealth Green**. Every few minutes, the familiar pneumatic *hiss-clack* of an air brake bleeding down drifted across Industrial Park Road, followed by the distant murmur of impact wrenches in the bus maintenance bays.

That was why Holly had bought this old industrial warehouse at the edge of town in the first place. Out here among the sheet-metal fabrication shops, the limestone quarry sidings, and the Blue Ridge Transit bus yard, there were no residential subdivisions to call the town police when Evan and Tyler pushed a bass rig past a hundred and ten decibels at two in the morning. The alumni of **Commonwealth Polytechnic Institute**—the fiercely loyal **Ironheads** who had pooled their own money into the *Forger Nation War Chest* when the band walked out of Los Angeles—had helped them turn this cavernous brick-and-steel shell into a sanctuary.

And yet, tonight, even The Fortress felt like a cell.

"Cass?"

Cassidy didn't have to turn around to recognize the whisper of pneumatic rubber tires rolling across the hardwood floor behind the mixing console. In the dark reflection of the studio glass, she saw her older brother Ryan come to a smooth stop beside her left hip. He was twenty-four now, his dark hair tucked behind his ears, wearing a charcoal flight-crew jacket over a faded CPI Engineering tee. Across his lap rested his cherished cherry-sunburst **Sainte-Claire** solid-body electric guitar, already latched inside a pressurized carbon-fiber flight case.

"You're listening to the ballast transformer again," Ryan said softly.

"I can't unhear it, Ry," Cassidy admitted, her breath fogging a tiny crescent on the soundproof glass. Her eyes drifted to the corner of the lounge table, where a stack of trade magazines and legal briefs from the *Apex Records* litigation sat bound in red tape ready for the archive vault. "We won the verdict. We got our masters back. We built twelve-inch acoustic walls on Industrial Park Road so nobody could touch us. And every time I walk to the grocery store or turn on a radio, we're still the tabloid punchline. The *'wholesome girl'* with the hot chocolate. The *'victim-saints'* who nuked their own careers. Even winning in that courtroom left their fingerprints all over our skin."

Ryan reached out with his right hand and covered hers where it rested on the oak meter bridge of the console. His calloused fingertips were warm and steady.

"That's why we aren't staying in the courtroom, Cass," Ryan said. "And we aren't staying in the basement."

Just then, the heavy steel fire door at the back of the control room swung open. Their eldest sister, Holly O'Connell, stepped in carrying two ruggedized **MagnaPad** flight slates under her arm, followed closely by their twenty-one-year-old twin cousins, Tyler and Evan Wright, who were hauling the last two anodized aluminum road cases of master tape reels and atmospheric sensors between them.

"All surface ties are severed," Holly announced, her voice calm, brisk, and electric with a quiet gravity she had been holding in since the day the *Friction* subpoenas were served. "Power grid transfer to internal geothermal is complete. If anyone from Los Angeles comes knocking on Industrial Park Road tomorrow morning, all they're going to find is a locked rehearsal warehouse and a post-office box. Are we ready to go downstairs?"

Cassidy looked once more at the buzzing fluorescent tube overhead, then reached over to the master wall panel and flipped the studio breaker off.

The sixty-cycle hum died instantly.

"Take us down, Hols," Cassidy said.

***

#### Two Hundred Feet Beneath Industrial Park Road

To anyone driving past the Blue Ridge Transit garage on Industrial Park Road, the southern half of The Fortress appeared to be nothing more than an overgrown grading lot backed against a steep, wooded Appalachian limestone bluff.

What the county tax maps did not show was what lay two hundred feet beneath the warehouse slab.

Millions of years of subterranean groundwater flowing under the New River Valley had hollowed out a colossal karst dolomite cavern directly beneath the industrial park—a natural cathedral of dense, load-bearing Appalachian stone that **Stardust Strategic Assets** and a quiet coalition of CPI aerospace and civil-engineering alumni had spent four years reinforcing with arched titanium-steel ribs.

The five of them rolled and walked out of the back of Studio A into the warehouse's heavy freight bay. Evan punched a six-digit harmonic sequence into the keypad beside the oversized cargo lift. With a deep hydraulic hum, the twelve-by-twelve steel platform unlocked from the concrete floor and began its smooth descent through the bedrock.

For thirty seconds, rough-hewn walls of gray **Ironstone** and dolomite slipped past the safety grating, lit by recessed strips of cool cyan LEDs. Then the rock wall fell away, and Cassidy felt her breath catch in her chest.

> **FACILITY TELEMETRY // SUBTERRANEAN LAUNCH SILO ALPHA**<br>
> **Surface Coordinates:** Industrial Park Road, Blacksburg, VA (`Depth: -62.0 Meters MSL Buffer`)<br>
> **Geological Structure:** Reinforced Appalachian Karst Dolomite Cavern & Quarry-Bluff Exhaust Shaft<br>
> **Berthed Vessel:** Deep-Space Acoustic Cruiser ***U.S.S. Aethelgard*** (`Registry: ERR-NCC-1996`)<br>
> **Propulsion Architecture:** Dual Sub-Light Acoustic-Fusion Thrusters + **Harmonic Continuum FTL Drive**<br>
> **Internal Deck Gravity:** **1.00G** (`9.81 m/s²` Artificial Gravity Deck Plates — Active)

The cavern was enormous—easily the size of **The Crucible** stadium bowl on campus, carved out of living mountain stone beneath a vaulted ceiling where camouflaged hydraulic blast doors blended seamlessly into the abandoned limestone quarry bluff above. Arcs of warm amber work-lights illuminated suspended catwalks, liquid-deuterium fuel lines, and acoustic baffling towers that lined the subterranean walls.

And resting in the center of the cavern floor on four massive hydraulic launch cradles was the ***U.S.S. Aethelgard***.

She was a sleek, swept-wing deep-space survey cruiser forged in matte charcoal titanium and brushed silver alloy, with twin acoustic-fusion nacelles faired smoothly into her rear hull and a glowing cyan Continuum collector ring nested beneath her forward nosecone. Along her dorsal spine, painted in crisp, unpretentious lettering beside the Engine Room Records gear emblem, was her registry: **`U.S.S. AETHELGARD • ERR-NCC-1996`**.

"Every single time the elevator clears the rock shelf," Tyler whistled softly, setting his end of the road case down as the lift locked flush with the polished ferro-concrete hangar deck, "I still half-expect to wake up in our dorm room at Pritchard."

"Don't wake up now, Ty," Evan grinned, slapping his twin brother on the shoulder as the elevator gate slid open. "You're flying port helm in twenty minutes."

At the stern of the *Aethelgard*, the wide aft cargo hatch stood open, its zero-lip titanium ramp resting flat against the hangar floor—the exact same threshold that would, seventy-two hours from tonight, open onto the purple shoreline of another star system.

#### Boarding & The Multipoint Flight Harness

They crossed the cavernous hangar deck together, the echo of four pairs of boots and the quiet purr of Ryan's high-pressure wheelchair tires rising toward the vaulted limestone roof. Up the wide aft cargo ramp they went, passing the amber-lit starboard telemetry console where Holly immediately docked her MagnaPad to initialize the pre-launch environmental seal.

From the aft cargo bay, a wide, obstacle-free companionway with flush brushed-titanium deck plates led straight forward to the main bridge and cockpit—designed from the very first blueprint with zero stairs, zero raised bulkhead coamings, and thirty-eight-inch clear pocket doors that parted automatically as Ryan's front casters approached.

Inside the forward cockpit, the geometry was built around the five-member family like a custom-tailored recording studio.

Ahead of them stretched a panoramic, octagonal forward viewport framed by overhead status matrices and environmental bus breakers. Flanking the glowing cyan-and-amber center command pedestal were two high-back, contoured charcoal leather flight seats fitted with five-point aviation harnesses: **Port Helm** on the left for Tyler, and **Starboard Astrogation** on the right for Evan. Just aft of the center pedestal, where a traditional captain's chair would have been bolted on an older vessel, the deck plates featured a precision-machined **Four-Point Magnetic & Mechanical Wheelchair Docking Lock** flanked by two swiveling jump-seats for Cassidy and Holly.

Ryan rolled smoothly into the center command position between Tyler and Evan's stations. As his rear wheels settled over the recessed titanium channels, two padded pneumatic clamps rose half an inch from the deck and engaged the frame hubs of his chair with a solid, reassuring *clack-lock*.

"`WHEELCHAIR FRAME LOCK: ENGAGED — RATED 18.0G LATERAL / 24.0G AXIAL`," the center console chimed in a warm, low-glare amber readout.

"Let's get your harness rigged before we seal the outer hatch, Ry," Evan said, stepping over from the starboard seat before strapping himself in.

Because atmospheric breakaway and high-G emergency maneuvers would subject everyone on the flight deck to heavy chest-to-back acceleration—and because a sudden inertial shear in orbit could pitch an unrestrained rider out of a manual seat—Ryan and the CPI mechanical engineering team had designed a custom **Six-Point Quick-Release Flight Harness System** integrated directly into the high-tensile backrest and frame uprights of his flight wheelchair.

Ryan reached over his shoulders first, pulling down the twin padded Kevlar-weave shoulder straps while Evan handed him the left and right pelvic lap belts from the lower frame anchors. Unlike restrictive medical straps—which Ryan had refused to let near his body since leaving the Roanoke rehab ward in 1990—this was pure aerospace hardware: matte-black anodized aluminum cam-lock buckles modeled after sprint-car and orbital shuttle crew restraints, complete with a single center-chest rotary quick-release puck that Ryan could pop open with a quarter-turn of his own wrist whenever he wanted.

*Click. Click. Click. Click.*

Ryan snapped the four outer tangs into the center rotary buckle at his sternum, then pulled the twin cinch tabs snug across his collarbone and hips until his back was locked firmly against the contoured lumbar cushion of his chair. He gave the shoulder harness a practiced two-finger tug. Neither he nor the wheelchair frame moved a millimeter relative to the deck plates.

"Harness green," Ryan reported, resting his forearms comfortably on the wrap-around armrest pads where secondary pitch, trim, and communications toggles were positioned directly beneath his fingertips. "Tether locked at one-point-zero Earth G."

"Aft cargo hatch sealing now," Holly called from the starboard companionway station, tapping the master pneumatic closure switch before taking her seat beside the environmental telemetry bus and buckling her own five-point harness. Behind them, the massive stern ramp lifted from the limestone floor of Silo Alpha and sealed into the hull with a deep, airtight *thump* that cut off the last echo of the underground cavern.

Cassidy buckled her five-point harness in the port-center mission seat right beside Ryan's left wheel. Through the octagonal forward viewport, the dark dolomite wall of the subterranean launch shaft rose two hundred feet toward the hidden quarry doors above Industrial Park Road.

For the first time since 1992, the air inside the cabin didn't smell like courthouse paper, ozone, or fear.

"Holly," Ryan said quietly from the center lock, his eyes fixed on the forward viewport. "Sound the silo clear."

## Movement II: Ignition (Ad Astra — Breaking the Tether & MECO)

### Ad Astra, Breaking the Tether & MECO

**Date:** 1996-07-16 at 23:45 America/New_York

On the center pedestal of the *U.S.S. Aethelgard*'s cockpit, the dark ambient idle of the pre-flight board was suddenly cut by a rapid, sixteenth-note pulse—a driving, crystal-locked `120.0 BPM` sequencer clock that lit the cyan and amber telemetry rings from port to starboard.

> **LAUNCH TELEMETRY // U.S.S. AETHELGARD (REGISTRY: ERR-NCC-1996)**<br>
> **Departure Vector:** Subterranean Launch Silo Alpha (`37.218° N, 80.413° W` — Industrial Park Road, Blacksburg, VA)<br>

From the center command lock between the twin high-back leather pilot seats, Ryan O'Connell felt the vibration before it ever reached the speakers.

Through the four titanium floor locks gripping the frame of his manual wheelchair, and through the snug Kevlar weave of his six-point flight harness across his chest and hips, the ship felt like an extension of his own spine. For six years since the rain-slicked asphalt of Route 460 at Mile Marker 98, the world had tried to tell him that mobility was something measured by what his legs couldn't do. Sitting here at the heart of the *Aethelgard*'s bridge—his left hand resting on the auxiliary throttle trim, his right hand hovering over the acoustic phase bus—he wasn't a passenger being carried anywhere. He was wired directly into forty thousand pounds of thrust.

"Silo acoustic dampeners flooding now," Holly called from the starboard systems console, her fingers flying across the glowing cyan touch-matrix as cooling water and nitrogen foam cascaded into the subterranean flame trench beneath the ship's tail. "Blue Ridge Transit just logged their final midnight route check across the street. Industrial Park Road is completely clear of surface traffic."

In the port helm seat to Ryan's left, Tyler Wright flipped the twin brass safety guards off the primary reaction-control switches. In the matching starboard seat to Ryan's right, his twin brother Evan locked in the azimuth coordinates for the orbital corridor above the Allegheny ridges. As always when the twins were locked into a rhythm—whether behind a drum kit and a bass rig or at the dual yokes of a starship—they barely needed full sentences to finish each other's thoughts.

"Inertial dampers at ninety-two percent," Tyler reported, leaving just enough physical acceleration in the cabin so they would feel the ship leave the mountain behind. "Pitch trim slaved to center lock."

"Upper silo blast doors unlatching," Evan confirmed, pointing up through the octagonal forward viewport.

Two hundred feet above the *Aethelgard*'s nosecone, the vaulted ceiling of the limestone cavern split down the center. Massive, counterweighted steel quarry doors—camouflaged on the surface with three feet of native Appalachian shale, moss, and scrub pine—parted silently on greased hydraulic tracks.

Through the widening rectangle of the shaft, the humid July night sky of Virginia appeared: a wash of high mountain stars glimmering above the dark silhouette of Price Mountain and the distant amber glow of the **Commonwealth Polytechnic Institute** campus.

Cassidy leaned forward against her five-point shoulder straps in the port-center mission seat beside Ryan. Her eyes were fixed on that patch of open sky above the rock walls, her voice rising clear and steady over the internal flight loop:

*"I see a signal in the endless black. A single light, no turning back."*

"T-minus ten," Holly read off the master chronometer as the four hydraulic launch cradles retracted into the cavern walls and the *Aethelgard* hovered on her vertical lift thrusters. "Nine. Eight. Internal pressure locked. Six. Five. Acoustic-fusion chambers at full resonance. Three. Two. One. *Ignition.*"

#### A Pillar of Fire in the Night

Ryan pushed the twin center throttle levers forward to the stops.

Deep within the rear nacelles, the acoustic-fusion injectors ignited in a blinding, focused column of crimson plasma. Inside the flooded limestone flame trench of Silo Alpha, the roar wasn't a dirty chemical explosion—it was a colossal, phase-coherent low D-sharp that shook the dust from fifty-million-year-old dolomite stalactites and turned the underground cavern into a cathedral of red fire.

*The "Engine" roars, a crimson light—a pillar of fire in the night!*

The *U.S.S. Aethelgard* leaped upward through the vertical shaft like an arrow released from a bowstring.

Instantly, the axial G-load surged against the cabin. Even with the artificial gravity deck plates and inertial dampers absorbing eighty percent of the sheer crushing force, a heavy, exhilarating 2.4-G pressure pressed Ryan squarely back into the contoured lumbar cushion of his wheelchair. His six-point harness held his shoulders and pelvis rock-steady against the backrest, zero lateral slip, zero strain on his core, leaving his hands completely free on the center armrest controls as the limestone walls of the shaft blurred into vertical ribbons of silver and shadow.

In less than three seconds, the nosecone breached the quarry ridge above Industrial Park Road and punched straight into the Virginia night.

Below their twin crimson exhaust plumes, the corrugated roof of The Fortress, the quiet rows of parked Blue Ridge Transit buses across the street, the limestone arches of **Keystone Bridge**, and the familiar streetlamps of **Progress Street** shrank into a glittering miniature map of memories.

"Clear of the ridge!" Tyler shouted over the thriving 120-BPM pulse of the flight instrumentation, banking the port yoke ten degrees east as the cruiser pitched into her steep trans-atmospheric climb. "Airspeed Mach 2... Mach 4... punching through the cirrus deck!"

Outside the octagonal forward viewport, the high summer clouds over the Blue Ridge tore apart like silver silk.

*The pressure builds, I'm pinned to the seat / My heart is pounding with the beat / The G-force pulling, a heavy stone / The last chain breaks, I'm not alone!*

Ryan glanced to his left. Cassidy's face was lit by the crimson-and-cyan glow of the upper status panels, her dark hair lifted slightly by the cabin air circulators, singing the ascent into the open telemetry recorder as if every legal brief, every tabloid headline, and every fluorescent ceiling tile in Los Angeles were burning up in the friction along their hull shields. To his right, Holly had one hand braced on her MagnaPad and the other gripping the edge of Ryan's armrest, her eyes shining with a fierce, triumphant pride as the altimeter spun past fifty kilometers, then eighty, then a hundred.

Outside the reinforced silica-quartz glass of the viewport, the deep indigo of the upper stratosphere thinned into violet, and then—all at once—gave way to the vast, diamond-studded velvet of the endless black.

"Kármán line crossed," Evan called out, his fingers dancing over the starboard astrogation keys as Earth's curvature dropped away beneath their belly in a sweeping arc of sapphire ocean and white cloud bands. "Passing one hundred and eighty kilometers. Velocity seven point nine kilometers per second... ten point two... approaching eleven point two kilometers per second."

"Escape velocity in three," Tyler counted down, his eyes locked on the glowing cyan vector ring in the center of the HUD. "Two. One. *Mark.*"

#### Main Engine Cutoff (MECO)

Back in Blacksburg, during the final night of recording the studio master of *"Escape Velocity (Ad Astra)"* inside Studio A, Ryan had wondered how to make a listener sitting in a bedroom with headphones truly understand the moment a spacecraft finishes tearing itself free of a planet's gravity well. A polite studio fade-out hadn't felt honest. Neither had a cymbal crash.

So at the climax of Movement II, with his vacuum-tube amplifier glowing cherry-red and shaking the floorboards of The Fortress, Ryan had reached down and physically yanked the quarter-inch instrument cable straight out of the amplifier's input jack—cutting the roar off mid-waveform with a raw, mechanical *clack* that dropped the studio into breathless, ringing silence.

Now, two hundred and forty miles above the Atlantic, Ryan reached forward to the master propulsion bus on the center pedestal.

"Main Engine Cutoff," Ryan said.

He pulled the twin crimson booster disconnects back to the mechanical detent.

*CLACK.*

In a single millisecond, the thunderous acoustic-fusion roar behind the cabin bulkhead vanished.

The 2.4-G chest pressure pressing them into their seats evaporated as the sub-light boosters went dark and the ship's internal deck plates settled back to a smooth, whisper-quiet `1.00G` cruise. Outside the octagonal forward viewport, the blinding crimson fire of the ascent was gone—replaced by the silent, majestic sweep of the Milky Way and the distant, pulsing cyan beacon of the **Harmonic Continuum** corridor ahead.

Nobody spoke for five full seconds. Inside the cockpit, the only sound was the quiet, synchronized breathing of five family members who had finally outrun the gravity of the old world.

*I'm floating now, the engine's off. The silence is a holy sound.*

Cassidy reached across the narrow gap between her mission seat and Ryan's left wheel, her fingers wrapping tightly around his hand.

"The Cage is gone, Ry," she whispered.

Ryan looked out through the forward viewport at the tiny blue marble receding behind their stern, then forward toward the stars waiting twelve hundred light-years ahead.

"Evan, Tyler," Ryan said softly, his voice steady in the quiet cabin. "Bring the Continuum coils online. Let's take her to the second star to the right."

## Movement III: The Cosmos (Zero-G Hugs & The Nebula Stream)

### Zero-G Hugs & The Nebula Stream at Continuum Factor 8.4

**Date:** 1996-07-17 at 00:15 America/New_York

Twelve thousand kilometers above the night side of Earth, the forward collector ring of the *U.S.S. Aethelgard* began to glow with a soft, crystalline cyan light.

Inside the cockpit, the sharp, driving telemetry of the launch sequence had given way to the vast, sweeping harmonic pads of **Movement III**. On the center console between Tyler and Evan's seats, the **Harmonic Continuum Drive** displayed a rising lattice of overtone ratios—locking the ship's field coils onto the twelve-hundred-light-year navigational corridor toward **Kepler-442c**.

> **FTL PROPULSION LOG // U.S.S. AETHELGARD (REGISTRY: ERR-NCC-1996)**<br>
> **Drive Architecture:** **Harmonic Continuum Field Coils** (Non-Relativistic Spatial Metric Folding)<br>

> **Deck Plate Gravity:** Transitioning from **`1.00G` (`9.81 m/s²`)** $\rightarrow$ **`0.00G` (`Zero-G Sanctuary Mode`)**

"Continuum field geometry is phase-locked," Evan announced from Starboard Astrogation, his eyes reflecting the violet-and-gold trajectory spline curving across his monitor. "No gravitational mass shadows along the corridor. Course laid in: bearing mark two-one-four by zero-zero-eight."

In the port seat, Tyler rested his right hand on the twin FTL engagement throttles and glanced back over his shoulder toward the center wheelchair lock.

"Your call, Captain," Tyler said with a quiet smile.

Ryan looked out through the octagonal forward viewport. He thought of all the weeknights they had spent as kids curled up on the living-room carpet after dinner, watching the captain of the flagship on television raise a single hand toward the main viewer before the stars stretched into rainbows. Back then, it had been a story that helped them survive school and hospitals. Tonight, the bridge was theirs.

"Engage," Ryan said.

Tyler slid the Continuum throttles forward.

There was no violent jerk, no ear-splitting roar, and no crushing acceleration. Instead, a deep, resonant chord—tuned to a warm, enveloping F-sharp major ninth—hummed softly through the titanium hull ribs. Outside the octagonal viewport, the pinpoint stars of the Milky Way elongated into luminous needles of gold, sapphire, and violet, and then the *U.S.S. Aethelgard* slipped smoothly through the threshold of the Continuum at **Factor 8.4**.

Rather than slamming through normal space like a battering ram, the Continuum coils wrapped the cruiser inside a stationary bubble of serene, uncurved space while folding the interstellar medium ahead and behind them in rhythmic harmonic intervals. Outside the reinforced quartz glass, great river-like currents of interstellar gas and ionized starlight flowed past the hull—majestic streams of deep crimson, electric blue, and royal amethyst drifting by like a cathedral of living stained glass while the ship glided forward in absolute peace.

*I see the colors of the nebula / A place I've only seen in dreams / The reds, the blues, the purples call / Flowing in majestic streams.*

"Cruising velocity stable at Continuum Factor eight-point-four," Holly reported from the starboard console, setting her MagnaPad down into its magnetic cradle. "Estimated transit to the Vespera system is seventy-two hours shipboard time. Autonavigation is locked in, and the inertial envelope is smooth as glass."

She turned in her seat and looked at Ryan, her expression softening.

"Deck plates are currently holding at one-point-zero Earth gravity, Ry," Holly said gently. "Do you want to keep Earth's weight on the floor, or do you want me to turn it off?"

#### Unbuckling the Tether in Zero-G

For six years—two thousand one hundred and ninety days since the rainy night a reckless driver had run them off Route 460 at Mile Marker 98—gravity had been a negotiation Ryan could never take a break from.

Even on his strongest days, even with custom titanium wheelchairs, transfer boards, and upper-body strength built from thousands of miles of pushing, Earth's `1.00G` pull was relentless. It compressed his spine into his seat cushion after twelve-hour studio sessions. It turned a four-inch concrete curb into a wall. And whenever the people he loved most in the world stood up to hug him, they always had to bend down over his armrests, angling their shoulders around his push-rims and brake levers so they wouldn't catch a jacket zipper or put awkward pressure on his knees.

Ryan looked down at the matte-black rotary quick-release puck of his six-point flight harness resting against his sternum.

"Turn it off, Hols," Ryan whispered. "All the way to zero."

Holly smiled, turned to the environmental control panel on the starboard bulkhead, and slid the master **Artificial Gravity Deck Plate** fader smoothly down from `1.00G` through `0.50G`, `0.20G`, and into the glowing cyan detent marked **`0.00G — SANCTUARY ZERO-G`**.

A soft three-note chime echoed through the cabin.

Across the cockpit, loose jacket drawstrings and the braided headphone cable coiled beside the mixing bus rose lazily into the air. In the port and starboard seats, Tyler, Evan, Cassidy, and Holly popped their five-point seat buckles and floated an inch above their leather cushions.

In the center command lock, Ryan's wheelchair remained firmly clamped to the titanium deck channels by its magnetic floor locks—which meant the chair itself stayed anchored like a solid launchpad while Ryan reached up with his right hand, gripped the center rotary puck of his six-point harness, and turned it forty-five degrees counter-clockwise.

*Click.*

All six harness tangs released at once.

Ryan didn't have to brace his triceps on his armrests or execute a seated transfer. He simply pushed lightly with two fingers against the anodized push-rims of his chair—and rose weightlessly into the center of the cabin.

*I'm floating now, the engine's off / The silence is a holy sound / The rage is gone, the fear is lost / In this new universe I've found.*

A quiet, breathless laugh escaped Ryan's throat as his boots drifted clear of the footrest plate. For the first time since he was eighteen years old, there was zero pressure on his lower back, zero weight pressing his hips into a cushion, and zero separation between where he stood and where his family stood. He floated upright in the warm violet-and-crimson light of the nebula streaming past the forward viewport—six feet tall again, suspended eye-to-eye with the four people who had carried him through the fire.

Cassidy was the first to reach him.

She didn't have to lean down over an armrest or twist sideways around a wheel. She simply pushed off gently from the port console, drifted straight into the center of the cabin, and wrapped both arms all the way around Ryan's neck as Ryan wrapped his arms tightly around her waist and shoulders—pulling his little sister into a full, unbroken, weightless embrace as they rotated slowly together in mid-air.

"I've got you, Ry," Cassidy whispered against his collar, her voice thick with happy tears. "Look at us. Not a single stone pulling us down."

"Not a single one, Cass," Ryan murmured, holding her close for a long, unhurried moment before Holly drifted over from the starboard bulkhead and wrapped her arms around both of them at once.

Holly—who had spent the last six years carrying the weight of every contract, every hospital bill, every ramp inspection, and every legal battle on her own shoulders—rested her forehead against Ryan's temple at exact eye level, her eyes squeezed shut as she held her brother and sister in the middle of the starlight.

Then two pairs of strong hands caught their shoulders from either side as Tyler and Evan floated out of the forward helm seats and piled into the embrace—all five of them linked together in a single, floating circle in the center of the cockpit while the crimson, blue, and purple currents of the Continuum glided past the glass.

"Group hug in zero-G," Evan grinned, his arm slung squarely across Ryan's shoulders while Tyler's hand gripped Ryan's forearm. "Try putting *that* in a courtroom deposition, Los Angeles."

#### The Solo in the Nebula Stream

On the second day of the seventy-two-hour transit—with the cabin still set to `0.00G` Sanctuary Mode so they could drift freely between the cockpit and the aft acoustic lounge—Ryan unlatched the pressurized carbon-fiber case anchored to the port bulkhead and floated his cherry-sunburst **Sainte-Claire** solid-body electric guitar out into the air.

He didn't even need a guitar strap to hold the mahogany weight against his collarbone; in zero gravity, the instrument hovered effortlessly at chest height while he clipped a coiled wireless transmitter into the output jack, braced the heel of his left boot lightly against a padded handrail next to the panoramic viewport, and switched on the cabin's studio monitor array.

Beside him, Cassidy floated with one hand resting lightly on the upper viewport frame, her eyes following a thirty-light-year ribbon of ionized hydrogen and violet dust unfurling across the dark.

Ryan touched the strings.

What poured through the *Aethelgard*'s cabin wasn't the jagged, defensive distortion of *The Cage*, and it wasn't the desperate, white-knuckle sprint of launch. It was the long, soaring, bell-clear guitar melody of **Movement III**—every bent note sustaining endlessly against the warm analog synth pad, full of pure, unguarded hope as the red, blue, and purple nebula currents washed across the glass.
