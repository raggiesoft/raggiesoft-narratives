### Scene Draft: The Third Floor Landing

The metal doors of the elevator slid shut, sealing David inside a mirrored box with the loudest sound in the world: Vesper’s voice.

"I don't even know why I bother," Vesper snapped, her reflection glaring at him from three different angles. "You just shut down. You're acting like a child, David. Look at me when I'm talking to you!"

The sensory overload of the bustling first floor had already frayed his nerves, but the echoing acoustics of Vesper's rage in the confined space was physically painful. The doors slid open to the third floor. The warm, fried-sugar scent of the food court washed over them, but David barely registered it. Standing right in front of the elevator doors, digging her car keys out of her purse, was Meredith.

She looked up, her expression morphing from mild distraction to absolute delight. "David!"

Before David could even process the coincidence, he was moving. He stepped out of the elevator and practically collapsed into his older cousin’s arms. Meredith hugged him dearly, wrapping her arms around his shoulders in the deep, grounding squeeze she knew he needed. David hugged her back just as fiercely, burying his face in her shoulder.

When Meredith pulled back to look at him, her smile vanished. David’s eyes were glassy, his jaw tight, and he was trembling. He looked like he was desperately fighting back tears.

"Well, isn't this sweet," a sharp voice sneered from the elevator threshold.

Meredith looked past David. Vesper stood there, arms crossed, staring daggers at Meredith. Her eyes raked over the twenty-year-old with naked hostility. "And who the hell are you?"

David swallowed hard, forcing the words out past the lump in his throat. "Vesper... this is Meredith. She's my cousin. She's my best friend." He looked down, overwhelmed. "My favorite."

Vesper let out a harsh, disbelieving laugh. "Your cousin. Right. You expect me to buy that? I turn my back for two seconds and you’re all over some girl at the mall?"

The sheer absurdity of the accusation, coupled with Vesper's venom, was too much. The words in David's head scrambled like television static. He couldn't defend himself. He stepped forward and pulled Meredith into a second hug, but this time, he ducked his head down right next to her ear.

"Merry," he whispered, his voice cracking. "I want to break up with her. Please help me."

Meredith's posture instantly shifted. She understood the assignment. She gave the back of David's neck a reassuring rub, gently tucked him slightly behind her, and locked eyes with Vesper. The warmth in Meredith's face was gone, replaced by a cold, protective steel.

"I'm his cousin, and his roommate, and his translator," Meredith said, her voice dangerously calm. "And right now, he's asking me to tell you that you're done. This date is over, and so are you two."

Vesper’s jaw dropped. "Excuse me? He doesn't get to—"

"He just did," Meredith interrupted, taking a step forward. "I don't know what you did to make him look like that, but you are going to turn around, get back in that elevator, and go pound sand. If I see you near him or our house, Vesper, you'll find out exactly how much of a favorite cousin I am."

Vesper sputtered, looking from Meredith's unflinching glare to David, who refused to meet her eye. With a dramatic scoff, she hit the lobby button. "Whatever. You're a freak anyway, David," she spat, just as the doors slid shut between them.

The silence on the landing was deafening. David let out a long, shuddering breath, the tension leaving his body so fast he almost swayed.

Meredith turned to him, her face softening instantly. "Hey," she said gently. "You okay, bud?"

David nodded slowly, though a single tear finally escaped down his cheek.

"Tell you what," Meredith said, slinging an arm around his shoulders and steering him toward the food court. "I know I just ate, but I could definitely go for some fries. Let's get you something good to eat, and then I'm driving you home."
