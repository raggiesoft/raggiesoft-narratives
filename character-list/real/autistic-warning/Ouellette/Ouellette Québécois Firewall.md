# Protocol: The Québécois Firewall

## 🛡️ The Objective
To deploy a linguistic shift that engages the prefrontal cortex, actively preventing an amygdala hijack (autistic meltdown/freeze response) upon visual or digital confirmation of an Ouellette twin at the scan station. 

By utilizing formal Québécois terminology, the interaction is completely depersonalized, reinforcing the "Corporate We" defense mechanism and establishing immediate emotional detachment.

---

## 🗣️ The Cognitive Separator (Pronunciation)

The pronunciation of the surname dictates the timeline and the threat level. 

* **The Ghost (2007-2009):** `Oh-let`
  * *Context:* The Americanized, intimate pronunciation. The Safe Person. The trigger.
  * *Status:* **DO NOT USE.**
* **The Patron (2026-Present):** `Well-ET`
  * *Context:* The formal Québécois pronunciation. A standard customer holding a facility membership.
  * *Status:* **ACTIVE PROTOCOL.** Focus on this phonetic structure upon reading the scan log.

---

## 📥 Arrival Protocols (The Greetings)

To be deployed the moment the 1:1 visual match is established or the scan log updates. Maintain professional, detached eye contact.

* **Bonjour** (Good Morning / Good Afternoon)
  * *Pronunciation:* Bohn-ZHOOR
  * *Usage:* The standard, impenetrable corporate greeting. Valid for almost the entire operating day.
* **Bienvenue** (Welcome)
  * *Pronunciation:* Bee-en-veh-NOO
  * *Usage:* The direct translation of "Welcome." Highly professional, highly detached. 
* **Salut** (Hi / Hello)
  * *Pronunciation:* Sah-LOO
  * *Usage:* Slightly more casual. Use only if a routine/regular patron dynamic is eventually established. The 't' is silent.

---

## 📤 Departure Protocols (The Farewells)

To be deployed when the patron crosses the scan threshold and proceeds into the facility, definitively ending the interaction on your terms.

* **Bonne après-midi** (Have a good afternoon)
  * *Pronunciation:* Bunn ah-preh mee-DEE
  * *Usage:* Strictly used as a dismissal/farewell during the afternoon shifts.
* **Bonne journée** (Have a good day)
  * *Pronunciation:* Bunn zhoor-NAY
  * *Usage:* The standard dismissal. Signals the absolute end of the customer service exchange.
