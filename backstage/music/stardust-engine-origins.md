Before [**The Stardust Engine**](https://stardust.raggiesoft.com) became the flagship progressive synth-rock force of [**Engine Room Records**](https://engineroom.raggiesoft.com), they were five college students crammed into a drafty, unheated garage at the corner of **Progress and Turner Streets** in **Blacksburg, Virginia**, just off the campus of [**Commonwealth Polytechnic Institute (CPI)**](/locations/cpi).

---

## 1. The True Meaning of "The Stardust Engine" (`1986`)

In the fall of **1986**, when twin cousins **Evan and Tyler Wright** transferred to CPI to join brother and sister **Ryan and Cassidy O'Connell** and their cousin **Holly O'Connell**, the very first song the four musicians wrote together was an anthemic synth-and-guitar odyssey titled **[*"The Stardust Engine"*](https://music.raggiesoft.com/artists/the-stardust-engine/1987-electric-color?track=1-13-the-stardust-engine)**.

At first, the band was almost too nervous to shop that song around to record labels—because it was so deeply personal to who they were as CPI science and engineering students. The title celebrated their two greatest shared passions:
* **"Stardust":** Their awe for the cosmos, stellar nucleosynthesis, and deep-space astrophysics.
* **"Engine":** The rockets, propulsion systems, and human engineering built to take you there.

When they released their debut album [***Electric Color***](https://music.raggiesoft.com/artists/the-stardust-engine/1987-electric-color) in the summer of **1987**, *"The Stardust Engine"* closed the record as Track 13.

### Why the Band Hated Apex Records' Corporate Nickname Gimmick
When **Apex Records** later signed the band, Apex's corporate marketing department tried to manufacture a cheap 1980s teen-idol branding gimmick—billing lead vocalist **Cassidy O'Connell** as *"Stardust"* and keyboardist/composer **Ryan O'Connell** as *"The Engine."*

Every member of the band **despised** that corporate branding gimmick, because reducing their band name to a pair of solo nicknames stripped away the actual meaning of the song: a five-person family united by their love of the cosmos (*Stardust*) and the rockets that carry humanity across the stars (*Engine*).

## 2. How *Electric Color* (`1987`) and *Starfield Calibration* (`1995`) Connect to *The Eleventh Child*

Twenty-eight years after *Electric Color* was pressed to vinyl in Blacksburg, its grooves spin once again in **Christiansburg, Virginia** on the Friday evening of **November 13, 2015** ([*Episode 6, Chapter 5: "An 80s Education"*](https://books.raggiesoft.com/aubrie/episode-6-fall-semester-2015/chapter-5-an-80s-education)):

1. **Awakening from a Grey World into *Electric Color*:**
   After **Mark Anderson** plays [*"An 80s Education"*](https://music.raggiesoft.com/artists/the-paper-wall/2003-eleventh-child-book-2?track=1-02-an-80s-education) in the living room of **412 Geneva Street**, **Daniel Brooks** pulls his original 1987 pressing of [***Electric Color***](https://music.raggiesoft.com/artists/the-stardust-engine/1987-electric-color) from his walnut vinyl crate. When the needle drops onto Track 1 ([*"Electric Color"*](https://music.raggiesoft.com/artists/the-stardust-engine/1987-electric-color?track=1-01-electric-color)), something profound awakens inside nineteen-year-old Jordan—hearing a song born right at CPI that declares his world is no longer grey, *just color*.
2. **Astrophysics & *Starfield Calibration* (`1995`):**
   Later that same night ([*Scene 4: "Starfield Calibration"*](https://books.raggiesoft.com/aubrie/episode-6-fall-semester-2015/chapter-5-an-80s-education/scene-4-starfield-calibration)), Daniel pulls out The Stardust Engine's 1995 concept album [***Starfield Calibration***](https://music.raggiesoft.com/artists/the-stardust-engine/1995-starfield-calibration)—a record inspired by deep-space optical navigation and how a spacecraft lost in the dark uses three fixed guide stars to triangulate its true position and plot its trajectory home. For Jordan and **Aubrie Jean Kelly**, both CPI Astrophysics majors who found their own guide stars in Suite 214/216, the album becomes the soundtrack of their lives.
