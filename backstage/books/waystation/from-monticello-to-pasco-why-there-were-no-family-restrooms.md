Across both the mainline Norfolk breakup ([**Chapter 1, Part 9**](https://books.raggiesoft.com/waystation/book-one-tidewater-and-studio-205/chapter-1-two-names-on-one-studio-lease/part-9-route-13-forty-eight-hours-notice-and-the-locker-alcove-on-monticello)) and the **Seattle Pathfinder Exodus** Story Crossroads routes of [***The Uncharted Waystation***](https://books.raggiesoft.com/waystation), the narrative camera stays locked strictly inside twenty-three-year-old **Nolan Lennox's** first-person point of view.

From Nolan's wheelchair, two moments three thousand miles apart appear unrelated at first glance:
1. At **`9:48 AM` on Tuesday, August 21, 2001**, inside the **Norfolk Pathfinder Terminal** on the seven-hundred block of Monticello Avenue, **Tammy** wheels Nolan behind a concrete structural column into a recessed steel coin-locker alcove, shields both sides with her unzipped pastel-blue cardigan, and flashes him for two silent seconds beneath her braless shelf-bra camisole.
2. Ninety hours later at **`7:45 AM` on Saturday, August 25, 2001**, inside the **Pasco Intermodal Terminal** in eastern Washington, Tammy suddenly guides exhausted, feverish Nolan toward a locking **single-occupancy family restroom** (`intercepted five minutes early by Gwen and Mallory Walsh on Sub-Route 2A, or carried out on Sub-Route 2B`).

Behind the camera, those two moments are actually the **beginning and end of the exact same plan**—delayed across nine states solely because Tammy had **zero knowledge of the floorplan of any Pathfinder terminal on the route**.

## 1. `9:48 AM` in Norfolk: Tammy Was Looking for a Family Bathroom, Not Coin Lockers

Because Tammy decided on the ninety-nine-dollar Seattle bus trip on pure Tuesday-morning impulse—never calling Pathfinder forty-eight hours ahead for a hydraulic wheelchair lift and never having stepped inside an intercity bus depot along the corridor—she walked through the glass doors on Monticello Avenue assuming the station would have a modern, single-room locking **family or companion bathroom** like a shopping mall.

When Nolan balked at hearing *"Seattle"* in front of the waiting room, Tammy didn't grab his rear wheelchair push-handles intending merely to flash him beside a payphone. **Tammy was fully prepared to wheel Nolan straight inside a locking family bathroom right there in Norfolk, throw the deadbolt, and have sex with him on the spot just to "prove" that the two of them were back together again**—using physical intimacy the exact way she had after Breakup #1 (`September 2000`) and Breakup #2 (`May 2001`) to erase every argument and cement her hold over him before buying the non-refundable cash tickets.

Rounding the thick poured-concrete column, Tammy hit a brick wall of **1961 mid-century architecture**:
* Designed and opened in **1961**—nearly thirty years before the Americans with Disabilities Act—the utilitarian Monticello Avenue terminal had **zero single-occupancy family or companion restrooms**.
* Beyond the rows of dented gray-and-blue steel coin luggage lockers sat only open-threshold tiled **MEN** and **WOMEN** multi-stall restrooms where strangers walked in and out every minute.

Forced to improvise in two seconds flat without a locking door, Tammy backed Nolan's wheelchair against the coin lockers, used her own back and the open lapels of her fleece cardigan as side blinders at Nolan's four-foot seated eye level, flashed him beneath her white ribbed shelf-bra camisole, and promised *"there's plenty more of that waiting for you"* down the line.

## 2. Ninety Hours Across Pre-ADA Depots (`Virginia` Through `Salt Lake City`)

On the **Seattle Pathfinder Exodus** branch—where literal-minded Nolan believes Tammy's velvet Week-One promise and Richmond payphone assurance—Tammy spent the next four days scanning every single transfer depot and highway rest stop along the ninety-six-hour route looking for that missing single-occupancy locking bathroom so she could reassert that they were still "together" as the miserable trip ground both of them down.

She never found one across the East, South, Midwest, or Rockies—because Tammy didn't know the layout of *any* of those facilities either:
* **Legacy Mid-Century Bus Terminals (`Richmond`, `Roanoke`, `Knoxville`, `Nashville`, `Downtown St. Louis`, `Denver`, `Salt Lake City`):** Every commercial downtown bus station along Legs 1 through 3 dated back to mid-twentieth-century construction with strictly segregated multi-stall *Men's* and *Women's* rooms, crowded transfer concourses, and zero private companion rooms.
* **Highway Stops & Tight Clocks (`Marion`, `St. Louis Municipal Airport`, `Colby`, `US-287`):** Ten- to fifteen-minute diesel stops left barely enough time for Nolan to haul himself down and up four steep ribbed-aluminum coach steps on his matte-silver forearm crutches—compounded outside St. Louis when Nolan's improperly stowed steel wheelchair legrests slid off the shallow overhead rack onto Tammy's head.

## 3. Why It Happens Specifically in `Pasco, Washington`

When their coach rolled into the **Pasco Intermodal Terminal** on Saturday morning, August 25, Tammy walked inside a completely different generation of transit building.

Constructed at the end of the **1990s** as a newly built, post-ADA intermodal rail-and-bus hub, **Pasco Intermodal Terminal was the very first facility Tammy and Nolan encountered on the entire three-thousand-mile trip from Norfolk that actually featured a locking single-occupancy Family / Companion Restroom** down its quiet rear corridor.

Spotting that single locking door near the end of the line triggered the exact plan Tammy had been carrying in her head ever since Tuesday morning in Norfolk—explaining why both nested Crossroads routes pivot on those exact five minutes in Pasco:

* **On Sub-Route 2A (*Intercept Before the Restroom*):** Because **Mallory Walsh** broke corporate HR's three-day waiting protocol on Wednesday night to alert **Gwen** and **Grandpa Walter**, Gwen and Mallory burst through Pasco's automatic glass doors off their *Meridian Airlines* flight **minutes before Tammy reaches that family-restroom latch**, rescuing Nolan cleanly in the open concourse.
* **On Sub-Route 2B (*Wait Until Friday's Third No-Show*):** Without anyone in Pasco to stop her, Tammy uses her soft Week-One voice about how much she missed being in bed with him to talk four-day-sleep-deprived, disoriented Nolan inside that locking family restroom—where Nolan lacks the cognitive capacity to consent under **Washington `RCW 9A.44.050(1)(b)`**.

## 4. Five Hours Later in Downtown Seattle: What Nolan's First-Person Camera Never Saw Outside the Glass Doors

When that plan backfires across the Cascades—leaving Nolan quietly crying for five straight hours through Yakima, Ellensburg, and Snoqualmie Pass as it dawns on him that what happened behind the Pasco lock was rape—three final off-camera details stay hidden outside Nolan's locked first-person perspective at the **Downtown Seattle Pathfinder Terminal**:

1. **The Other Half of the Payphone Argument:** Twenty feet away at the wall payphones, Nolan only hears Tammy's voice turn defensive and loud. On the other end of the line, Tammy's cousin—living in a cramped apartment down in **Kent**—is blindsided to learn Tammy showed up unannounced expecting to move an uninvited boyfriend with a manual wheelchair into a walk-up unit, flatly refusing to let Nolan come south and giving Tammy transit directions solely for herself.
2. **Where Tammy Stormed Off To (`TAPS Route 150` South Toward Kent):** All Nolan knows inside the concourse is that Tammy screamed they were through and stormed out the rain-streaked street doors. Outside on the downtown transit corridor, Tammy walked two wet blocks to catch **TAPS (`Transit Authority of the Puget Sound`) King County Route 150** southbound into the Kent valley—vanishing anonymously with zero Washington forwarding address long before Seattle Police could route Nolan's courtesy report across the Cascades to Pasco.
3. **Why the Pathfinder Ticket Agent Grabbed the Phone So Fast:** When Nolan unfolds his abandoned wheelchair and wheels himself crying to the accessible ticket window asking for a hospital and the police because he thinks he was raped in Pasco, Nolan himself still thinks his violent shivering and chattering teeth are just exhaustion and cold fifty-degree Seattle rain. Looking through the glass under bright counter lights, the veteran Pathfinder ticket agent immediately spots the unmistakable red malar flush, sweat sheen, and glassy eyes of a **`102.8°F` cellulitis fever** radiating from Nolan's undiagnosed stage-three sitting-bone pressure ulcer—prompting him to dial `911` on the spot for both an emergency aid car and Seattle Police, saving Nolan from full systemic sepsis before nightfall.
