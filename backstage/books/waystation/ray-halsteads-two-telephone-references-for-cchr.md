Across every storyline of [***The Uncharted Waystation***](https://books.raggiesoft.com/waystation), twenty-three-year-old **Nolan Lennox** lands the exact same life-changing role at the **Community College of Hampton Roads (`CCHR`) Norfolk Campus**: a twenty-eight-hour-a-week seated **Part-Time Campus Computer Lab and Helpdesk Technician** post inside the Martin Building, one six-minute ride down `CVTA Route 2` or `Route 11` from `Harborview Towers`.

And across every branch, the decisive voice that turns Nolan's four-minute hands-on TCP/IP and print-spooler diagnostic test into an immediate job offer at two dollars an hour above retail belongs to fifty-two-year-old retired U.S. Navy Chief Electronics Technician **Ray Halstead**—Nolan's department supervisor at Emerald Creek **Blue Tech (`Store #421`)**, whose five-year-old granddaughter is autistic and who understood Nolan's literal, hyper-dependable wiring from Day One.

Because the novel never leaves Nolan's perspective, readers never sit inside **CCHR Campus IT Director Marcus Brennan's** third-floor office when Brennan picks up his desk telephone to verify Nolan's employment. Depending on which **Story Crossroads** route the reader walked in August 2001, **two completely different telephone calls** unfold—requiring two very different kinds of leadership from Ray Halstead.

---

## Version 1: Mainline Norfolk & Gate Four Routes (`February 2002` — *Store #421 Back-Office Line*)
### *Context: Nolan Broke Up With Tammy in Norfolk and Never Missed a Single Shift*

On both the **Norfolk Terminal Route** ([**Chapter 1, Part 15**](https://books.raggiesoft.com/waystation/book-one-tidewater-and-studio-205/chapter-1-two-names-on-one-studio-lease/part-15-clocking-out-together-one-car-ride-home-and-crimson-red-outside-the-lobby)) and the **Gate Four Route**, Nolan escaped Tammy at the Norfolk Pathfinder station on Tuesday morning, August 21, 2001 without ever missing a shift. By February 2002, Nolan has worked **twenty-eight consecutive spotless months** at *Blue Tech* and only applies to `CCHR` because commuting from Norfolk's `Studio 205` back to Chesapeake in winter sleet takes three buses and nearly four hours round-trip for post-holiday four-hour shifts.

When CCHR IT Director Marcus Brennan dials Store #421's main number at `2:10 PM` and asks for Home Theater & Floor Supervisor Ray Halstead, Ray closes his office door with zero need to guard against corporate HR flags:

> **Brennan:** *"Mr. Halstead, this is Marcus Brennan over at CCHR's Norfolk Campus IT department. Nolan Lennox listed you as his current supervisor for our part-time Computer Lab Technician bench. Do you have two minutes?"*
>
> **Ray Halstead:** *"For Nolan Lennox, I've got ten, Mr. Brennan—though I'll warn you upfront you're trying to poach the single most reliable diagnostic technician in my building."*
>
> **Brennan:** *(laughs)* *"He cleared a broken gateway route and a hung NTFS print queue on our test rig in under four minutes. How is he day-to-day on reliability and working with non-technical users?"*
>
> **Ray Halstead:** *"Twenty-eight months on my roster—through two Black Fridays, hurricane warnings, and three-bus winter sleet commutes out of Norfolk—and that young man has not clocked in late or missed a single scheduled shift once. Not one. He's straightforward, literal, and rock-solid honest: if Nolan tells you a workstation kernel is clean, you can stake your server room on it, and when a frustrated freshman or retiree sits down in front of him, he never talks down to them or loses his temper. Frankly, watching him push half a mile across Emerald Creek Parkway in February freezing rain for retail hours has been tearing me up all winter. Putting him six minutes from his apartment at a seated college workbench is exactly what he's earned. Hire him today before somebody else gets smart—and know he'll give me every minute of a spotless two-week notice before he walks in your door."*

## Version 2: Pasco & Seattle Rescue Routes (`November 2001` — *Ray's Direct Home Landline Off the Clock*)
### *Context: Tammy Tricked Nolan Onto the Coast-to-Coast Bus, Triggering Corporate's Automated Rehire Freeze*

On both the **Pasco Rescue Route** and the **Seattle Rescue Route**, Nolan's employment record carries a landmine. Tricked onto Tammy's unreserved coast-to-coast Pathfinder coach on August 21, 2001 and physically blocked from every rest-stop payphone across the country, Nolan missed three consecutive Week A shifts (`Wednesday, Thursday, and Friday`), causing Blue Tech's automated corporate PeopleSoft system at headquarters to process an involuntary job-abandonment termination on Friday evening, August 24—automatically flagging Nolan's employee number **ineligible for rehire**.

Knowing any automated call to Blue Tech's `1-800` corporate verification line would immediately disqualify Nolan without context, Ray instructed Nolan over Mallory Walsh's cell phone right after his rescue **never** to list corporate's verification number—and to put down **Ray's direct home phone number** instead.

When Marcus Brennan dials that residential `757` number on his off-day in early November 2001, Ray walks a surgical tightrope: telling **100% honest truth** about why Nolan left *Blue Tech*, **protecting Nolan's medical and personal dignity** (never disclosing private abuse details, Pasco's restroom, or anything messy that could stigmatize a candidate), and explaining clearly why Nolan gave out a supervisor's home number:

> **Brennan:** *"Hello, is this Mr. Ray Halstead? This is Marcus Brennan calling from the IT department at the Community College of Hampton Roads in Norfolk. Nolan Lennox interviewed with us this morning and listed this number as his direct supervisor reference from Blue Tech."*
>
> **Ray Halstead:** *"Yes sir, Mr. Brennan—glad you called me directly. I'm a retired twenty-year Navy electronics chief and Nolan's department supervisor over at Store Four-Twenty-One in Chesapeake. I'm taking your call at home off the clock on purpose, and I want to be completely upfront with you about why."*
>
> **Brennan:** *"I appreciate candor, Chief. Go right ahead."*
>
> **Ray Halstead:** *"Nolan worked for me for twenty-two straight months—October ninety-nine right up to late August of this year—without a single late punch, write-up, or missed shift. Sharpest Quantum OS 2000 diagnostic eye in our district. In late August, through zero fault of his own, a former household member took Nolan out of state without wheelchair accessibility arrangements and left him stranded three thousand miles away without access to a telephone—landing him in medical care before his sister and our Computers supervisor flew out to bring him home."*
>
> **Brennan:** *"Good grief. Is he alright now?"*
>
> **Ray Halstead:** *"He's a hundred percent healed up and ready to work. Here's the bureaucratic catch I didn't want sinking a good kid: while Nolan was cut off from a phone for seventy-two hours, our corporate mainframe up at headquarters automatically logged three missed shifts as an unexcused separation with a corporate rehire lock before store management even knew he was in trouble. I appealed it to District HR myself with documentation, and corporate's software still doesn't let a store manager override an automated three-shift flag. That's why I told Nolan to give you my home line instead of our corporate verification number. If corporate handed me an override key this afternoon, I'd put Nolan back on my bench at eight-thirty tomorrow morning without blinking."*
>
> **Brennan:** *"That tells me everything I needed to hear—and it matches every impression he gave me at our test bench this morning. How is he under pressure around students?"*
>
> **Ray Halstead:** *"Calmest, most methodical technician you'll ever put behind a helpdesk. Give him clear expectations, a seated workbench for his Quantum OS imaging discs, and basic human respect, and he won't miss a beat for you. You're getting a gem, Mr. Brennan."*

Fifteen minutes after hanging up with Ray Halstead's living room, Marcus Brennan dialed Grandpa Walter's Chesapeake landline and offered Nolan the Martin Building campus technician bench at two dollars an hour more than retail ever paid.
