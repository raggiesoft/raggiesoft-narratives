## 1. Thursday, February 4, 2016 (`6:04 PM EST`) — `Hillside Court` & The Six-O'Clock News Shock

Two miles south of Downtown Richmond across the James River, tucked between Jefferson Davis Highway and Harwood Street in South Richmond, sat the brick low-rise rows of **Hillside Court** public housing.

On paper, Hillside Court was less than fifteen minutes by bus from Capitol Square. Psychologically, living south of the wide, cold expanse of the James River felt like living on another planet—especially when thirty-one-year-old **Tyler** kept the only car keys in his pocket, checked grocery receipts down to the penny, screamed whenever dinner was five minutes late, and reminded thirty-year-old **Brittany Fletcher** almost every week that she had burned every bridge back home in Virginia Beach fourteen years ago on New Year's Eve 2002. (Crucially, when Brittany had met Tyler four years earlier at a tire shop on Little Creek Road, she had **no idea** he was the exact same loudmouth senior from New Year's Eve 2002 who had stumbled down the Prairie hallway banging on Katie's locked bedroom door and maliciously lied to **Heather Collins** in senior homeroom on January 6, 2003 about who Nate was locked in that room with—because seventeen-year-old Brittany had been wasted in the living room that night and banned by Uncle David the next morning without ever hearing what happened in Heather's homeroom; otherwise she never would have started dating him in the first place. Only at `9:00 AM` on Saturday wedding morning—when the Richmond morning news replays Katie's Capitol clip panning past Nate in his wheelchair and Tyler laughs and brags about lying to Heather—will that sickening truth finally dawn on Brittany as the straw that breaks the camel's back, while Katie and Nate figure out who Tyler is the instant they lay eyes on him at the wedding in [***Part 4***](https://books.raggiesoft.com/katie/the-gentlewoman-from-virginia-beach-the-house-of-delegates/house-bill-663-live-on-meet-the-nation/part-4-via-southside-plaza-speaking-up-at-the-altar-taking-brittany-to-meet-the-nation-left-onto-macgregory-street).)

At four minutes past six on Thursday evening, February 4, 2016—forty-one hours before a Saturday afternoon church wedding in South Richmond that Brittany didn't even want to walk down the aisle for—Tyler was still across the living room scrolling his phone when the Richmond six-o'clock local broadcast cut straight to its lead story from the State Capitol:

> *"—a fiery floor exchange in the House of Delegates this afternoon over House Bill 663, the controversial bathroom bill restricting public restrooms by 'anatomical sex,' after Virginia Beach Delegate Katie Prairie brought the House Chamber to a standstill with a single question for the bill's patron."*

Standing by the tiny kitchen counter with a dish towel in her hands, Brittany froze.

Behind her thin silver wire-rimmed oval glasses, her eyes locked onto the small television screen. She already knew Katie and Nate used motorized `SwiftChair` power wheelchairs—she had stood on the far side of the uncut `Lions Way` curb at Northwood High School in eleventh grade watching them load onto the yellow lift bus. What stole every ounce of breath from Brittany's lungs was *where* her thirty-year-old cousin was sitting: upright in a royal-blue tailored blazer on the green carpet of the Virginia House of Delegates, holding up two printed pages of legislation beneath the brass chandelier of Thomas Jefferson's Capitol and fearlessly staring down a powerful committee patron on live television.

*That's Katie!* Brittany's mind raced, her heart slamming against her ribs as she pressed a trembling hand over her mouth so Tyler wouldn't see her gasp. *That's my cousin!! What is she doing in the House of Delegates!?*

Even more than the shock of seeing Katie's title on the lower-third television chyron (`DEL. KATIE PRAIRIE, D-VIRGINIA BEACH`), something else hit Brittany right in the chest: Katie wasn't across the Chesapeake Bay or two hours down Interstate 64 anymore. For the winter session of the General Assembly, Katie—and wherever Katie was, Nate was guaranteed to be right beside her—was **right here in the City of Richmond**, just across the Manchester Bridge.

That night, lying awake staring at the ceiling while bruise marks from earlier in the week still ached beneath her flannel shirt, Brittany made up her mind. She didn't have an appointment, she didn't know if Katie or Nate would slam the door in her face after fourteen years, and she knew she couldn't call from the apartment while Tyler was around. Tomorrow while Tyler was clocked in at the shop on Midlothian Turnpike, she was getting on the bus.

## 2. Friday, February 5, 2016 (`12:32 PM EST`) — `JRTC Route 74` (`"Via Southside Plaza"`) Across the James River

How does an ordinary citizen without a lobbyist badge or a scheduled appointment actually meet a Virginia State Delegate during session?

* **The Normal Capitol Routine (and the Katie Prairie Exception):** In ninety-nine out of one hundred offices in the General Assembly Building, walking in cold without an appointment means signing a visitor log at the outer reception desk, speaking for three minutes with a twenty-three-year-old Legislative Aide, leaving a yellow message slip, and hoping for a form letter in March—whereas anyone who called `Room 518` when Katie was at her desk usually got startled out of their shoes because Katie answered her own phone directly (*"Delegate Katie Prairie's office, this is Katie speaking"*).
* **Why Brittany Couldn't Call Ahead:** Calling from Southside risked missing Katie while she was down on the House Floor for the noon session—or losing her nerve completely if a staffer asked what bill number she was calling about. If she was going to beg for her life back, she had to look Katie and Nate in the eye.

At `12:32 PM` on Friday afternoon, wearing her dark winter coat over a plum sweater, jeans, and sneakers with her worn shoulder purse clutched against her side, Brittany stood at the Harwood Street bus stop outside Hillside Court as a blue-and-white forty-foot **`JRTC`** (*James River Transit Corporation*) low-floor bus rounded the corner heading inbound toward Downtown Richmond.

Anyone who ever rode transit south of the James River in Richmond knew **`JRTC`'s** unmistakable Southside obsession: making dead sure every single passenger on the sidewalk knew whether a bus was routing through **`Southside Plaza`** (`the massive mid-century Belt Boulevard & Hull Street transfer hub`). Above the windshield, the amber LED headsign flashed in alternating two-second frames—

> `74 OAK GROVE — DOWNTOWN`  
> `VIA SOUTHSIDE PLAZA`

—and the moment the folding front doors hissed open at the curb, the external speaker boomed across the block:

> *"Route 74. Downtown — via Southside Plaza. Welcome aboard JRTC!"*

Dropping exact change into the farebox, Brittany sat near the middle window with both hands knotted tightly in her lap as `Route 74` climbed out of Southside, rolled up Commerce Road onto the high steel deck of the **Manchester Bridge (`Ninth Street Bridge`)** high above the rocky rapids of the James River, and glided straight up Ninth Street to the brick sidewalk outside Capitol Square.

## 3. Friday, February 5, 2016 (`1:08 PM – 1:15 PM EST`) — Clearing Security, `Room 518`, & *"Hold the Elevator!"*

Stepping off the bus into the crisp February wind at Ninth and Broad, Brittany stared up at the towering limestone facade of the General Assembly Building.

For three full minutes she stood frozen by the curb, half convinced Capitol Police would ask why someone from Hillside Court without a suit jacket or an appointment calendar belonged inside. Taking one shaky breath, she walked through the heavy glass doors into the ground-floor security vestibule, set her purse into the gray plastic bin on the X-ray conveyor belt, and stepped cleanly through the metal detector archway.

"Have a good afternoon, ma'am," the Capitol Police officer said politely, sliding her purse down the rollers.

Brittany stepped past the stanchion into the high-ceilinged marble lobby and had to stop beside a fluted column just to catch her breath.

*She was in the General Assembly Building. She had actually done it.*

Steadying her glasses with two cold fingers, she walked up to the tall glass-framed legislative directory mounted beside the information desk and scanned down the alphabetical column of House members until her finger stopped on a single white plastic strip:

> `PRAIRIE, KATIE — 85TH DISTRICT — ROOM 518`

*Fifth floor,* Brittany told herself, swallowing hard as she looked left toward the gleaming brass doors of the main elevator bank. *Okay. Relax, Brittany. Elevators are right over there. Just go up to five, tell the receptionist you're her cousin from Virginia Beach, and if Katie tells you to leave, you walk quietly back down to the bus stop and go.*

Her knees feeling like water, she walked across the polished marble floor and pressed the brass `UP` call button.

Ten seconds later, a soft bell chimed above the right-hand shaft. The heavy brass doors slid open onto an empty wood-paneled elevator cab. Brittany stepped inside alone, clutched her purse strap with both hands like a lifeline—a complete nervous wreck—and watched the brass doors begin sliding smoothly shut, narrowing her view of the marble lobby down to a six-inch gap.

Right across the lobby echo came a young man's voice shouting at the top of his lungs:

***"HOLD THE ELEVATOR!"***

Pure reflex moved before Brittany's brain could catch up—thrusting her left hand straight between the closing brass doors to trigger the rubber safety edge and pop them wide open again, bringing her face-to-face with two compact black-and-silver `SwiftChair` power wheelchairs rolling straight toward the threshold.
