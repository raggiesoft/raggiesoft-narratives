## 1. 10:44 PM – 11:26 PM — The Unlocked-Door Camouflage Works Too Well

At `10:44 PM` on Tuesday night, March 13, 2007—five hours after classmate **Christopher Vance** struck Katie across the face in the ninety-two-degree therapeutic pool and Student Health taped sterile wound-closure strips and white gauze across the split bridge of her nose—Nate and Katie slip down the darkened rear corridor of the **Campus Recreation Hall** in their dry jeans, sneakers, and zip-up fleece hoodies.

Rolling their two collapsible black cross-brace manual wheelchairs (outfitted with right-armrest joysticks and removable under-seat battery packs while their heavy `SwiftChairs` stay home in Virginia Beach) inside the deep walk-in seasonal storage closet, Nate reaches back to twist the brass interior thumb-turn lock.

In this timeline, Katie catches his wrist in the dark before the bolt can click.

"Wait a second, Nate—don't lock it!" Katie whispers past the white medical tape across her nose, her analytical brain connecting two weeks of dining-hall gossip in a flash. "Why was this closet door unlocked when we just turned the knob? Maintenance leaves it unlocked! Every couple on campus who sneaks off to hook up locks the door behind them—if a staffer checks the hallway and feels a locked storage door, they'll pull out a master key in two seconds! Leave it unlocked, fold our chairs flat behind the winter-coat racks, and get behind the stage curtains!"

Moving with quiet twin teamwork in the cedar-scented dark:
1. Both twins **power off the ringers on their flip phones** so a stray text chime or low-battery beep can't give away their hiding spot.
2. They unclip both under-seat battery boxes, **collapse both cross-brace joystick wheelchair frames flat**, and slide them completely out of sight behind a waist-high row of lumpy canvas winter-coat bags.
3. Behind the coat racks in the far back corner, they arrange two soft foam bundles on the linoleum, drape a heavy folded burgundy velvet stage curtain warmly over their shoulders, and wrap their arms tightly around each other.

At **`11:25 PM`**, footsteps echo down the rear Rec Hall corridor right on schedule after Barton Hall's `11:00 PM` bed check finds both of their dorm beds empty.

Katie and Nate hold their breath behind the stage curtains.

Outside, the **Night Supervisor** grabs the brass doorknob expecting the telltale resistance of two students locked inside. Instead, the knob turns Effortlessly. He cracks the door six inches without bothering to reach inside for the wall switch, sweeps a lazy half-second flashlight beam across the empty front linoleum and dusty canvas coat bags—seeing zero people and zero wheelchairs—mutters *"Nobody in the closet either—come on, let's check Cantor Hall,"* pulls the door shut, and **locks the outer Rec Hall doors for the night**.

Under the velvet curtain, Katie and Nate exchange a silent, triumphant squeeze in the dark and drift peacefully off to sleep—100% convinced that the absolute worst thing happening across the courtyard in Barton Hall is a dorm aide checking *Absent at 11:00 PM Bed Check* on tomorrow morning's clipboard.

## 2. 11:30 PM – 3:35 AM — Why Two Empty Beds Trigger a Statewide Missing-Persons Panic

Across the grass in **Barton Hall**, however, *not* catching Katie and Nate in the Rec Hall at `11:25 PM` turns the Night Supervisor's evening into a five-alarm institutional nightmare:

* **Midnight — Every Building & Walkway Comes Up Empty:**
  Staff sweep Cantor Hall's lounges, the Bates-Harding breezeways, the gazebo, and the parking lots. Nothing. Neither twin drives, neither signed out a yellow `Level 2` Pass, and rural Fishersville's daytime bus stopped running before supper. Outside the gates runs unlit, sidewalk-free **U.S. Route 250** in thirty-eight-degree Shenandoah Valley darkness, where tractor-trailers barrel past ditches at fifty-five miles per hour.
* **12:15 AM — Mandatory Missing Vulnerable Adult Protocol:**
  Under state rehabilitation regulations, two full-time wheelchair users unaccounted for sixty minutes past bed check—one of whom was treated at `7:30 PM` for an acute facial and nasal injury carrying concussion or secondary hemorrhage risk—cannot be shrugged off until morning. By `12:45 AM`, RRC Campus Police are sweeping Route 250 with cruiser spotlights and a pale Night Supervisor has to wake up **Center Director Dr. Evelyn Cross**.
* **1:15 AM — The Phone Call That Blows RRC's Cover-Up Sky-High:**
  In main canon, catching the twins at `11:25 PM` let RRC keep **David Prairie** completely in the dark. Here, before notifying the **Augusta County Sheriff's Office** and **Virginia State Police**, Dr. Cross has **no legal choice** at `1:15 AM` except to call forty-eight-year-old David Prairie at his new home on Sunset Drive in Alexandria—forcing her to stammer into the phone both that *a male student broke Katie's nose open in the pool that afternoon without anyone notifying him*, and that *both of his disabled twenty-one-year-old twins have vanished into the March night*.
  David immediately dials Katie's and Nate's flip phones. Inside a thick brick-and-concrete interior storage room with both ringers turned off, both calls go straight to voicemail. Within four minutes, David has thrown on his navy parka, grabbed the brass keys to his Dark Midnight Blue and Ultra Silver **`1991 Crestline Garrison 350` Crew Cab 4x4**, and roared west onto Interstate 66 toward Interstate 81 South.

## 3. 3:35 AM – 4:05 AM — Dad's Engineer Question & *"Who Went Missing?!"*

At **`3:35 AM`**, David's 1991 Crew Cab rumbles through the front gates of Roosevelt Rehabilitation Center, pulling right behind two **Augusta County Sheriff's** cruisers and a **Virginia State Police** sedan idling outside Barton Hall.

Stepping onto the cold concrete in front of a shaken **Dr. Evelyn Cross**, the **Night Supervisor**, and **Augusta County Sheriff's Lieutenant Miller**, David doesn't waste ten seconds on bureaucratic spin. After demanding the name of the boy who struck Katie in the pool, David listens for thirty seconds to the Night Supervisor's frantic theory that the twins might have rolled down U.S. Route 250 toward Staunton or Waynesboro—and cuts straight through the panic with calm systems-engineer logic:

> *"Stop right there. My kids don't own a car, their heavy outdoor powerchairs are back home in Virginia Beach, and neither Katie nor Nate would ever roll lightweight indoor joystick chairs down a gravel highway ditch in thirty-eight-degree pitch dark with a bandaged nose. Who was the last person on earth who saw them with his own eyes?"*

When the Rec Hall aide admits he saw them walk toward the Rec Hall restrooms at `10:42 PM` and never saw them exit the front glass doors—while the Night Supervisor protests, *"I checked the seasonal storage closet at eleven-twenty-five! The door was unlocked and empty!"*—David narrows his eyes:

> *"Did you walk inside that closet, flip the wall light switch on, and look behind the racks—or did you crack an unlocked door six inches in the dark?"*

The Night Supervisor's sudden two-second silence tells Lieutenant Miller everything he needs to know.

At **`3:50 AM`**, keys jingle down the silent rear corridor of the Rec Hall. David opens the unlocked solid-wood closet door wide, reaches inside to flip the wall toggle switch **ON**, and steps back half a pace beside Lieutenant Miller as the warm sixty-watt ceiling bulb floods the storage room.

In the back corner behind the winter-coat bags—right beside two neatly folded black joystick wheelchairs—two twenty-one-year-old twins wrapped to the chin in a burgundy velvet stage curtain scrunch their eyes shut against the sudden light, blink three times, and sit up groggily in their hoodies, jeans, and sneakers.

Having slept soundly for four and a half hours, **neither Katie nor Nate has the slightest clue that anyone on earth thinks they are missing.**

Rubbing his eyes in total bewilderment, Nate stares past a uniformed county sheriff's badge at his forty-eight-year-old father standing one hundred and fifty miles away from Alexandria at ten minutes to four in the morning.

"Dad?!" Nate blurts out, completely baffled. "What are you doing in a closet in Fishersville at four in the morning?! Wait—why is the Sheriff here? Did somebody go missing on campus?!"

Beside him, Katie blinks innocently past the white gauze strip across her nose, looking anxiously from Lieutenant Miller's flashlight to Dr. Cross in the hallway. "Did somebody run away from Barton Hall? We didn't hear anything back here!"

For two full seconds nobody in that doorway can speak—until forty-eight-year-old David Prairie lets out a ragged half-laugh, half-exhale that takes twenty years off his chest, drops straight to both knees on the linoleum beside the stage curtain, and pulls both of his bewildered twins into a fierce two-armed hug while Lieutenant Miller clicks off his flashlight and grins sideways at the beet-red Night Supervisor.

"Nobody ran away from Barton Hall, sweetheart," Lieutenant Miller chuckles, tipping his hat back an inch. "Half the Augusta County Sheriff's Office, the Virginia State Police, and your dad have been combing Route 250 since one in the morning looking for **the two of you**!"

Katie and Nate stare at each other with their mouths hanging open: *"US?! We were sixty yards across the grass taking a nap!"*

## 4. Wednesday Morning Aftermath: Pressing Criminal Charges & Saving Natalie by Accident

Once the sheer comedy of waking up inside their own missing-persons search settles—and David gets a close look under the ceiling bulb at the purple bruising and `MedStrip` tape across Katie's nose—every trace of humor leaves David Prairie's face.

Because **Augusta County Sheriff's Lieutenant Miller is already standing right inside the building**, Dr. Evelyn Cross has zero opportunity to bury Tuesday afternoon's natatorium assault in an internal administrative file:
1. **Criminal Arrest at Daybreak (`Preventing Wednesday Night's Attack on Natalie`):** Right there in the Rec Hall lobby at `4:15 AM`, Katie and Nate give Lieutenant Miller a sworn criminal complaint detailing **Christopher Vance's** unprovoked battery and destruction of Katie's swimsuit top in the therapeutic pool—plus the poolside staffer's threat to write Nate up for shielding his sister's chest until someone brought a towel. With David demanding formal charges on the spot, Augusta County deputies arrest Christopher Vance in Barton Hall at `7:00 AM` Wednesday morning—meaning Vance is sitting in the Augusta County jail long before Wednesday evening, **inadvertently sparing cognitively vulnerable classmate Natalie from the horrific Wednesday-night scrambled-keycard assault** that occurs in main canon ([***Episode 6, Chapter 3, Part 1***](https://books.raggiesoft.com/katie/twelve-days-at-roosevelt-rehabilitation-center-walking-out-for-good/the-dormitory-room-mix-up-blind-red-tape-room-reassignments-packing-up-barton-hall/part-1-the-dormitory-room-mix-up-blind-red-tape-room-reassignments-a-sleepless-night-for-natalie))!
2. **Packing Up Four Days Early (`With Tina's Parking-Lot Thumb Drive`):** Refusing to leave his twins under RRC's roof for one more hour, David backs his 1991 Crew Cab up to Barton Hall at `8:00 AM` Wednesday (`Day 10` instead of `Day 12`) to load their suitcases and hoist their wheelchairs beneath the Midnight Blue camper shell—just as sharp-eyed classmate **Tina** hurries across the walkway before Mr. Alderman's `8:30 AM` class, grins through her glasses at hearing how they accidentally mobilized two police departments with an unlocked doorknob, and presses a `2-gigabyte` USB thumb drive containing the entire rest of the **Web Applications Developer (`PHP` & `SQL`)** curriculum straight into Katie's palm before they ride up Interstate 81 for good.
