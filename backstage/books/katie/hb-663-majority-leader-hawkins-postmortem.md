## 1. *"Gentleman, This Embarrassment Was Entirely Preventable"*

From **House Majority Leader Richard Hawkins's** perspective—backed by twenty-four years of legislative instincts in the House of Delegates—what happened on the House floor on February 11, 2016 was not *"Katie Prairie embarrassed one of my members."*

It was: ***"Gentleman, this embarrassment was entirely preventable."***

What infuriated both Majority Leader Hawkins and the **Speaker of the House** was that the **Gentleman from Spotsylvania** (`HB 663`'s patron) had brought a bill onto the House calendar whose central operative concept (*"anatomical sex"*) collapsed under the very first implementation question—turning a partisan talking point into an institutional humiliation on live Capitol television.

---

## 2. The Closed-Door Dressing-Down in the Majority Leader's Office (`2:15 PM`)

Thirty minutes after moving that the House pass by `HB 663` for the day and re-referring it to the **Committee on Rules** to die quietly, Majority Leader Hawkins closed the heavy mahogany door of his Capitol office with the Gentleman from Spotsylvania and the Speaker of the House inside:

* **Hawkins:** *"You should've put this draft in front of Katie before you ever dropped it in the hopper."*
* **The Patron:** *"Prairie would've opposed it!"*
* **Hawkins:** *"Of course she would've opposed it! That's why you should've put it in front of her! In this building, you give your bill to the smartest person you know who is determined to kill it. If it survives her, you might actually have a statute."*
* Leaning across his desk, Hawkins delivered the line that became legend among the Capitol committee clerks:
  > *"Better yet, you should've given it to Katie and those three Pages who follow her around—my thirteen-year-old twin niece and nephews, Sarah, Mandy, and Sam. Because if three thirteen-year-olds eating lunch in Room 412 can immediately discover a question your legislation cannot answer, you sure as hell do not want to discover that while standing at your microphone on the House floor!"*

---

## 3. Why Majority Leader Hawkins Defended Katie on the Floor

When conservative members of his own caucus complained in the cloakroom that Delegate Prairie's blunt question on the floor (*asking whether she would have to lift her skirt and pull down her pants in front of a Capitol Police officer to prove her anatomical sex*) was *"indecorous,"* Majority Leader Hawkins shut them down cold:

1. **The Statute Introduced Anatomy—Not Katie:**
   As Hawkins pointed out on the floor and in caucus, the Gentleman from Spotsylvania didn't get to write a criminal and civil statute based on genital anatomy and then complain that the Gentlewoman from Virginia Beach was being indecorous by asking how the Commonwealth intended to verify genital anatomy.
2. **The Prairie-Triplets QA Standard:**
   For the rest of his tenure as Majority Leader until his retirement to Danville, whenever a member brought an half-baked cultural bill into leadership review without a defined enforcement mechanism, Richard Hawkins would tap the folder on his desk and ask one quiet question:
   > *"Did this survive the Prairie-Triplets QA Department, or is my thirteen-year-old niece going to embarrass us all on Tuesday?"*
