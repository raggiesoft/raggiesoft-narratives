## 1. Cousin Chelsea's Lease Protections on "Dirty Old Tonomy Hill" (`December 24–27, 2004`)

When nineteen-year-old **Katie, Nate, and Heather** and seventeen-year-old **Wesley** rode north up the Eastern Seaboard to **Newport, Rhode Island** for Christmas Eve 2004 after the passing of **Aunt Elizabeth** and **Grandma Ruth**, twenty-two-year-old **Chelsea** welcomed all four of them into her ground-floor Tonomy Hill apartment—forming an immediate, lifelong bond with Wesley over toy city buses and stuffed sea turtle *Shelly*:

1. **Chelsea Was the Sole Named Tenant on the Newport Housing Authority Lease:**
   The ground-floor unit in the World War II-era Tonomy Hill public-housing complex was **100% Cousin Chelsea's apartment**, leased solely in Chelsea's name through her Rhode Island adult developmental-disability caseworker after Aunt Liz passed away. Under Rhode Island landlord-tenant law and Newport Housing Authority guest rules, Chelsea held complete legal authority over who was welcome inside her front door—and only trusted family (`Uncle David, Katie, Nate, Heather, and Wesley`) had their names listed on her caseworker's approved family-visitor sheet.
2. **Why Tonomy Hill's Later Demolition Set Up King's College's 2008 Probate Fraud (`Episode 9, Chapter 4`):**
   When the Newport Housing Authority later demolished the aging Tonomy Hill complex and Chelsea struggled in an unsupported private apartment, King's College's **Regalis Community Living Foundation** in Braxton exploited her move by mailing notice to a house Uncle David had sold in 2001—falsely swearing on `Form PC-2.2` that Chelsea had *"No Known Living Next of Kin"* until David, Katie, Nate, and Brittany tore that perjured order apart in **Braxton Municipal Probate Court** in July 2016.

## 2. Two Transit Rules Brandon Mercer Never Bothered to Read (`October 16–17, 2004`)

Two months earlier in Williamsburg, Brandon Mercer prided himself on outsmarting everyone around him—yet his entire October 16 flight collapsed because a controlling abuser knew nothing about how rail tariffs or municipal cross-border transit actually work:

1. **Regular Adult Cash Fares vs. `PWD + Companion` Tariffs:**
   Walking up to the interior **ARTS** counter in Williamsburg ahead of Katie and Nate's Saturday day visit, Brandon bought Regular Adult one-way tickets toward Detroit (`DET`) without realizing rail carriers offer discounted *Passenger with a Disability (`PWD`) + Companion* fares—and without realizing Regular Adult promotional cash fares carry strict forfeiture rules unless exchanged intact at a staffed counter. When he spitefully tore Katie's unused ticket and purchase receipt in half on **East Jefferson Avenue** in Detroit before boarding the **Transit Windsor Tunnel Bus**, he threw away the only exchange credit that could have gotten him out of Michigan.
2. **Why Taking a City Bus Across an International Border Is *Harder*, Not Easier, Than Cross-Border Rail:**
   Afraid that boarding a direct New York-to-Toronto **ARTS** train would put his name on an advance electronic **CBSA** manifest while carrying two external hard drives full of skimmed credit-card numbers, Brandon assumed a local **Transit Windsor Tunnel Bus** worked like an ordinary city bus hop. In reality, walking into **`555 Goyeau Street`** and **`35 Park Street East`** off the Tunnel Bus with a fifty-pound duffel bag, zero return ticket, `$1.75` left after feeding his last `$10` U.S. bill into a no-change Canadian farebox that rejected American dimes and dollar coins, and printed notes about free **`OHIP`** provincial healthcare triggered an immediate **CBSA Secondary Inspection**—and guaranteed a mandatory warrantless **U.S. CBP** forensic search (`19 U.S.C. § 1582`) of both contraband hard drives the moment the caged CBSA van brought him back to **East Jefferson Avenue and Randolph Street**.
