## 1. The Legal Irony on "Dirty Old Tonomy Hill" (`December 24–27, 2004`)

When nineteen-year-old **Katie and Nate** rode eighteen bus stops up the Eastern Seaboard to **Newport, Rhode Island** so their twenty-two-year-old paternal cousin **Chelsea** wouldn't spend her first Christmas Eve alone after losing her mother **Aunt Elizabeth** and **Grandma Ruth**, **Tiffany Sloan** flatly refused to stay behind in Norfolk and tagged along—spending Christmas Eve screaming at Katie, Nate, and Chelsea inside Chelsea's own living room.

Underneath Tiffany's bullying sat a remarkable legal irony that Chelsea's severe developmental disability prevented her from realizing:

1. **Chelsea Was the Sole Named Tenant on the Newport Housing Authority Lease:**
   The ground-floor unit in the World War II-era Tonomy Hill public-housing complex was **100% Cousin Chelsea's apartment**, leased solely in Chelsea's name through her Rhode Island adult developmental-disability caseworker. Tiffany had zero legal standing on the lease and hadn't paid a dime toward rent or utilities.
2. **One Phone Call Would Have Put Tiffany on the Curb in the December Wind:**
   Under Rhode Island landlord-tenant law and Newport Housing Authority guest rules, Tiffany was purely a revocable houseguest. At any moment between Friday afternoon and Monday morning, Chelsea had the complete legal authority to pick up her wall phone, call her caseworker or the **Newport Police Department**, and say she wanted Tiffany removed. Within twenty minutes, Newport officers would have escorted Tiffany Sloan out onto the curb in the Rhode Island cold while Katie and Nate stayed warm inside with their cousin.
3. **How Chelsea Enforced Her Own Version of an Eviction Anyway:**
   Because Chelsea's developmental delays meant she did not grasp statutory lease rights—viewing loud, angry adults as scary authority figures—she never knew she could call the police to remove Tiffany. Instead, Chelsea improvised the fiercest eviction she knew how: pulling **both Katie's wheelchair and Nate's wheelchair** down the hallway into her bedroom, slamming the door straight in Tiffany's face (*"Just Nate an' Katie! Mean Tiffany stay out!"*), twisting the brass thumb-lock shut for three straight nights, and guiding Katie and Nate out to **`OSTA Route 60`** on **West Main Road** Monday morning so they wouldn't miss their five-minute Pathfinder connection in Fall River.

## 2. Two Transit Rules Brandon Mercer Never Bothered to Read (`October 16–17, 2004`)

Two months earlier in Williamsburg, Brandon Mercer prided himself on outsmarting everyone around him—yet his entire October 16 plan collapsed because a car-driving abuser knew nothing about how rail tariffs or municipal cross-border transit actually work:

1. **Regular Adult Cash Fares vs. `PWD + Companion` Tariffs:**
   Walking up to the blind interior **ARTS** counter at `468 North Boundary Street` while Katie and Nate waited outside, Brandon asked for two Regular Adult one-way tickets to Detroit (`DET`) without ever realizing rail carriers offer discounted *Passenger with a Disability (`PWD`) + Companion* fares—and without realizing Regular Adult promotional cash fares carry strict forfeiture rules unless exchanged intact at a staffed counter. When he spitefully tore Katie's unused ticket and purchase receipt in half on **East Jefferson Avenue** in Detroit before boarding the **Transit Windsor Tunnel Bus**, he threw away the only exchange credit that could have gotten him out of Michigan.
2. **Why Taking a City Bus Across an International Border Is *Harder*, Not Easier, Than Cross-Border Rail:**
   Afraid that boarding a direct New York-to-Toronto **ARTS** train would put his name on an advance electronic **CBSA** manifest while carrying two external hard drives full of skimmed credit-card numbers, Brandon assumed a local **Transit Windsor Tunnel Bus** worked like an ordinary city bus hop. In reality, walking into **`555 Goyeau Street`** and **`35 Park Street East`** off the Tunnel Bus with a fifty-pound duffel bag, zero return ticket, `$1.75` left after feeding his last `$10` U.S. bill into a no-change Canadian farebox that rejected American dimes and dollar coins, and printed notes about free **`OHIP`** provincial healthcare triggered an immediate **CBSA Secondary Inspection**—and guaranteed a mandatory warrantless **U.S. CBP** forensic search (`19 U.S.C. § 1582`) of both contraband hard drives the moment the caged CBSA van brought him back to **East Jefferson Avenue and Randolph Street**.
