## 1. Monday, January 6, 2003 (`7:48–8:03 AM`) — What Happened Inside Homeroom 212

When seventeen-year-old **Heather Collins** walked through the front doors of **Northwood High School** at `7:45 AM` on the first Monday back from winter break, she was carrying a holiday tin of homemade peppermint bark in her tote bag—half for **Nate** and half for **Katie**—brought back from her family's holiday trip home to Minnesota outside Saint Paul with **Aunt Linda** and her younger cousin **Wesley** *(and even if Heather and Wesley hadn't left for Minnesota over the holiday, rule-following Nate never would have invited them over on New Year's Eve in violation of David Prairie's strict no-guests house rule)*.

All autumn long, what had made Heather and Nate's relationship so warm was that Heather had never treated Katie like a third wheel. Having grown up fiercely protective of Wesley, Heather had sat on the tan-brown sofa on **Arvin Road** in August 2002, hugged Nate tightly beside Katie, and told him outright that his bond with his twin sister was the most important bond in his life.

Twelve minutes later inside **Senior Homeroom 212**, before the announcements came over the wall speaker, **Tyler** perched on the corner of a front desk in his varsity jacket holding court in front of twenty seniors about the unauthorized New Year's Eve party at **Brittany Fletcher's** cousins' house off Military Highway.

* **Heather's First Instinct (`7:52 AM`):** When Tyler started laughing about how *"wheelchair Nate wedged a chair under a back bedroom doorknob and locked himself inside all night with some girl while the party raged in the living room,"* Heather's immediate reaction was pure loyalty. She looked straight up from her desk in front of the entire homeroom and snapped, *"That was his twin sister Katie, you idiot! Neither one of them drinks, and they were obviously staying away from you guys!"*
* **Tyler's Malicious Escalation & Sleazebag Motive (`7:53 AM` & `Thursday, Jan 9`):** Furious at being called an idiot in front of twenty classmates—and unable to stand watching pretty Heather Collins date *"wheelchair Nate"* instead of him after Nate had shut Katie's bedroom door in his drunken face on New Year's Eve—Tyler ran the **exact same serial sleazebag breakup scam he pulled on every girl with a boyfriend whom he wanted to date**. Looking Heather dead in the eye with a practiced, pitying smirk, he doubled down and lied through his teeth: *"Keep telling yourself that, Collins! His sister wasn't the blonde girl carrying two red cups of vodka punch into that back room before he locked the door on us—everybody in the hall saw them!"* (Three mornings later, on Thursday, January 9, Tyler slid onto the front edge of Heather's homeroom desk smelling of cheap cologne to complete Stage Three of his routine—telling her *"Forget that loser, Collins, let me take you out Friday night so a real guy can treat you right"*—and even though Heather shot him down in disgust on the spot, she was still too proud to stop and question *why* he had wanted her single!)
* **Pride Over Trust (`7:54–8:05 AM`):** Snickers rippled across three rows of desks while two girls in the next aisle looked at Heather with wincing, secondhand pity. Stung to the bone by public humiliation, Heather made the worst mistake of her seventeen years: she let embarrassment in front of strangers override eighteen months of knowing Nathaniel Prairie's character. By the time she stormed out to her locker outside Room 204 at `8:04 AM`, she had tossed the tin of peppermint bark into the hallway trash bin and armored herself in cold fury—cutting off both Nate and Katie before they could get three sentences out and ordering Nate never to speak to her again.

## 2. January 8 – February 10, 2003 — Five Weeks Beside Two Empty Wheelchair Spaces

When Heather walked into **Mr. Callahan's** Gold-Day Second-Block Computer Lab (**Room 204**) at `9:09 AM` on Wednesday, January 8, she had steeled herself all morning for an awkward confrontation at the wide front accessible table (`Workstations One, Two, and Three`), where she, Nate, and Katie had earned a `100 / A+` together on December 20.

Instead, she walked in to find two empty spaces of waxed linoleum beside her plastic chair.

Right against the solid cinderblock rear corner wall beside the humming beige server cabinet (`Workstations Thirteen and Fourteen`), Katie and Nate were already logged into their two separate side-by-side CRT workstations so each twin could complete their individual coursework, sitting side by side in their compact black `SwiftChair` power wheelchairs with their inner hands laced together and their eyes fixed strictly on their own glowing screens while rows of classmates worked on essays and browser research ahead of them. Neither twin glared at her, passed notes about her, or complained to mutual classmates. Nate simply took *"Don't ever talk to me again"* at face value and vanished behind a quiet wall of dignity.

What Heather did not know as she stared at her screen at Workstation Three was that Nate didn't need a portable CD player or detention-inducing classroom headphones to drown out the silence of Room 204. Without Nate, Katie, or their father David ever knowing there was a clinical name for it, Nate's mind replayed Track Eight of **Crimson Node's** September 2002 debut album—***"Unhandled Exception"***—in crystal-clear, note-for-note studio fidelity inside his head while Katie (*"Player Two"*) held his hand across the gap between their armrests.

Meanwhile, up at Workstation Three, Heather's righteous anger lasted less than ten school days before an uncomfortable question began gnawing at her stomach:

* **Week 2 (`Jan 13–17`):** Every morning when **Mr. Henderson** and bus aide **Miss Gloria** unloaded **Bus 417** (the short yellow Special Needs lift school bus) at **Stairway A**—closest to the interior B-Wing elevator—every passing period in B-Wing, and every B-Lunch at **Table Twelve** in the Commons, Nate was only ever with Katie.
* **Week 3 (`Jan 21–24`):** Nobody from Brittany Fletcher's crowd ever mentioned a "blonde party girl" again—and Brittany herself looked miserable and hollow-eyed in the hallways after being banned from her Uncle David's house on New Year's Day.
* **Weeks 4 & 5 (`Jan 27 – Feb 10`):** Watching Nate quietly open zip-top bags of dry toasted oat cereal and green cans of ginger ale beside Katie at Table Twelve—the *exact* two things Nate had pleaded that he and Katie were eating behind that locked bedroom door on New Year's Eve—planted a cold, creeping dread in Heather's chest that she was terrified to examine too closely.

## 3. Tuesday, February 11, 2003 (`11:48 AM`) — Cousin Hannah's Green Parka & The Gut-Punch Truth

At twelve minutes before noon on Tuesday, February 11, 2003, that dread turned into sickening certainty in less than forty-five seconds.

Sitting at the end of **Table Ten** six feet across the aisle from Katie and Nate's wheelchairs at **Table Twelve**, Heather overheard **Tyler** lean across the adjacent varsity table toward senior girls' soccer captain **Kristen Bauer** using the *exact same lowered, fake-sympathetic voice* he had used in Homeroom 212 on January 6:

1. **The Identical Three-Beat Sleazebag Playbook (`Bait, Double-Down, & Rebound Pitch`):** First, Tyler told Kristen he *"hated to be the guy to tell her"* right before Valentine's Day, claiming he had walked behind the Kempsville Plaza movie theater at `9:30 PM` Saturday night and caught Kristen's boyfriend **Gavin Whitfield** hugging *"some pretty redhead in a forest-green parka"* beside his pickup truck before climbing into the cab together—immediately adding that Kristen should *"dump the zero before Friday and let me take you out for Valentine's Day instead."* When Kristen laughed and called him an idiot—telling him just as Heather had in Homeroom 212 that the girl was family, specifically Gavin's nineteen-year-old maternal cousin **Hannah** (`a student at Virginia Dominion University` who had blown a tire behind the cinema)—Tyler immediately fired the **exact same second-stage double-down lie** that had broken Heather on January 6, scoffing *"Keep telling yourself that, Bauer! I know what Gavin's cousin looks like—that wasn't his cousin! That was some totally different redhead... seriously, ditch him and come out with me Friday night!"*
2. **How Real Trust Sounded & Exposing That Tyler Does This to Every Woman:** Unlike Heather—who had cracked in Homeroom 212 the second Tyler insisted the blonde girl wasn't Katie—**Kristen Bauer and her varsity teammates blew Tyler's entire M.O. wide open** loud enough for four tables to hear: first Kristen nailed his bluff (`Tyler had never met Cousin Hannah in his life, Kristen had helped Gavin's mother pick out that forest-green parka at Emerald Creek Mall, and Kristen had been on three-way speakerphone with Gavin's mother and Hannah while Gavin bolted her spare tire on`), and then her soccer teammates laughed him right out of his seat—calling out that **every girl in the senior class knew Tyler pulled the exact same fake "I saw your boyfriend with another woman" breakup scam on every girl with a boyfriend whom he wanted to date** (`including two girls on varsity track the previous autumn`).
3. **The Last Time Tyler Ever Pulled That Stunt — & His Spring 2003 Pivot to Brittany at Smoker's Corner:** As Tyler slunk toward the dish-return window with his ears burning red, Kristen shook her head in disgust: *"What's sketchy is a total sleazebag making up trashy cheating stories about guys standing next to their own sisters and cousins just because nobody single will go near you! Everybody who actually knows Gavin knows the only women in his life are his mom, his cousin Hannah, and me!"* Getting publicly roasted by the entire Varsity Girls' Soccer team ended Tyler's fake-cheating scam for good—every mainstream girl inside Northwood High treated him like radioactive slime for the rest of senior year. Shut out inside the building, Tyler slunk out past the galvanized fence to **Smoker's Corner** on Macgregory Street, where shunned, guilt-ridden **Brittany Fletcher** (`who had skipped homeroom since New Year's Day and had zero idea what Tyler had done to Nate and Heather`) was cutting class alone—talking her into dropping out right after her eighteenth birthday in March 2003 and beginning the thirteen-year spiral that eventually trapped her in **Hillside Court** public housing in South Richmond (documented in [***Across the James River in Hillside Court***](/books/katie/hillside-court-six-oclock-news-and-holding-the-elevator)).

When Heather's metal spoon slipped from her fingers and clattered onto the cafeteria linoleum, the wave of self-disgust that washed over her made her physically nauseous.

Looking six feet across the aisle at Nathaniel and Katie sitting side by side in their black wheelchairs holding hands over pretzels and ginger ale, every detail—including Tyler leaning on her homeroom desk on January 9 asking to take her out on Friday night—locked together in Heather's mind with merciless clarity. Just as Tyler had doubled down trying to turn Gavin Whitfield's redhead cousin Hannah into *"some totally different redhead"* so he could hit on Kristen Bauer before Valentine's Day, Tyler had taken a two-second hallway glimpse of **blonde-haired Katie** retreating into her own bedroom on New Year's Eve and run his sleazy, copy-paste breakup routine on Heather because he wanted to break them up and date her himself. For five straight weeks across every classroom, hallway, lunch table, and bus loop at Northwood High, **the only woman—blonde or otherwise—anywhere near Nathaniel Prairie's life had ever been his twin sister Katie.** On New Year's Eve, Nate had done everything right: he had refused alcohol, protected his sister behind a locked bedroom door, and stayed completely faithful to her. And in return, because she couldn't bear two minutes of homeroom embarrassment when a serial sleazebag doubled down, she had ambushed Nate at his locker, called him a liar to his face while he begged her to listen, and thrown away eighteen months of trust in sixty seconds.

When Heather half-rose from her bench with tears stinging her eyes to cross the six feet to Table Twelve, Katie calmly met her gaze across the aisle and gave a single, quiet shake of her head while Nate returned his eyes to his transit notebook without looking up.

## 4. Spring & June 2003 — Stairway A, Wesley's Anger, & Graduation Day at the Pavilion

Twice over the spring months before graduation—once beside the library return slot in late February and again outside Room 204 on Thursday, April 10, two days after Katie and Nate turned eighteen—Heather stood three steps back from the hallway crowd holding a folded square of notebook paper on which she had written a full, unvarnished apology confessing that she knew Tyler had lied and that Nate and Katie had told her the absolute truth on January 6.

All spring, she never handed that folded paper to him, and she never crossed the covered walkway at `2:05 PM` dismissal to corner his wheelchair.

![At two-oh-five on a sunny April afternoon outside Northwood High School—where the two-story 1992 red-brick building and covered concrete walkway continue from Stairway A past Stairway B toward Stairway C beyond the left edge of the frame—eighteen-year-old twins Katie and Nathaniel Prairie roll side by side in matching black SwiftChair power wheelchairs along the wide concrete sidewalk outside Stairway A toward the lowered right-side hydraulic lift of short yellow Special Needs Bus 417 pulled alongside the unbroken yellow curb, while across the wide asphalt drive lane departing students exiting Stairways A, B, and C walk across the white crosswalk toward the regular yellow school buses parked side by side in their assigned numbered stalls and Heather Collins glances back over her shoulder with quiet sorrow.](https://assets.raggiesoft.com/raggiesoft-books/images/scenes/katie/b004/apr_2003_northwood_stairway_a_special_needs_bus_heather_regular_stalls.jpg)

At Northwood High (which opened in **1992**), afternoon bus dismissal physically mirrored the distance between them: every departing student exited through **Stairways A, B, and C** onto the long covered concrete walkway. Riding the interior elevator down from their separate fourth-block second-floor classes, **Katie and Nate** rolled out onto the wide concrete sidewalk outside **Stairway A** at the head of the walkway. Because there is **no curb ramp** at Stairway A, **Mr. Henderson** pulled **Bus 417**—their short yellow hydraulic-lift Special Needs school bus—right up on the asphalt curb lane **facing Stairway A** so the right-side wheelchair lift bridged over the unbroken yellow curb directly onto the concrete sidewalk, while **Heather** stepped off the covered walkway alongside the students streaming out of Stairways A, B, and C and crossed the wide asphalt drive lane **toward the regular full-size yellow school buses** parked side by side in their **assigned numbered diagonal parking stalls**.

What made those five months between January and June graduation hurt even more at home was **sixteen-year-old Wesley's** reaction from the very first week after Heather screamed at Nate and broke up with him:

* **Wesley's Fury Since January & His Only Photo With Nate:** Having grown up surrounded by Aunt Linda, Heather's mother, and Heather while teenage boys his age either stared or steered wide around him, **Nate was the very first real guy buddy Wesley had ever had in his life**, and Aunt Linda's framed November 2001 snapshot at *Annie's Pretzels* was **the only picture Wesley owned of himself and Nate together**. From the very first Saturday after Winter Break (`January 11, 2003`), when Heather had to admit they weren't meeting Nate and Katie at the mall anymore because she had screamed at Nate and broken up with him, **Wesley was furious with Heather**—taking that framed mall photo off the living-room shelf to hide it in his bedroom so Heather couldn't touch it, and relentlessly demanding through vocalized and non-vocalized cues that Heather go apologize to the twins.
* **Heather's Secret Longing & Nate's Silent Tears:** Every time Heather looked back toward Stairway A at `2:05 PM`, she missed Nate terribly—remembering his shy smile over cinnamon pretzels, his natural kindness toward Wesley, and how safe it had felt hugging him on the **Arvin Road** sofa and out on that December sidewalk. What Heather never saw as Bus 417's folding lift doors closed at Stairway A was the hot, silent tear slipping down Nathaniel's cheek while Katie quietly held his hand across the aisle.
* **Saturday, June 14, 2003 (`Virginia Beach Pavilion` — *Pomp and Circumstance* & One Strictly Bounded Chance for Wesley):** On graduation morning ([***Episode 4, Chapter 2, Part 4***](https://books.raggiesoft.com/katie/seventeen-senior-year-greenway-court/drunken-new-year/part-4-purple-gowns-at-the-pavilion-pomp-and-circumstance-heather-s-apology)), after sitting hopefully through forty minutes of *Pomp and Circumstance*, sixteen-year-old Wesley marched Heather across the Pavilion plaza to Katie and Nate's powerchairs. When Katie and Nate gently told Heather **no** to reforming a romantic relationship right as *Pomp and Circumstance* echoed out of the lobby, **it broke Wesley's heart right in front of them—setting off a lifelong trigger so that graduations forever remained sad events to Wesley**. Seeing innocent Wesley's tears broke the twins' hearts too, and when Heather explained that their family was **originally from Minnesota ("going home" on June 30)** and already planning for Heather to become Wesley's lifelong companion aide, guardian, and conservator (`since Wesley could never be out on his own`), Katie and Nate agreed to one strictly bounded Saturday at **Emerald Creek Mall** strictly for Wesley's sake.
* **Saturday, June 28, 2003 (`Emerald Creek Mall` — Backed Away From the Table, The Last Time in Person, & The Postcard Brandon Trashed):** Two weeks later ([***Episode 4, Chapter 2, Part 5***](https://books.raggiesoft.com/katie/seventeen-senior-year-greenway-court/drunken-new-year/part-5-waiting-by-the-route-15-stop-if-you-walked-away-wesley-s-laminated-buddy-card)), Heather drove Wesley to the **`CVTA Route 15`** stop and sat respectfully backed two feet away from their old corner table with her hands folded in her lap—gently stopping Wesley when he pleaded for her and Nate to hug it out (`even at the end of the afternoon, Wesley still wanted them to hug it out, though it was not meant to be`), publicly taking 100% of the blame when Homeroom 212 classmate **Lauren Mitchell** asked if they were getting back together, and watching quietly as Nate and Katie gave Wesley a miniature toy city bus and a laminated **Buddy Card** printed with their new **Greenway Court** address while **Wesley tried his hardest to understand that moving thirteen hundred miles home to Minnesota meant this would be the last time he ever saw Nate in person**. Across the next year in Minnesota, Wesley and Nate happily traded postcards—until **July 2004**, when **Aunt Linda's medical crisis** turned Wesley's guardianship and conservatorship over to Heather (`shortly before Heather met and started dating her future husband Dan Lindstrom as a package deal with Wesley`) and **Brandon Mercer** intercepted and trashed Wesley's Saint Paul address-update postcard in Williamsburg, cutting off their mail for twelve years while Katie and Nate lay in dark closets wondering if they should have let Heather back in after all.
