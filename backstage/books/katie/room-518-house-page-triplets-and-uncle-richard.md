## 1. Why the Triplets' Page Badges Read `HAWKINS` While the Majority Leader's Desk Read `CALLAWAY`

Capitol visitors who saw thirteen-year-old eighth-grade House Pages **Sam, Mandy, and Sarah Hawkins** eating their crustless sandwich quarters in `Room 518` with **Delegate Katie Prairie (`D-Virginia Beach`)** every day at noon often had no idea that the three quiet kids in navy-blue Page blazers were the niece and nephews of the most powerful Republican on the House floor: **House Majority Leader Richard Callaway (`R-Danville`)**.

1. **Maternal Uncle & The Early-2015 Kinship Rescue:**
   Sam, Mandy, and Sarah (`born October 2002`) were the children of Majority Leader Richard Callaway's younger sister, **Jenny Callaway Hawkins**, and her ex-husband **Wayne Hawkins**. All three triplets were diagnosed on the autism spectrum in elementary school. In early 2015, after Wayne Hawkins abandoned the family and Jenny suffered a severe medical and financial crisis that left the twelve-year-old triplets facing an abusive, chaotic household where they were screamed at whenever they had sensory overload, **Richard Callaway** and his wife **Aunt Marge** drove across Pittsylvania County in a snowstorm, petitioned the Juvenile and Domestic Relations District Court for permanent kinship custody, and brought all three triplets home to their farmhouse outside **Danville**.
2. **Why Uncle Richard Appointed Them to the House Page Class of 2016:**
   When the triplets turned thirteen in the fall of eighth grade, Uncle Richard and Aunt Marge arranged for all three of them to serve together in Richmond in the **House of Delegates Page Program** so they would never be separated during the sixty-day winter session—and privately asked **Delegate Katie Prairie** and **Nate** during the first week of January 2016 if the triplets could eat their quiet lunches inside `Room 518` away from the loud, echoey Capitol cafeteria.

## 2. Why Sam, Mandy, and Sarah Bolted From the Doorway of `Room 518` (`Friday, February 5, 2016, 9:40 AM`)

When the neighboring delegate's legislative assistant slammed open the door of `Room 518` and yelled at Katie and Nate over the ringing `Meet the Nation` phone line—triggering a full sensory-overload meltdown for both thirty-year-old twins—Sam, Mandy, and Sarah arrived at the open doorway with the `9:40 AM` floor packet.

To the mortified staffer in the hallway, it looked like three thirteen-year-old Pages had been frightened away by two crying adults in wheelchairs.

In reality, the exact opposite happened inside the triplets' heads:

* **Instant Autistic Recognition:**
  Being autistic themselves—and having spent the past year learning from Uncle Richard and Aunt Marge how to protect one another during sensory overload—Sam, Mandy, and Sarah recognized within half a second that Katie and Nate were having an **autistic meltdown caused by someone yelling at them in their safe space**.
* **Why They Didn't Step Inside `Room 518`:**
  The triplets knew from their own meltdowns that three more voices crowding into a small room would only make the sensory overload worse—and they also knew that three thirteen-year-old Pages didn't have the institutional authority to clear the fifth-floor hallway or lock down the corridor.
* **Straight to the One Adult Who Could Command the Building:**
  Without saying a word to the staffer, Sam, Mandy, and Sarah spun on their heels, sprinted down the marble stairs to the Majority Leader's suite, and burst straight past the reception desk to tell **Uncle Richard** (who immediately grabbed **Democratic Minority Leader Tom Toscano** down the hall):
  > *"Uncle Richard! Some jerk yelled at Katie and Nate in Room 518 and they're having a really bad meltdown and you have to go fix the hallway right now!"*
  Within three minutes, the Republican Majority Leader and the Democratic Minority Leader of the Virginia House of Delegates were standing shoulder to shoulder outside `Room 518` like two bouncers while Sam, Mandy, and Sarah slipped quietly under the worktable to sit on the carpet with Katie and Nate until the room felt safe again.

## 3. Inside Studio 6B: How *Saturday Night Sketch* Wrote the Viral `HB 663` Cold Open (`Saturday, February 6, 2016`)

Thirty-six hours later, while Katie, Nate, and their newly rescued cousin **Brittany Fletcher** were safely locked inside `Apartment 104` at the **Broad & Boulevard Lofts** in Richmond ahead of Sunday morning's `6:05 AM` train to Washington for ***Meet the Nation*** on `GBS`, the writing room on the eighth floor of **30 Rockefeller Plaza (`Studio 6B`)** in New York City was rewriting the **Saturday Night Sketch** cold open from scratch:

1. **Scrapping the Presidential Primary Cold Open on Friday Afternoon:**
   Originally, the February 6, 2016 cold open was slated to be a standard New Hampshire primary debate sketch. Then the `WCVE` floor clip of thirty-year-old **Delegate Katie Prairie** asking **Delegate Martin Cobb** whether she would have to lift her skirt and pull down her pants in front of a police officer to prove her *"anatomical sex"* crossed `18 million` views on Friday afternoon.
2. **The Cast's Strict Rule: Parody the Bill and the Patron, Never "Crip Up" to Mock the Wheelchair User:**
   In the Friday night table read, head writer **Claire Montgomery** laid down a non-negotiable rule for the sketch: *Saturday Night Sketch* would **not** put an able-bodied cast member in a prop wheelchair (`"cripping up"`) to impersonate Katie Prairie—because Katie wasn't the punchline of the story; **the patron's unenforceable bill was the punchline**.
3. **The `"Department of Anatomical Verification"` Sketch (`11:30 PM EST`):**
   Instead, the six-minute cold open opened inside a fictional Virginia Capitol committee room where a flustered **"Delegate Cobb"** attempts to demonstrate his new `"Virginia Restroom Anatomical Verification Kiosk"`—complete with a bewildered Capitol Police sergeant holding a ruler, a birth certificate, a magnifying glass, and a stack of $50 civil-penalty tickets—while three deadpan thirteen-year-old House Pages (`played by the show's three youngest cast members`) raise their hands every ten seconds to ask literal software-QA questions (*"Sir, what if someone left their chromosomes in their other pants?"*) until the fictional Speaker drops a giant wooden gavel from the ceiling and yells: ***"Live from New York, it's Saturday Night!"***
