## 3. `3:40 PM – 4:25 PM` — Who Resigned, Why They Left, & The Legal Difference Between Being *Fired* vs. Resigning

### The Legal & Career Difference Between Being *Fired* vs. Demanded Resignation in Virginia Government

In executive state governance, *"have your resignation on my desk before five o'clock"* is not a polite favor—it is a razor-sharp legal and administrative lever that differs dramatically from an involuntary firing:

1. **At-Pleasure Appointees (`Va. Code § 2.2-106`) vs. Agency Employees (`Virginia Personnel Act` & `DHRM Policy 1.60`):**
   * **Commissioner Marcus Sterling** was a direct gubernatorial appointee serving *"at the pleasure of the Governor"* (`Va. Code § 2.2-106` & `§ 51.5-131`), meaning Governor Langford could fire him unilaterally at `5:00 PM` with zero appeal rights—and Sterling resigned at `10:22 AM` solely to avoid having a public **Executive Order of Removal for Cause** (`citing his June 14 email and false statements to Congress`) permanently destroy his ability to sit on any board or hold any job in his field.
   * **Director Dr. Evelyn Cross** and **Assistant Director Greg Saunders**, by contrast, were **agency-appointed DARS administrators** governed by the **Virginia Personnel Act (`Va. Code § 2.2-2900`)** and **Department of Human Resource Management (`DHRM`) Policy `1.60` (*Standards of Conduct*)**. Firing a state facility employee involuntarily for a **Group III Offense (*Falsification of Official State/Federal Records*)** legally requires issuing a formal **Written Pre-Disciplinary Due-Process Notice**, placing them on suspension during a mandatory **24-to-48-hour employee response window**, and opening the door to **months of administrative hearings** under the **Virginia State Grievance Procedure (`Va. Code § 2.2-3000`)**.
   * **What a Signed Resignation Accomplishes Instantly:** The second an agency administrator signs a resignation letter effective `5:00 PM`, **100% of their statutory due-process waiting periods, grievance appeals, and litigation hooks vanish permanently**—allowing Acting Commissioner Angela Thornton to install an interim facility leadership team at `5:01 PM` the exact same day without six months of personnel hearings!
2. **Why Cross and Saunders Signed Instead of Fighting (`The Group III Guillotine`):**
   Sitting across the Bates-Harding conference table from **Arthur Lawrence**, **State Inspector General Dalton**, and **Acting Commissioner Thornton** between `3:40 PM` and `4:25 PM`, neither Cross nor Saunders resigned "out of politeness." They signed because forcing the Commonwealth to fire them carried catastrophic lifelong consequences:
   * **Dr. Evelyn Cross (`Signed at 3:48 PM`):** Cross had co-signed Line 2 of the false congressional compliance report, authored the May 3 *"wait until the July 29 markup"* faculty slide deck, and sat on VITA's March 24 classroom VLAN approval for 120 days while blaming VITA to a United States Senator. At `3:48 PM`, Cross signed a three-sentence resignation effective `5:00 PM`, packed one cardboard box alongside the cold travel mug of coffee she had walked through the front doors with at `9:00 AM`, and drove out of the staff lot at `4:12 PM`.
   * **Assistant Director Greg Saunders (`Signed in Lieu of Group III Termination at 4:20 PM`):** Confronted ten minutes later with (a) his co-signature on the internal Fishersville attestation worksheet, (b) June's **Purchase Order `#27-0894`** for 5,000 newly printed canary-yellow passes, and (c) the **July 15 front-lobby security video** catching him grabbing twenty-year-old Marcus's wheelchair push-handles without consent and threatening a retaliatory aggression write-up, Saunders learned that `OSIG`'s formal investigative report would name him personally for both **record falsification** and **retaliation against a disabled resident** if a Group III termination hearing went forward.
