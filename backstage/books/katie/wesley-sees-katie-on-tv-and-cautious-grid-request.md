## 1. Twelve Years on the Maplewood Dresser (`Why There Hadn't Been Any Postcards in a While`)

On the oak dresser inside **Wesley Sorenson's** bedroom in **Maplewood, Minnesota**, three small things from Virginia had occupied the exact same front-right corner through three apartments and a house purchase:

1. **Aunt Linda's Silver-Framed November 2001 Snapshot:** The single four-by-six film photograph taken at the upstairs *Annie's Pretzels* table in **Emerald Creek Mall**—the only picture in the world of Wesley with **Nathaniel Prairie**, his very first guy buddy.
2. **The Three-Inch Blue-and-White Miniature City Bus:** With its smooth die-cast body and tiny black rubber wheels still rolling smoothly whenever Wesley spun them against his palm beside his blue silicone ring.
3. **The Clear-Laminated June 2003 Buddy Card:** Stacked neatly atop the four Norfolk harbor and bus postcards Nate and Katie had mailed up from **Greenway Court** between July 2003 and June 2004.

Wesley did not count twelve years (`2004–2016`) on a calendar the way neurotypical adults did. To twenty-eight-year-old Wesley—who had lived full-time with **Heather** ever since **Aunt Linda's** sudden July 2004 medical crisis turned his legal guardianship, conservatorship, and lifelong companion care over to nineteen-year-old Heather (`months before Heather ever met her husband Dan Lindstrom on a Twin Cities campus bus as an explicit package deal with Wesley`)—it had simply been *"a really long while"* since a postcard with two blue circles arrived.

Every December when holiday cards filled the Maplewood mailbox, and every April when the snow melted along the curb, Wesley still walked out to the street post beside Heather or seven-year-old **Molly**, flipped slowly through every envelope looking for a picture of a coastal Virginia bus, and walked back up the front steps with his shoulders drooping quietly in disappointment.

Whenever Heather saw Wesley touch his miniature toy bus and look sad—or whenever Heather herself thought of Nate over the years and felt that quiet, permanent ache in her chest—Wesley never hesitated to point from his framed 2001 *Annie's Pretzels* photo squarely at Heather's sternum with a firm vocal *hmph*: reminding his cousin without sugarcoating that **she** had done that to herself back in Homeroom 212. Neither Heather nor Wesley had any way of knowing that back in July 2004, **Brandon Mercer** had intercepted their Saint Paul address-update postcard out of a Williamsburg porch mailbox and thrown it into the kitchen trash.

## 2. Sunday Morning, February 7, 2016 (`8:05 AM CST`) — Overjoyed in Front of the Living-Room TV

![Inside a sunlit snowy morning living room in Maplewood, Minnesota on Sunday, February 7, 2016, twenty-eight-year-old Wesley Sorenson stands joyfully in a navy fleece holding his blue silicone ring and miniature blue-and-white toy city bus while pointing excitedly toward the angled living-room television airing GBS News | Meet the Nation with thirty-year-old twins Katie and Nathaniel Prairie seated side by side in black power wheelchairs on screen, as thirty-year-old Heather Lindstrom, her husband Dan, nine-year-old fraternal twins Jonas and Nora holding hands, and seven-year-old Molly smile warmly from the sofa.](https://assets.raggiesoft.com/raggiesoft-backstage/images/scenes/katie-maplewood-wesley-sees-katie-on-tv.jpg)

On snowy Sunday morning, February 7, 2016, thirty-one-year-old **Dan Lindstrom** poured mugs of hot cocoa in the kitchen and switched the living-room television to ***Meet the Nation*** on `GBS` at `8:00 AM` Central Time while thirty-year-old **Heather** sat on the sectional sofa beside their nine-year-old severely disabled fraternal twins—**Jonas** (`non-verbal, humming contentedly`) and **Nora** (`100% mute, resting her cheek against her twin brother's shoulder while holding his inner hand`)—and their observant seven-year-old daughter **Molly**.

Five minutes past the hour, **Wesley** walked down the hallway in his navy fleece pullover holding his blue silicone ring and his miniature 2003 toy city bus on his way to breakfast—right as moderator **David Vance** rolled the high-definition replay from the floor of the **Virginia House of Delegates** in Richmond.

Filling the screen was thirty-year-old **Katie Prairie** speaking calmly into a wood-paneled desk microphone—and as the chamber wide shot panned two feet to the left, there sat thirty-year-old **Nathaniel Prairie** right beside his twin sister in his matching black motorized SwiftChair.

* **Wesley's Pure Joy:** Wesley froze mid-step on the hardwood floor, stared wide-eyed at the angled screen for two seconds, and let out a booming, jubilant two-note vocal cheer that made nine-year-old Jonas cheer out loud right along with him. Rocking joyfully on his sneakers, Wesley pointed straight at Katie and Nate on the glass, tapped the air twice with his knuckles (`two taps!`), and pointed triumphantly at the little Virginia toy bus in his left palm so Heather, Dan, and Molly would see that **his guy buddy Nate and Katie were right there on their television!**
* **Trying to Understand What Katie Does Now (`Zero Concept of State Government`):** Once the Washington studio interview came on, twenty-eight-year-old Wesley stood three feet from the television tilting his head with a deep, earnest furrow between his eyebrows. Wesley had no concept of abstract state government, House bills, or the General Assembly. Looking at the rows of polished wooden desks, the brass nameplates, the microphones, and the man at the front dais tapping a wooden gavel while dozens of adults in suits had to sit quietly and listen to Katie talk, Wesley turned toward Heather on the sofa and mimicked typing with both hands on an invisible computer keyboard (`remembering Northwood High's Room 204!`), questioning with a rising hum whether Katie had become the principal of a giant school or a television host.
* **Heather's Concrete Translation:** Using the exact clear, zero-baby-talk language she used every day with Wesley, Jonas, and Nora, Heather patted the cushion beside her and explained it in concrete everyday cause-and-effect Wesley understood right away:
  *"Katie helps make the rules for the buses and the buildings in Virginia now, Wes—so nobody in a wheelchair ever gets left behind at the curb anymore."*
  Wesley's whole face lit up with pride—rolling his miniature 2003 city bus across his palm with a happy three-note hum because **his friends made the rules for the buses now**—before turning squarely to Heather, pointing from the television to Heather's closed laptop on the end table, and giving her the exact commanding look he had given her at graduation thirteen years earlier: *Say hi to Nate and Katie!*

## 3. Why the Connection on *The Grid* Was So Cautious (`March 26 – June 5, 2016`)

Even with Wesley tapping her laptop lid twice every Saturday, thirty-year-old Heather refused to rush or crowd Nate and Katie's boundary:

1. **Seeing the February 8 Northwood Bus Crash News & Waiting Out the Session:** Two days after *Meet the Nation*, online Hampton Roads news feeds carried word of a thirty-one-year-old Richmond man named **Tyler** fleeing domestic-assault warrants down **Macgregory Street** and T-boning a departing **Northwood High School** bus—leaving Heather shaken to her core all over again at the realization of what kind of monster she had believed over Nate at seventeen. Knowing Katie was also in the middle of a legislative session, Heather waited six quiet weeks until late March.
2. **Sending Only a Filtered *Message Request* (`Zero Friend Request, Zero Intrusion`):** With Dan sitting supportively beside her in Maplewood on Saturday night, March 26, Heather deliberately chose **not** to click *Add Friend* on **The Grid**—sending one calm, transparent note into Nate's *Message Requests* inbox (`identifying her married name upfront, explaining Wesley's joy at the TV, sharing that she and Dan were ten years married raising Jonas, Nora, and Molly alongside Wesley, and promising she would never message again if Nate preferred to leave it unanswered`).
3. **Ten Quiet Weeks, Two Knuckle Taps on June 5, & An Open Door for Wesley Whenever He Feels Like Checking In:** Because filtered requests drop silently without chiming, ten full weeks passed in Maplewood with zero *"Seen"* receipt—and true to her word, Heather never sent a second nudge. When Nate and Katie's reply finally chimed on Sunday morning, June 5—revealing after twelve years that a bad Williamsburg housemate had thrown Wesley's 2004 postcard away, admitting how worried Nate had been back in 2005 that Wesley was upset over the missing postcards, keeping personal boundaries with Heather respectfully cautious, and explicitly telling Wesley he was **welcome to check in on *Grid Messenger* as many times as he ever felt like**—twenty-nine-year-old Wesley beamed across the Maplewood kitchen, tapped two blue circle icons and a little city-bus icon straight onto the keyboard himself, and watched with pure delight as Nate tapped two blue circles right back.
