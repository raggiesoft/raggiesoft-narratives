## 1. Holding the Line During the Twenty-One-Day Finals Bridge (`Nov 26 – Dec 16, 2004`)

Kristine assumed David's Thanksgiving afternoon walkout was a temporary tantrum engineered by his sisters—and that once **Cassandra** and seventeen-year-old **Casey** boarded their Sunday flight back to Sea-Tac on **November 28**, David's caretaking guilt would pull him straight back to Portsmouth, Virginia.

At `7:45 PM` on Sunday evening—less than two hours after Cassandra's flight pushed back from Norfolk International—the Vance landline rang in Virginia Beach while David was upstairs reviewing anatomy flashcards with **Morgan** and **Meredith Delaney**.

**Wendy Vance** picked up the kitchen extension before the second ring finished.

On the other end of the line, Kristine didn't sound angry; she deployed the exact fragile, tear-choked register that had overridden David's threat detection for six months, claiming she had suffered a panic attack alone in the apartment and needed David to drive over *"just for twenty minutes"* to help her calm down.

Wendy didn't raise her voice, and she didn't call upstairs. Having raised three neurodivergent children—and having listened to Cassandra's glacial debriefing on Thursday afternoon—Wendy spoke with quiet, immovable finality.

"David is studying for his finals, Kristine, and he no longer lives at that address," Wendy said evenly. "Everything belonging to him was removed on Thursday. Do not call this house again, and do not approach him on campus."

When Kristine pivoted forty-eight hours later—driving across the Elizabeth River on Tuesday morning to stake out the student drop-off loop at the **Community College of Hampton Roads**—she discovered why Meredith Delaney had promised David he wouldn't spend a single second of those twenty-one days unguarded.

* **Campus Escort Rotation:** Every morning and afternoon between November 29 and December 15, either **Morgan** or **Meredith** walked David door-to-door from the Delaney minivan directly to his classroom threshold and waited outside the hall during exam blocks. Spotting the six-foot-two Morgan leaning against the concrete stairwell pillar outside the science building on Tuesday morning, Kristine reversed out of the visitor lot without ever getting out of her sedan.
* **The Burton Station Road Property Release:** Rather than letting Tom or David negotiate the remaining lease paperwork on the Portsmouth apartment, **Cassandra** retained a Norfolk real-estate attorney from **Building 33** on Monday, November 29. Within seventy-two hours, the landlord accepted full lump-sum lease termination funds wired directly from Cassandra's credit union account alongside a formal written notice barring any further contact regarding shared tenancy.
* **Parallel West Coast Execution:** While the Virginia Beach pack held the physical line, Cassandra spent her evenings in Federal Way executing two quiet miracles simultaneously: filing Liz Carter's spring transfer admission to **Northwest Pacific University** (`3.8` Shenandoah Valley University GPA) and closing all-cash on the `3,800`-square-foot single-story accessible rambler three flat blocks off the **Quantum Campus** in Redmond so the four of them would never have to squeeze into a two-bedroom apartment again.

## 2. Permitting the 6:00 PM *Réveillon* Under the Mount Hope Bridge (`December 2004`)

Five hundred miles northeast on Aquidneck Island, a completely different logistical meeting unfolded inside **Portsmouth Town Hall** on the first Wednesday of December.

For generations, the French-Canadian families clustered along Mussel Bed Shoal Road—anchored by patriarch **Marc-André Bouchard** and his wife **Céline**—had celebrated *Le Réveillon* the traditional Québécois way: gathering *after* Midnight Mass at `1:00 AM` inside cramped, booming dining rooms packed wall-to-wall with forty relatives shouting over fiddles and clattering china until dawn.

When **Robert Delaney** walked next door in late November to tell Marc-André that **Erin, Megan, Chloe, and Claire's** Virginia Beach cousins and extended Vance pack—fourteen people total, including three wheelchair users (**Cassandra**, **Kate**, and **David Delaney**) and two teenagers recovering from a brutal autumn (**David** and **Casey Vance**)—were flying into T. F. Green Airport for Christmas week, Marc-André didn't hesitate for a second.

"My front steps have four granite risers and a twenty-six-inch parlor doorway," Marc-André noted immediately in his thick Québécois cadence, setting his coffee mug down on the kitchen counter. "We are not leaving three wheelchairs out on the porch, and we are not keeping kids who need their sleep up until two in the morning. We take the feast under the bridge."

Together, Marc-André Bouchard and Robert Delaney filed an application with the **Town of Portsmouth** for a neighborhood holiday block-party closure at the foot of Mussel Bed Shoal Road directly beneath the towering concrete-and-steel piers of the **Mount Hope Bridge**:

> **Town of Portsmouth — Special Neighborhood Event & Municipal Right-of-Way Permit (`#2004-1224-MBS`)**<br>
> **Applicants:** M. Bouchard & R. Delaney (Mussel Bed Shoal Road Neighborhood Association)<br>
> **Approved Window:** Friday, December 24, 2004 • `5:00 PM – 10:00 PM EST` (Strict `10:00 PM` Municipal Quiet-Hours Cutoff)<br>
> **Barricade Zone:** Dead-end waterfront cul-de-sac beneath the southern suspension piers of the Mount Hope Bridge (Public Works wooden sawhorses staged at `4:30 PM`; full emergency-lane clearance maintained).<br>
> **Accessibility & Safety Stipulations:**<br>
> 1. Twelve commercial-grade stainless-steel outdoor propane patio heaters spaced along the level asphalt apron to maintain a warm microclimate against coastal Narragansett Bay wind.<br>
> 2. Unbolted eight-foot folding banquet tables arranged with forty-eight-inch clear turning aisles for manual and power wheelchairs.<br>
> 3. Zero amplified PA speakers—overhead golden bistro string lights only, preserving neighborhood quiet hours.

Neither Marc-André nor Robert had consciously set out to engineer an autism-friendly sensory environment—they simply wanted their neighbors' wheelchairs on flat ground and everyone home in warm beds before midnight. Yet by trading cramped indoor acoustics for open winter air, golden string lights, steaming paper boats of *poutine* and *tourtière*, and the low, rhythmic white-noise hum of tires crossing the green steel bridge grid two hundred feet overhead, the Mussel Bed Shoal neighbors accidentally built one of the safest holiday memories of David and Casey Vance's lives.
