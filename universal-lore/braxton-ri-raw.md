Braxton: World Bible
1. Overview & Geography
Braxton, Rhode Island is a major Northeastern coastal metropolis (a fictional amalgam of Boston’s academic density and Chicago’s waterfront geography). The city is defined by a stark geographical and class divide. It is physically located where the real-life town of Narraganset, Rhode Island is (but encompasses a much larger area as this is meant to be such a massive metropolitan city).
	•	The Valley & The Point (The Public): The city center and the public university sit on a flat, expansive peninsula jutting into the Narraganset Bay. This area is modern, accessible, and integrated with transit.
	•	The Hill (The Elite): To the North, a steep ridge known as Mount Regalis (locally "The Knob") looms over the city. This is where the old money lives, physically looking down on the rest of the population.
	•	The South Shore (The Retreat): Far to the south, separated by the river delta and industrial zones, are the quiet, spacious coastal suburbs where the Fortress resides.

	•	Access: A gated fortress of privilege, accessible primarily by luxury cars.
3. The Braxton Transit Authority (BTA)
Nickname: "Better Take Alternatives" or "Broken Train Again."
The maps are logical, the system is extensive, and it is his path to independence.
A. The Metro (Subway/Heavy Rail)
	•	Accessibility: 100% Universal. All stations have elevators.
	•	The Blue Line (East-West Spine):
	•	Connects Narraganset Esplanade (BSU) to Beacon Central (Downtown) and the western suburbs.
	•	The Red Line (The "J-Loop"):
	•	Route: Starts North at Regalis Park (base of King's College hill), runs South along the coast to BSU, turns inland to Downtown, then hooks Southwest to the Airport.

	•	Function: Neighborhood hopper.

	•	Line: The South Shore Line.
	•	Equipment: BiLevel Coaches with "Low Floor" boarding (roll-on/roll-off).
	•	Route: A diagonal "Hypotenuse" express track.
	•	Stops: Iron Point (Home) --> BCI Airport --> Beacon Central --> Narraganset Esplanade (BSU).
	•	Speed: 22 minutes from Airport to Campus.
	•	Function: The "Teleporter."
C. The Bus Network
	•	The Core Network: Key routes in the city center run 24 Hours a Day, 7 Days a Week. High frequency (every 10-15 mins).
	•	Route 99 (The Scrub Bus): The only bus that goes near King’s College. It stops outside the gate. Used exclusively by service staff.
D. The Hub: Narraganset Esplanade Station
Located on the BSU campus.
	•	Level 2 (Elevated): Regional Rail (South Shore Line). Beautiful view of Narraganset Bay and the Newport Bridge.
	•	Level 1 (Street Level Plaza): Bus Terminal & The Esplanade (Car-free zone).
	•	Level B-M (Underground 1): Blue Line Metro.
	•	Level B-R (Underground 2): Red Line Metro.

5. Transfer Policy (The "Smart" Rules)
The Window: Transfers are valid for 120 Minutes (2 Hours) from initial Tap On.
Rule A: Lateral Transfer (Same or Lower Price)
	•	Example: Bus ($2.00) --> Bus ($2.00).
	•	Cost: Free.
	•	Time: The 2-hour clock continues counting down. It does not reset.
Rule B: Step-Up Transfer (Higher Price)
	•	Example: Bus ($2.00) --> Metro ($2.75).
	•	Cost: Pay the difference ($0.75).
	•	Time: The 2-hour clock RESETS to zero.
	•	Why: Because you paid "New Money" into the system, you get "New Time."
Rule C: The "Ghost" Penalty (Rail Only)
	•	You must Tap On and Tap Off for Regional Rail.
	•	Failure to Tap Off: The system assumes you rode to Zone 15.
	•	Penalty Charge: $30.00 (Maximum Peak Fare) deducted from LinkPass balance.

BTA / BSU Policy: The "Bull Pass" Identity
1. What is it?
	•	Official Name: The Braxton State University Identification Card.
	•	Colloquial Name: "The Bull Pass."
	•	Function: It is a 4-in-1 Smart Card.
	•	Building Key: Access to dorms, labs, and practice rooms.
	•	Meal Plan: Swipe for dining halls.
	•	Library Card: Check out materials.
	•	Transit Pass: Hard-coded as a Zone 15 Unlimited LinkPass.
2. Activation
	•	Status: Instant.
	•	The Process: The moment the card is printed at the ID Office and handed to the student/staff member, the RFID chip is live. You can walk out of the office, walk onto a bus, and it works.
3. The "Lost Card" Penalty
Because the card is worth roughly $600/month in transit value alone, losing it is a crisis.
	•	Replacement Fee: $25.00 (University Fee).
	•	The Real Cost: You have to pay standard BTA fares (cash or vanilla LinkPass) until you get the new one.

Location Profile: Narraganset Esplanade Station
1. Overview
	•	Official Name: Narraganset Esplanade Transit Center.
	•	Rank: #2 Busiest Station in the BTA System (behind Braxton Central).
	•	Nickname: "The Lantern" or "The Point."
	•	Location: The eastern tip of the Braxton Point Peninsula, marking the boundary between the Braxton State University (BSU) campus and Narraganset Bay.
	•	Architectural Style: Marine Modernism.
	•	Design: Soaring white tensile fabric roofs (evoking sails) and floor-to-ceiling glass walls facing the Narraganset. At night, it glows like a beacon visible from ships entering the harbor.
	•	Significance: It is the primary interchange for the Eastern Zone of the metropolis. It doesn't just serve students; it serves tourists, festival-goers, and commuters transferring from the ferries.
2. The Layout: "The Stack"
The station is a masterclass in density, built into the slope of the reclaimed parkland to allow seamless transitions between levels.
Level 2: The Sky Platform (Regional Rail Terminus)
	•	Structure: An elevated viaduct running parallel to the coastline.
	•	Service:
	•	The South Shore Line: (The Fortress Commute). Terminates here.

	•	Accessibility: 100% Level Boarding. The platform is raised to match the floor height of the BiLevel coaches perfectly.

Location Profile: Braxton Central Terminal (BCT)
1. Overview
	•	Official Name: The Braxton Central Intermodal Terminal.
	•	Nickname: "The Glass Cathedral," "The Central," or simply "The Hub."
	•	Opened: October 15, 2005. (It still smells like new steel and ozone when the family arrives in 2006).
	•	Location: Downtown Braxton, bridging the gap between the Financial District and the Historic Riverfront.
	•	Architectural Style: High-Tech Modernism.
	•	Inspiration: Berlin Hauptbahnhof (Berlin Central Station).
	•	The Design: A massive, transparent exoskeleton of curved glass and tubular steel. There are no dark corners. Natural light filters from the roof all the way down to the subway platforms 60 feet below ground.
	•	The Philosophy: "Visible Motion." From the central atrium, you can see trains moving East-West in the sky, and trains moving North-South in the earth.

	•	The Western Mainline.
	•	Leo’s Take: Waiting here feels like floating. You can see the bay to the East and the rolling hills to the West.
Level 4: The Mezzanine (The Girders)

Level 3: The Grand Concourse (Street Level)
	•	The Atrium: An immense indoor piazza.
	•	The Feature: The Solari Board (See Section 4).
	•	The Bus Wing: Extending from the South facade is the Braxton Central Bus Terminal. Unlike old "dungeon" bus stations, this is a soaring glass pavilion with 40 bays.
	•	Serves: Pathfinder (Intercity), Regional Coach, and BTA Local Buses.
Level 2: The Retail Deck
	•	A balcony level encircling the atrium, filled with coffee shops, newsstands, and quick-service dining.
Level 1: The Deep (Lower Train Shed)
	•	Structure: A cavernous subterranean hall running North-South.
	•	Tracks: 8 Tracks (Tracks 1–8).
	•	Primary Service:
	•	ARTS Intercity Rail: The Coastal Service and The Patriot.
	•	BTA Metro: The Red Line and Silver Line.
	•	The Blue Line: Runs in a separate, deeper tunnel perpendicular to Level 1, accessible via a dedicated escalator bank.
3. Vertical Circulation
The Crystal Core (The Elevators)
	•	Design: A cluster of six massive, free-standing cylindrical glass shafts located dead center in the Grand Concourse.
	•	Function: "Turbo Lifts." They connect Level 1 (The Deep) directly to Level 5 (The Sky Deck).
	•	Narrative Role: This is Leo’s primary path. He loves the sensation of rising through the bustling station, watching the layers of transit rotate around him.
The Perimeter Towers ("The Locals")
	•	Location: Four massive glass shafts located at the four corners of the Grand Concourse (Northwest, Northeast, Southwest, Southeast).
	•	Service: All Levels (1 through 5).
	•	Stops: The Deep $\to$ Retail $\to$ Concourse $\to$ Mezzanine $\to$ Sky Deck.
	•	Design: unlike the cylindrical Crystal Core, these are rectangular, panoramic glass cabs that run along the exterior walls of the atrium.
	•	Function: These are the workhorses. They handle:
	•	Shoppers going to the Retail Deck (Level 2).
	•	Staff going to the Mezzanine Offices (Level 4).
	•	Travelers transferring from the Bus Wing (Level 3) to the Metro (Level 1).
The Narrative Distinction

	•	Perimeter Towers: The "Local." Stops everywhere. Slower, but offers views of the retail and bus levels that the express skips.

4. Sensory Details
The Heartbeat: The Solari Board
Despite the station being brand new in 2005, the architects installed a custom-built Split-Flap Display in the main hall as a nod to the golden age of rail.
	•	The Sound: CLACK-CLACK-CLACK-CLACK.
	•	The Rhythm: Every 60 seconds, the board refreshes. The sound ripples through the glass hall—a comforting, mechanical promise of order.

Because the station is glass and steel, it has a specific acoustic profile: bright, sharp, and slightly reverb-heavy.
	•	Announcements: They don't boom; they echo crisply.
	•	Train Noise: You don't hear the roar of diesel engines (most trains are electric here); you hear the high-pitched whine of inverters and the hiss of air brakes.

BTA Announcements
Here is the Official BTA Audio Style Guide & Scriptbook, covering every mode of transit in the Fortress's network.
I. The Rules of the Airwaves
The BTA uses a zoned audio system with distinct voice profiles for each mode of transport to help visually impaired (or distracted) passengers instantly know which vehicle they are on.
	•	Regional Rail Voice: "The Conductor."
	•	Tone: Deep, calm, authoritative, smooth (Male-leaning synthetic).
	•	Chime: A rich, two-tone orchestral bell (Ding-Dong).
	•	Metro (Subway) Voice: "The Navigator."
	•	Tone: Crisp, rapid, high-treble, efficient (Female-leaning synthetic).
	•	Chime: A sharp, electronic triad (Beep-Beep-Beep).
	•	Bus Voice: "The Driver."
	•	Tone: Flat, robotic, slightly compressed audio (Gender-Neutral).
	•	Chime: A single digital tone (Bling).
	•	Ferry Voice: "The Captain."
	•	Tone: Resonant, slightly echoed (to cut through wind/water noise).
	•	Chime: A digital foghorn blast (Brah-Braahh).
The Golden Rule: Every transfer announcement, no matter how obvious, must end with the phrase: “Please check the schedule for availability.”

Official Designation: I-895 North / South.

The Confusion: You drive East toward the ocean, but the signs say North.
1. The "Master Plan" (1965)
The federal government didn't want to build a local spur; they wanted to build a Super-Regional Bypass to relieve pressure on I-95 in Braxton and Providence.
The Intended Route (The "I-895 Corridor"):
	•	Leg 1 (Built): Split from I-95 in Iron Point. Head East across the Jamestown/Newport Bridges.
	•	Leg 2 (The Ghost): Turn North in Newport. Run up the spine of Aquidneck Island (bulldozing the farms in Middletown/Portsmouth).
	•	Leg 3 (Existing as Route 24): Cross the Sakonnet River Bridge. Continue North through Tiverton/Fall River.
	•	Leg 4 (The Connection): Merge with I-93 near Randolph, MA.
The Logic:
If built, I-895 would have been a massive, continuous North/South highway paralleling I-95. Therefore, the entire road was federally designated North/South from the beginning.
2. The "Broken Chain" (1972)
When the "Island Segment" (Leg 2) was cancelled due to the Freeway Revolt, the chain broke.
	•	Route 24: Remained a state highway (never got the Blue Shield).
	•	I-895: Became a "Stub Interstate" that runs East/West but still carries its "North" designation because the federal paperwork was never updated.
3. The "Stub" Reality
	•	West End (Iron Point): 6 Lanes. High Speed. Designed to accept traffic from DC/NY.
	•	Middle (The bridges): 4 Lanes.
	•	East End (Newport): Dumps traffic into a traffic light at Admiral Kalbfus Road, exactly where the "Ghost Highway" was supposed to continue North to connect to what is now Route 24.

# The East Bay Transit Spine (OSTA Operations)

The East Bay corridor is served by the Ocean State Transit Authority (OSTA), bridging the critical gaps between Braxton, Newport, PVD Airport, and Providence. The primary hub for these regional connections is the **Jamestown Bridge Park & Ride**, located at the I-895 and Route 1A interchange (mirroring the real-life North Kingstown Park & Ride).

## OSTA Surface Routes
*   **Route 14 (The Braxton Express):** Operates from Braxton Central Terminal to the Jamestown Bridge Park & Ride as an Express service. From the Park & Ride, it shifts to local service, running up the US-1 corridor to PVD Airport, terminating at Kennedy Plaza in Providence.
*   **Route 12:** Operates from Newport Gateway Center as an Express service over the Newport Pell and Jamestown Bridges to the Jamestown Bridge Park & Ride. From there, it mirrors Route 14, operating local service up US-1 to PVD Airport and Kennedy Plaza.
*   **Route 64:** A direct 1:1 copy of the real-life RIPTA Route 64, with the addition of a scheduled stop at the Jamestown Bridge Park & Ride.

## Schedule & Headways
*   Routes 12 and 14 each operate on a **120-minute (2-hour) headway**.
*   Because Routes 12 and 14 share the exact same local stops north of the Park & Ride, they are staggered to provide a combined **60-minute headway** on the US-1 corridor between the Park & Ride, PVD, and Kennedy Plaza.
*   **Short Turn Route 12:** A short-turn variant of Route 12 operates exclusively between Kennedy Plaza and PVD Airport. When combined with the standard Route 12 and Route 14 schedules, this creates a reliable **30-minute headway** between the airport and downtown Providence.
