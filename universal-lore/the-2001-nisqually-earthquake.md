# Lore Dossier: The 2001 Nisqually Earthquake (The Bedbound Anchor)

**Date:** Wednesday, February 28, 2001 (10:54 AM PST)
**Location:** Federal Way, Washington (Ground-Floor Apartment)
**Characters:** Cassandra Vance, Casey Vance

## The Setup (The Sick Day)
Because the 6.8 magnitude Nisqually Earthquake struck on a Wednesday morning at 10:54 AM, the standard assumption is that the Vance sisters would be separated by a massive 35-mile logistical nightmare (Cassandra at Quantum in Redmond, Casey at the High School in Federal Way). 

However, the reality of that day was far more intimate. 
Casey was violently sick. She had left school early on Monday, spent all of Tuesday throwing up, and had an appointment with her Primary Care Provider scheduled for Wednesday afternoon. Because her sister was ill, Cassandra (the ever-protective Sentinel) used her Quantum PTO to stay home. 

When the tectonic plate fractured at 10:54 AM, they were both lying in Cassandra's bed watching a movie, with a plastic bucket sitting on the floor beside the mattress.

## The Strike & The Hollywood Myth
When the low, guttural roar of the earthquake hit, the power instantly cut out, plunging the bedroom into shadows. The wood-framed apartment building didn't just vibrate; it violently swayed and snapped, cracking the drywall and shattering picture frames.

In a moment of pure panic, fueled by outdated disaster movies (specifically the 1997 movie *Volcano*), Casey's immediate instinct was to bolt from the bed.
"We have to get to a doorway!" Casey shouted, trying to scramble across the shifting mattress. "We have to kiss our asses goodbye!"

Cassandra, who operates entirely on precise, logistical realities rather than Hollywood myths, knew that a doorway was actually one of the most dangerous places they could be in a modern wood-framed building. The swinging door would crush their fingers, and they would be completely exposed to flying glass and falling debris. 

Furthermore, Cassandra cannot bear any weight on her shattered heels. Running to a doorway wasn't just tactically stupid; it was physically impossible for her.

## The Sentinel Protocol
Cassandra physically intercepted her sister. She grabbed Casey by the shoulders and violently yanked her back down flat against the mattress. 
"Do not move!" Cassandra ordered. 

Using her upper body strength, Cassandra threw herself partially over Casey, shielding her younger sister with her own body. She reached down, grabbed the heavy winter comforter, and dragged it entirely over both of their heads. 

For 45 agonizing seconds, they were trapped under the heavy blanket while the room tore itself apart. The heavy comforter acted as a perfect physical shield against the chaotic debris—books falling off the nightstand, picture frames shattering, and jagged chunks of drywall dusting the room. 

## The Physical Toll
For Casey, whose sensory processors were already misfiring due to the severe nausea and fever, the sheer violence of the bed pitching like a ship in a storm was catastrophic. Under the safety of the comforter, trapped in Cassandra's iron grip, Casey's motion sickness peaked. The plastic bucket was out on the floor, buried under fallen debris. 

Cassandra didn't care in the slightest. As Casey dry-heaved into the sheets in the dark, Cassandra just held her tighter, rubbing her back, anchoring her while the world outside literally fractured.

## The Aftermath
When the shaking finally stopped and the adrenaline faded, they were left in a dark, wrecked apartment. The phone lines were dead, the floors were covered in glass, and Cassandra's wheelchair had been knocked over. 

But the overwhelming reality was that they were together. The 35-mile chasm of separation had been eliminated. They didn't have to navigate a paralyzed highway or freeze on a football field. They just had to survive the dark bedroom together until the grid came back online.
