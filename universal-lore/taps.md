# Lore Dossier: TAPS (Transit Authority of the Puget Sound)

## Overview
TAPS is the in-universe mega-agency representing a merger of King County Metro, Pierce Transit, Sound Transit, Community Transit, and Everett Transit into one unified regional grid.

## Route Naming Convention
`TAPS [jurisdiction] Route [number]`
*   **Jurisdiction options:** `Pierce County`, `King County`, `Snohomish County`, or `Everett` (since Everett Transit is historically separate from Community Transit).
*   *Example:* Jessica lives on `TAPS King County Route 105` (the in-universe representation of the real-life KC Metro Route 105).

## Fare Media
*   **Cascade Card:** The regional smart card (the 1:1 ORCA card counterpart). 
    *   *Lore Note:* The joint transit board initially pitched "e-pass" (Electronic Pass) during brainstorming, but the TAPS legal team immediately shot it down. After researching the trademark, TAPS legal realized it was too close to **ExpressToll (e-toll)**—the massive East Coast tolling system—and they did not want to invite a lawsuit from the **ETN (Eastern Toll Network)**.
*   **TAPS Pass:** The hard plastic monthly pass, or stored value, that is loaded onto the Cascade Card (equivalent to the real-life PugetPass).

## Fleet & Visual Identity
*   **Livery:** TAPS buses use a distinct **teal, gold, and white wavy/swooping livery design**, exactly matching early 2000s Sound Transit Express colors. The side of the bus prominently displays "TAPS" in large, clean white sans-serif letters.
*   **Fleet Vehicles:**
    *   **Gillig Phantom:** High-floor heavy-duty transit bus (historically accurate to the real-life Sound Transit 1999 service launch).
    *   **New Flyer C40LF:** 40-foot low-floor CNG bus.
    *   **New Flyer C60LF:** 60-foot articulated low-floor CNG bus.
    *   **Atlas Motorcoach:** Over-the-road commuter coach. For use ONLY on routes operated out of Pierce County (Routes 574, 586, 590, 591, 592, 594, 595), which directly reflects real-life operations out of the Pierce Transit garage in Lakewood, WA.

## Critical Narrative Points (The Quantum Tax District)
Mirroring the real-world history of the Sound Transit 2 Line and the Redmond Technology Station, Cassandra Vance's first major infrastructural act as the newly minted CEO of Quantum (in 2014) is to subvert the "private Silicon Valley shuttle" trope. Rather than building a closed-loop system, Quantum throws its massive corporate weight and financial leverage behind the proposed TAPS light rail. 

Quantum enters into a specialized municipal tax district, directly funding and integrating the TAPS light rail expansion right into the heart of the Redmond campus. Cassandra is fiercely committed to public transit over private jets/motorcades, viewing a robust public grid as the ultimate equalizer and the true mark of a functioning empire. When she commutes, she actively uses her wheelchair ramp on TAPS buses.
