# 🌨️ **Eira Rowlands**

**Meaning:** “Snow” **Pronunciation:**

- **AY‑rah** (English approximation)
    
- Welsh stress on the first syllable: **EY‑ra**
    

**Notes:**

- Not “Eye‑ra” — the vowel is bright, like “day.”
    
- Very common in North Wales, especially Anglesey.
    

# 🪶 **Rowlands** (surname)

**Pronunciation:**

- **ROW‑lands** (ROW rhymes with “cow”)
    
- In Welsh: **RHOW‑lands** with a slightly rolled R
    

**Notes:**

- This is a classic Anglesey surname.
    
- The “ow” is always the Welsh “aw” sound, never “row your boat.”
    

# 🌟 **Gwendolyn Arwen Rowlands**

### **Gwendolyn**

**Pronunciation:**

- **GWEN‑doh‑lin**
    
- In Welsh, the “Gwen‑” is crisp and short, not “Gwen‑duh‑lin.”
    

### **Arwen**

**Pronunciation:**

- **AR‑wen** (AR as in “car”)
    
- Stress on the first syllable
    

**Notes:**

- Tolkien borrowed this name from Welsh phonology — it’s authentically Cymraeg.
    

# 🔥 **Gabriel Idris Rowlands**

### **Gabriel**

**Pronunciation:**

- **GAY‑bree‑el** (standard English)
    
- In Welsh, sometimes softened to **GAB‑ree‑el**, but both are used.
    

### **Idris**

**Pronunciation:**

- **ID‑riss**
    
- Stress on the first syllable
    

**Notes:**

- From _Cadair Idris_, the legendary Welsh mountain.
    
- The “dr” cluster is quick and light.
    

# 🎸 **Julian Thomas Rowlands**

### **Julian**

**Pronunciation:**

- **JOO‑lee‑an**
    
- No Welsh variant — this is Tom’s contribution.
    

### **Thomas**

**Pronunciation:**

- **TOM‑as** (English)
    
- In Welsh, the name exists but is pronounced **TOM‑ass** with a short “a.”
    

# 🌙 **Cross** (their original surname)

**Pronunciation:**

- **KRAWS** (English)
    
- No Welsh variant — this is purely Tom’s American surname.
