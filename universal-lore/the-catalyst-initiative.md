# The Quantum Catalyst Initiative

**Sponsor:** Quantum Corporation (in partnership with Northwest Pacific University)
**Founder:** Initiated under the leadership of CEO William Keswick

## Overview
The Quantum Catalyst Initiative is a highly prestigious, nationwide annual competition and scholarship program designed to identify the next generation of elite software engineers and systems architects. It targets high school juniors and seniors across the United States who demonstrate extraordinary technical, engineering, or architectural aptitude in computing.

## The Grand Prize
Winners of the Catalyst Initiative receive:
- **Full-Ride Scholarship:** Complete tuition coverage for four years at Northwest Pacific University (NPU) in Seattle.
- **The Quantum Pipeline:** A guaranteed, fast-tracked internship and employment pipeline directly into Quantum Corporation upon graduation.

## Application Cycle & Timeline
The Catalyst Initiative operates on a strict annual cycle tied to the high school academic calendar:
- **Application Window:** Opens in the late summer / early fall.
- **Submission Deadline:** **March 1st**.
- **Notification ("The Call"):** Winners are typically notified via an official corporate packet and a personal phone call from Quantum executives in mid-to-late **April**. If a Junior wins, the scholarship is deferred and held in escrow until their high school graduation.

## Judging Committee
The judging committee consists of senior Quantum executives (including Steve Ballantine and William Keswick) and NPU computer science faculty. 
- **Strict Blind Review:** The committee operates under strict non-disclosure agreements (NDAs). To ensure absolute fairness, executives who interact with candidates prior to the deadline cannot explicitly promise positions or discuss the candidate's entry in a way that violates the neutrality of the judging process.
- **Rolling Review:** The committee internally reviews submissions as they arrive. Exceptional early submissions can establish a candidate as the front-runner, though judges are required to keep an open mind until the March 1st deadline formally closes the cycle.

## Known Winners & Submissions

### Cassandra Vance (1995–1996 Cycle)
- **Submission Date:** August 1995 (more than six months ahead of the deadline).
- **The Project:** A bare-metal 3D rendering engine written in hybrid Assembly that utilized time-delta loops and manual cache flushing to forcefully render 3D polygons without a math coprocessor.
- **Status:** Cassandra established such a massive lead early in the cycle that the judging committee was practically just waiting for the deadline to pass. She received her acceptance call in April 1996.
