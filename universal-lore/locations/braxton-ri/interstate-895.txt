Transit Profile: Interstate 895 (The Newport Expressway)
Official Designation: I-895 North/South.
Local Name: "Route 895" or "The Expressway."
Route: I-95 (Iron Point) à Newport (Admiral Kalbfus Rd).
Total Length: 16.5 Miles.
1. The "Great Bypass" Concept (The Dream)
The 1960s Master Plan:
Federal highway planners envisioned I-895 as the ultimate solution to the I-95 bottlenecks in Providence and Braxton.
	•	The Goal: To take long-distance traffic from Connecticut/New York and shoot it straight to Boston without ever touching the city centers.
	•	The Path:
	•	Split from I-95 in Iron Point.
	•	Cross Narragansett Bay (Jamestown/Newport Bridges).
	•	Run North up the center of Aquidneck Island (The "Island Segment").
	•	Connect to Route 24 and I-93, creating a seamless, high-speed coastal route to Boston.
2. The "Freeway Revolt" (The Failure)
The "Island Segment" Cancellation (1972):
While the bridges were built, the wealthy residents of Middletown and Portsmouth (Aquidneck Island) revolted against a 6-lane trench cutting through their farms and estates.
	•	The Result: The state cancelled the northern leg.
	•	The Consequence: I-895 became a "Stub Interstate." It blasts traffic onto the island at highway speeds, only to dump them unceremoniously onto surface streets (Admiral Kalbfus Rd) with nowhere to go.
3. The Infrastructure Schizophrenia (The Environmental War)
Phase 1: The Braxton Superhighway (Exits 1–6)
	•	Built: 1982.
	•	Design: Massive 6-Lane concrete interstate with wide shoulders and jersey barriers.
	•	Purpose: Served the booming suburbs of Iron Point, Exeter, and Liberty. It feels like the Jersey Turnpike.
Phase 2: The "Parkway" Compromise (Exit 6 to Bridge)
	•	Built: 1992.
	•	The Conflict: The RI Dept. of Environmental Quality (DEQ) sued to stop the 6-lane highway from crossing the Narrow River Watershed.
	•	The Compromise: The highway was allowed, but only as a 4-Lane "Scenic Parkway."
	•	No Concrete Median: Just a grassy swale.
	•	No Wide Shoulders: Tight, curbing breakdown lanes.
	•	The Bottleneck: At Exit 6 (US-1), the 3rd lane vanishes.
	•	Summer Impact: On Fridays in July, traffic backs up for 5 miles as three lanes of aggressive New York drivers try to merge into two lanes of scenic parkway. It is known locally as "The Funnel."

5. The Newport Terminus (The 2024 Realignment)
In the "Future" timeline (post-2020), the chaotic end of the highway was finally fixed, but it officially killed the "Interstate" status of the final mile.
Northbound (Arrival in Newport):
	•	The Pell Bridge: You cross the bridge.
	•	The Split: The highway descends.
	•	The End: The Interstate designation terminates at a massive traffic signal.
	•	Left Turn: SR-238 (To America's Cup Ave / Downtown).
	•	Straight: Becomes Halsey Street (Surface Arterial).
	•	The Final Turn: Halsey Street runs for 0.5 miles to Admiral Kalbfus Road, where the "Ghost Highway" stub sits behind barriers, mocking the drivers who have to turn right.
Southbound (Leaving Newport):
	•	The Start: You turn from Admiral Kalbfus onto Halsey Street.
	•	The Signs: SR-138 WEST / TO I-895 SOUTH.
	•	The Light: You cross the traffic signal at SR-238.
	•	The Ramp: Only after the light does the road widen, the speed limit increase, and the I-895 SOUTH shield appear.
Infrastructure Profile: Interstate 895 (The Lost Superhighway)
Official Designation: I-895 North / South. Local Name: "The Expressway." Design Era: The "Robert Moses" Era (Highways over Homes).
1. The Master Plan (1965)
The Goal: A seamless, 65 MPH, 6-lane superhighway connecting I-95 (Iron Point) to I-95 (what is now I-93) (Randolph, MA). The Specs:
	•	Dual Bridges: A new 6-lane Jamestown Bridge and a parallel 3-lane Newport Bridge (making 6 lanes total).
	•	I-395 Spur: A dedicated interstate spur blasting through the historic Point District of Newport to dump traffic directly onto Thames Street.
	•	The Island Trunk: 6 Lanes running up the spine of Aquidneck Island (bulldozing the farms) to connect to what is now Route 24.
2. The Reality (The Compromises)
The plan died a death of a thousand cuts (lawsuits, environmental acts, and gambling parlors).
Phase 1: The Mainline (I-95 to US-1)
	•	Status: Built as Planned.
	•	Config: 6 Lanes (3 per side). 65 MPH.
	•	Vibe: Massive, concrete, Interstate-standard.
Phase 2: The "Narrow River" Compromise (US-1 to Bridge)
	•	The Lawsuit: The RI Dept. of Environmental Quality (DEQ) sued to protect the watershed.
	•	The Result: The 3rd lane was cut. The shoulder was narrowed. The concrete barrier was replaced with grass.
	•	The Speed: Drops from 65 to 55 MPH.
Phase 3: The Bridge Waiver
	•	The Failure: The parallel bridge was never funded.
	•	The Fix: They squeezed I-895 onto the existing 4-lane bridge.
	•	The Waiver: Obtained an AASHTO Exception to run an Interstate over a non-compliant span with no shoulders and a double-yellow line.
	•	The Speed: 45 MPH.
3. The "Ghost" Interchanges (Newport End)
A. The Broken Trumpet (I-895 / I-395)
	•	Location: Just off the bridge.
	•	The Design: A massive "Trumpet" style interchange designed to split traffic between Middletown (I-895 North) and Downtown (I-395 South).
	•	The Reality: I-395 was cancelled. The "Highway" to downtown became Farewell Street / America's Cup Ave (a surface arterial).
	•	The Visual: You can still see the graded earthworks for the flyover ramps that were never poured.
B. The Jai Alai Curve (Exit 12 / Admiral Kalbfus)
	•	The Design: Supposed to be a standard Diamond Interchange.
	•	The Obstacle: Newport Jai Alai (The massive gambling fronton). The owners refused to sell the land for the northbound off-ramp.
	•	The Fix: Engineers forced a Partial Cloverleaf that curls 270 degrees to avoid the property line.
	•	The Traffic Pattern:
	•	Right: J.T. Connell Highway.
	•	Straight: The Jai Alai Parking Lot.
	•	Left: Admiral Kalbfus (Route 138).
Interstate 895 (The Great Bypass) Directional Quirks
Official Designation: I-895 North / South.
Physical Direction: West ß àEast.
The Confusion: You drive East toward the ocean, but the signs say North.
1. The "Master Plan" (1965)
The federal government didn't want to build a local spur; they wanted to build a Super-Regional Bypass to relieve pressure on I-95 in Braxton and Providence.
The Intended Route (The "I-895 Corridor"):
	•	Leg 1 (Built): Split from I-95 in Iron Point. Head East across the Jamestown/Newport Bridges.
	•	Leg 2 (The Ghost): Turn North in Newport. Run up the spine of Aquidneck Island (bulldozing the farms in Middletown/Portsmouth).
	•	Leg 3 (Existing as Route 24): Cross the Sakonnet River Bridge. Continue North through Tiverton/Fall River.
	•	Leg 4 (The Connection): Merge with I-93 near Randolph, MA.
The Logic:
If built, I-895 would have been a massive, continuous North/South highway paralleling I-95. Therefore, the entire road was federally designated North/South from the beginning.
2. The "Broken Chain" (1972)
When the "Island Segment" (Leg 2) was cancelled due to the Freeway Revolt, the chain broke.
	•	Route 24: Remained a state highway (never got the Blue Shield).
	•	I-895: Became a "Stub Interstate" that runs East/West but still carries its "North" designation because the federal paperwork was never updated.
3. The "Stub" Reality
	•	West End (Iron Point): 6 Lanes. High Speed. Designed to accept traffic from DC/NY.
	•	Middle (The bridges): 4 Lanes.
	•	East End (Newport): Dumps traffic into a traffic light at Admiral Kalbfus Road, exactly where the "Ghost Highway" was supposed to continue North to connect to what is now Route 24.
