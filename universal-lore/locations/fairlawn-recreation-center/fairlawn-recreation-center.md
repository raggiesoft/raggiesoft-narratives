# Fairlawn Recreation Center - Architecture & Lore

This document serves as a supplementary reference for specific architectural quirks, limitations, and features of the Fairlawn Recreation Center, based on its real-life inspiration (the 1977 Kempsville Recreation Center in Virginia Beach).

## The 1977 Building (Active 1977 - 2015)

The original building functions as a 1:1 drop-in replacement for the Kempsville Recreation Center as it existed from the 1990s through 2006-2007.

## The 2017 Building (Active June 2017 - Present)

The original 1977 building closes in **2015** and is completely demolished on the same site. Following a two-year, \$32 million ground-up rebuild (`2015–2017`, during which Fairlawn patrons and staff are temporarily displaced to neighboring municipal centers across the city), the brand-new **87,116-square-foot Fairlawn Recreation Center** opens in **June 2017**, modeled 1:1 on the modern 2017 Kempsville Recreation Center in Virginia Beach.

### Architectural Signature & Core Upgrades
*   **The "Sand Dune Fence" & Ocean-Wave Timber Roof:** Built with soaring glued-laminated timber (`glulam`) beams that twist and curve across the roofline to evoke coastal sand dunes and ocean waves, paired with massive northern glass walls for natural daylight harvesting, bamboo flooring, and a geothermal HVAC system.
*   **Single-Level Primary Circulation with Elevated Mezzanine Track:** Replaces the 1977 building's steep three-story concrete helix ramp and key-locked elevator with a unified ground-floor main concourse (housing the Front Desk, Weight Room/Fitness Center, Double Gymnasium, Natatorium, and 8 multipurpose rooms) crowned by a second-floor **Mezzanine** featuring an **elevated 1/8-mile (`8 laps = 1 mile`) indoor walking and jogging track**.
*   **3 Assisted Care Changing Suites:**
    *   The 2017 building features **3 dedicated Assisted Care / Family changing rooms** alongside the standard Men's and Women's locker rooms.
    *   Each private suite includes a **toilet**, **sink**, **roll-in shower**, and a **small changing chair**.
    *   **Purpose:** The small chair was specifically engineered with a parent, older sibling, or guardian helping an opposite-gender family member in mind—permanently resolving the strict single-gender locker room barrier of the 1977 building.

## New Building (Opened June 2017 -- Present)

Modeled 1:1 on the modern **Kempsville Recreation Center** in Virginia Beach (designed by HBA Architecture & Interior Design and Moody Nolan), the **2017 Fairlawn Recreation Center** is an **87,116-square-foot** multigenerational wellness facility built directly on the footprint of the demolished 1977 structure along Marlborough Drive (just off Princess Anne Road, five minutes down the road from the residential **Susquehanna Drive** corridor).

### The 2015–2017 Demolition & Displacement Window

- **2015 Closure & Teardown:** After 38 years of continuous service, the original 1977 brick-and-concrete-ramp building closed in **2015** and was completely demolished.
- **2015–2017 Patron & Staff Displacement:** During the two-year construction window, Fairlawn's regular patrons and recreation staff were temporarily displaced across neighboring city recreation centers before returning home when the new building opened its doors in **June 2017**.

### Operating Hours & Front-Desk Shift Architecture (2017–2026)

- **Monday -- Thursday:** Open until **9:00 PM** (`21:00`). Closing front-desk staff and the **Duty Supervisor / Lead Cashier** are scheduled until **9:15 PM** (`21:15`), with preliminary register till counts and evening closing prep beginning between `8:30 PM` and `8:45 PM`.
- **Friday:** Open until **8:00 PM** (`20:00`). Closing staff are scheduled until **8:15 PM** (`20:15`), with the `15-minute` closing sweep and Back Office register countdown initiating promptly at **7:45 PM**.
- **Saturday:** Open **9:00 AM -- 5:00 PM** (staff scheduled `8:45 AM -- 5:15 PM`).
- **Sunday:** Open **11:00 AM -- 5:00 PM** (staff scheduled `10:45 AM -- 5:15 PM`).

### Exterior & Structural Design

- **The "Sand Dune Fence" & Ocean-Wave Glulam Roof:** The building's signature architectural element is a sweeping, twisting roof structure framed with heavy glued-laminated timber (`glulam`) beams designed to evoke coastal sand dunes, drift fences, and rolling ocean waves.
- **Daylight Harvesting & Geothermal HVAC:** Massive northern glass curtain walls flood the lobby commons, natatorium, and fitness zones with diffused natural light while minimizing solar heat gain, supported by bamboo wood accents and a high-efficiency geothermal HVAC plant.
- **Resurfaced Parking Lot:** The notorious axle-breaking potholes of the 1977 parking lot were completely eliminated during the 2017 site reconstruction.

## Fairlawn Recreation Center: Operational Notes (2007)

### The \"Code Purple\" Protocol

This is a specific, crucial reasonable accommodation developed for the
Bennett siblings.

- **Initial Status:** The \"Code Purple\" system began as an **informal,
  unwritten understanding** among the compassionate staff and
  supervisors at Fairlawn. They knew Jack and Sarah were each other\'s
  primary support system and allowed them to respond to each other\'s
  needs during a crisis without a formal policy.

- **The System Failure:** The protocol\'s critical flaw was exposed the
  first time Sarah had an autistic meltdown in a **secure,
  key-restricted aquatics area** (like the pump or filter room). Jack,
  hearing the call, rushed to her location but was **blocked by a locked
  door**, leaving him helpless and unable to provide the immediate
  support the system was designed for.

- **Formalization and Training:** This failure prompted an immediate,
  official review.

  - The siblings\' reasonable accommodations were officially updated to
    include a formalized \"Code Purple\" protocol.

  - As part of this official update, Jack was required to undergo **all
    necessary aquatics safety training**, including watching safety
    videos and receiving briefings on heavy equipment, chemical handling
    (HazCom), and other dangers, ensuring his safety when entering these
    restricted areas.

  - Upon completion, Jack was issued a key granting him access to all
    aquatics areas, ensuring a locked door would never again be a
    barrier.

  - It is universally understood by **all staff** that a Code Purple
    call requires the designated sibling to respond **immediately**,
    without needing to ask for permission.

# Protocols Directly Involving the Bennett Siblings

These protocols are the most significant for Jack and Sarah\'s story,
representing their specific, recognized accommodations and the staff\'s
compassionate understanding of their needs.

- **Code Purple (Bennett Emergency) 🟣**

  - **Meaning:** A specific, formal reasonable accommodation for
    **Jack** or **Sarah Bennett** experiencing an autistic meltdown.

  - **Activation:** Can be called verbally over the radio. In a case
    where the meltdown is severe enough to cause situational mutism, the
    sound of the sibling **crying over the radio** is also recognized by
    all staff as a valid and urgent Code Purple call.

  - **Response:** The designated sibling is to respond immediately,
    without needing permission. This protocol **overrides all standard
    rules of workplace conduct**, including the general guideline to
    moderate physical affection. The responding sibling\'s primary duty
    is to provide grounding comfort, which often requires **holding the
    other sibling very tightly** to help them through the sensory storm
    of the meltdown.

- **Code Black: Bennett Sibling Protocol**

  - **Context:** During a **Code Black (Tornado)**, a specific
    accommodation is in place for the Bennetts.

  - **Protocol:** If it is safely possible, staff will ensure that
    **Jack** and **Sarah Bennett** are brought to the **same designated
    interior safe space**. The policy of calling a Code Black the moment
    NWS Wakefield (AKQ) issues a warning is specifically designed to
    provide the precious extra minutes that might be needed to
    accomplish this task safely.

- **Code Green: Bennett Sibling Protocol (Rescue)**

  - **Context:** In the specific scenario where **Sarah** has a meltdown
    while in a hazardous area (e.g., a chemical spill in the pump room),
    the Code Purple response becomes a critical part of the Code Green

  - **Protocol:** **Jack**, having received specialized aquatics safety

    entry and rescue\" PPE (respirator, goggles, gloves, etc.) to safely

    can be safely evacuated, allowing trained cleanup crews to handle

# Complete List of Fairlawn Emergency Protocols (c. 2006) 🚨

## General Medical Protocols

- **\"First Response\":** The highest-priority call for a
  **life-threatening** medical emergency (heart attack, unconsciousness,
  etc.). It signals an immediate need for advanced first aid and a
  simultaneous call to 911.a

- **\"First Aid\":** A lower-priority call for **non-life-threatening**
  medical incidents requiring more than a basic bandage from the front
  desk, such as an ice pack, gauze, etc. It does not automatically
  trigger a 911 call.

## Biohazard / Contamination Codes

- **Code Red (Blood):** For any incident involving significant blood.

- **Code Yellow (Vomit):** For any incident involving vomit.

- **Code Brown (Fecal):** For any fecal contamination incident.

## Operational & Safety Codes

- **Code Grey (Disruptive Person):** For an aggressive or violent
  individual requiring supervisor intervention.

- **Code Guardian (Missing Child):** Initiates a facility-wide lockdown
  and search protocol for a missing child.

- **Code Orange (Fire):** For a fire or fire alarm activation, requiring
  immediate evacuation.

- **Code Green (Chemical Hazard) 🧪:** For a hazardous material spill,
  typically involving pool chemicals.

- **Code Indigo (Facility System Failure) 🔌:** For a major building
  system failure like a power outage or burst pipe.

## Weather-Related Codes ⛈️

- **Code White (Lightning):** When lightning is sighted. The pool is
  closed for 30 minutes, with the clock resetting after each subsequent
  strike.

- **Code Black (Tornado):** Activated upon an NWS Wakefield (AKQ)
  tornado warning for the area. Requires immediate sheltering and
  activation of the community warning siren.

- **Code Blue (Severe Weather):** For a severe weather watch or warning,
  requiring staff to monitor conditions and take necessary precautions.
