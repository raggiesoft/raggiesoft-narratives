# Fairlawn Recreation Center - Architecture & Lore

This document serves as a supplementary reference for specific architectural quirks, limitations, and features of the Fairlawn Recreation Center, based on its real-life inspiration (the 1977 Kempsville Recreation Center in Virginia Beach).

## The 1977 Building (Active 1977 - 2015)

The original building functions as a 1:1 drop-in replacement for the Kempsville Recreation Center as it existed from the 1990s through 2006-2007. 

### The Lobby Ramp
*   An iconic, massive ramp in the lobby connects the 1st, 2nd, and 3rd floors.
*   The ramp helixes around the edges of the walls.
*   **Limitation:** Because the building was constructed in the 1970s, the ramp's slope may or may not be compliant with the 1990 ADA standards for wheelchairs.

### The Bowling Alley
*   Original to the 1977 building.
*   It has only 2 lanes.
*   **Limitation:** It is frequently down for maintenance. When it actually works, it feels like a miracle.

### Lounges
*   There is a Youth Lounge (well-documented in character experiences).
*   There is an Adult Lounge, but younger characters (and the narrative POV) have little to no knowledge of its interior or functions.

### Changing Rooms & Locker Rooms
*   The 1977 building **ONLY** features strict Men's and Women's locker rooms.
*   **CRITICAL LIMITATION:** There are absolutely **no** "Assisted Care" or "Family" changing areas. 
*   If any character requires assistance changing into swim gear, it *must* be done by someone of the same gender. Opposite-gender siblings cannot help each other change. This serves as a significant source of narrative friction for opposite-gender sibling pairs.
*   *Note for writers (The "No Magic Eight Balls" Rule):* Characters living in the 2006-2007 timeline have no concept of "Assisted Care" changing rooms, as they were not culturally prevalent at the time. Characters cannot wish for or predict their future existence.

## The 2017 Building (Active 2017 - Present)

The original 1977 building closes in 2015. The brand new building opens in 2017, introducing modern amenities that resolve many of the original building's limitations.

### Assisted Care Facilities
*   The new building features **3 Assisted Care facilities**.
*   Each facility includes:
    *   Toilet
    *   Sink
    *   Shower
    *   A small chair for changing
*   **Purpose:** The small chair was specifically built and included with a parent (or older sibling/guardian) helping an opposite-gender child in mind, officially resolving the changing room limitation from the 1977 era.

# Fairlawn Recreation Center

Knowing that it was (and the modern version still is) **\"a BELOVED
staple of the community\"** is crucial. It shifts the perception from
just a slightly dated, quirky building with some flaws (potholes,
often-broken bowling alley, unreliable snack machines) to a place that
holds genuine affection and importance for the people who use it.

This means:

- **More Than Just a Building:** For the characters in your story,
  especially those who grew up in the area, Fairlawn isn\'t just a place
  to go; it\'s part of their shared history, a landmark filled with
  memories (good, bad, and funny).

- **Community Hub:** Despite its imperfections, it was a central
  gathering place. The fact that it hosted birthday parties, community
  programming, and had lounges for different age groups underscores its
  role as a community hub.

- **Nostalgia and Fondness:** Even with its quirks (like the \"you have
  to call the vendor\" rule for the snack machines), these become part
  of its unique, perhaps even endearing, character for the locals. These
  are the stories people tell.

- **Sense of Ownership:** A \"beloved staple\" often engenders a sense
  of community ownership and pride, even if people also good-naturedly
  complain about its shortcomings.

- **Impact on Alex and Chloe\'s Visit:** Their visit isn\'t just to any
  rec center; it\'s to *Fairlawn*, a place that likely holds some level
  of nostalgic or community significance, even if they are just there
  for a swim and to hang out. This adds a subtle layer of warmth to
  their outing.

Fairlawn Recreation Center (circa 2007) - Comprehensive Details:

### Exterior

Brick construction with a metal roof.

### General Building Info

- The building looked much the same as it did when it opened in 1977.

- The parking lot was known for large potholes.

- The guardian for underage youth (in the youth lounge, gymnasium, snack

### First Floor

- **Front Desk:** Located by the entrance. This desk is UNABLE to help
  with snack machine issues; patrons must call the vendor directly.

- **Lobby:** Contained the massive, dark grey, unpainted concrete spiral
  ramp with railings on either side. The ramp had a reasonable grade but
  wasn\'t up to modern ADA standards, likely requiring a strong person
  to push a wheelchair.

- **Elevator:** Located next to the ramp, usually deactivated. Staff
  needed a key to activate it, primarily for wheelchair users to access
  upper floors. Often bypassed by Alex\'s father, who would wheel him up
  the ramp.

#### Youth Lounge

- Inviting for 14-year-olds like Alex and Chloe (and potentially Luna
  and Leo).

- Featured amenities like pool tables, board games, tabletop games, and
  **arcade machines**.

##### Age Rules

- Ages 4 and under: Parent/guardian had to be *in* the lounge.

- Ages 5-8: Parent/guardian had to be *somewhere in the Rec Center*.

- Ages 9-17: Could be unsupervised.

- Ages 18+: Directed to the Adult Lounge unless accompanying an underage
  patron.

#### Other First Floor Amenities

##### Adult Lounge

Featured similar amenities to the Youth Lounge (pool tables, board
games, tabletop games) but **no arcade machines**.

##### **Snack Bar & Snack Machines**

Accessible to underage kids. The snack machines often took money without
dispensing product.

##### **Meeting Rooms**

A bunch of meeting rooms were located on the first floor, in the same
wing as the snack lounge.

- On weekends, these were often rented out for child birthday parties,
  gatherings, baby showers, etc..

- During weekdays, they were used for Rec Center programming.

##### Bowling Alley

- Families permitted.

- Often down for maintenance.

- Youth ages 14+ could bowl unsupervised if it was operational.

*(The boxing ring and movie theater were long gone by 1999).*

### Second Floor

**Check-in Desk:** Located at the top of the ramp from the first floor.

#### Natatorium (Pool Area) & Changing Rooms

- **Changing Rooms:** No family changing room. Alex would need male

- **Pool:** 25 meters end-to-end; shallow end sloped 3-5 ft, deep end

- Youth ages 12-13 could play Racquetball/Walleyball with

- Teenagers ages 14+ permitted unsupervised.

#### Basketball Gymnasium

Open to anyone age 9+ unsupervised during Open Gym times.

### Third Floor (Restricted Access via Second Floor Check-in/Keyed Elevator)

#### Weight Room

- Youth ages 12-13 permitted with parent/guardian supervision.

## Fairlawn Recreation Center: Operational Notes (2007)

### The \"Code Purple\" Protocol

This is a specific, crucial reasonable accommodation developed for the
Bennett siblings.

- **Initial Status:** The \"Code Purple\" system began as an **informal,
  unwritten understanding** among the compassionate staff and
  supervisors at Fairlawn. They knew Jack and Sarah were each other\'s
  primary support system and allowed them to respond to each other\'s
  needs during a crisis without a formal policy.

- **The System Failure:** The protocol\'s critical flaw was exposed the
  first time Sarah had an autistic meltdown in a **secure,
  key-restricted aquatics area** (like the pump or filter room). Jack,
  hearing the call, rushed to her location but was **blocked by a locked
  door**, leaving him helpless and unable to provide the immediate
  support the system was designed for.

- **Formalization and Training:** This failure prompted an immediate,
  official review.

  - The siblings\' reasonable accommodations were officially updated to
    include a formalized \"Code Purple\" protocol.

  - As part of this official update, Jack was required to undergo **all
    necessary aquatics safety training**, including watching safety
    videos and receiving briefings on heavy equipment, chemical handling
    (HazCom), and other dangers, ensuring his safety when entering these
    restricted areas.

  - Upon completion, Jack was issued a key granting him access to all
    aquatics areas, ensuring a locked door would never again be a
    barrier.

  - It is universally understood by **all staff** that a Code Purple
    call requires the designated sibling to respond **immediately**,
    without needing to ask for permission.

# Protocols Directly Involving the Bennett Siblings

These protocols are the most significant for Jack and Sarah\'s story,
representing their specific, recognized accommodations and the staff\'s
compassionate understanding of their needs.

- **Code Purple (Bennett Emergency) 🟣**

  - **Meaning:** A specific, formal reasonable accommodation for
    **Jack** or **Sarah Bennett** experiencing an autistic meltdown.

  - **Activation:** Can be called verbally over the radio. In a case
    where the meltdown is severe enough to cause situational mutism, the
    sound of the sibling **crying over the radio** is also recognized by
    all staff as a valid and urgent Code Purple call.

  - **Response:** The designated sibling is to respond immediately,
    without needing permission. This protocol **overrides all standard
    rules of workplace conduct**, including the general guideline to
    moderate physical affection. The responding sibling\'s primary duty
    is to provide grounding comfort, which often requires **holding the
    other sibling very tightly** to help them through the sensory storm
    of the meltdown.

- **Code Black: Bennett Sibling Protocol**

  - **Context:** During a **Code Black (Tornado)**, a specific
    accommodation is in place for the Bennetts.

  - **Protocol:** If it is safely possible, staff will ensure that
    **Jack** and **Sarah Bennett** are brought to the **same designated
    interior safe space**. The policy of calling a Code Black the moment
    NWS Wakefield (AKQ) issues a warning is specifically designed to
    provide the precious extra minutes that might be needed to
    accomplish this task safely.

- **Code Green: Bennett Sibling Protocol (Rescue)**

  - **Context:** In the specific scenario where **Sarah** has a meltdown
    while in a hazardous area (e.g., a chemical spill in the pump room),
    the Code Purple response becomes a critical part of the Code Green
    solution.

  - **Protocol:** **Jack**, having received specialized aquatics safety
    and HazCom training, is authorized to don the appropriate \"rapid
    entry and rescue\" PPE (respirator, goggles, gloves, etc.) to safely
    enter the hazardous area. His sole mission is to ground Sarah so she
    can be safely evacuated, allowing trained cleanup crews to handle
    the chemical hazard.

# Complete List of Fairlawn Emergency Protocols (c. 2006) 🚨

## General Medical Protocols

- **\"First Response\":** The highest-priority call for a
  **life-threatening** medical emergency (heart attack, unconsciousness,
  etc.). It signals an immediate need for advanced first aid and a
  simultaneous call to 911.a

- **\"First Aid\":** A lower-priority call for **non-life-threatening**
  medical incidents requiring more than a basic bandage from the front
  desk, such as an ice pack, gauze, etc. It does not automatically
  trigger a 911 call.

## Biohazard / Contamination Codes

- **Code Red (Blood):** For any incident involving significant blood.

- **Code Yellow (Vomit):** For any incident involving vomit.

- **Code Brown (Fecal):** For any fecal contamination incident.

## Operational & Safety Codes

- **Code Grey (Disruptive Person):** For an aggressive or violent
  individual requiring supervisor intervention.

- **Code Guardian (Missing Child):** Initiates a facility-wide lockdown
  and search protocol for a missing child.

- **Code Orange (Fire):** For a fire or fire alarm activation, requiring
  immediate evacuation.

- **Code Green (Chemical Hazard) 🧪:** For a hazardous material spill,
  typically involving pool chemicals.

- **Code Indigo (Facility System Failure) 🔌:** For a major building
  system failure like a power outage or burst pipe.

## Weather-Related Codes ⛈️

- **Code White (Lightning):** When lightning is sighted. The pool is
  closed for 30 minutes, with the clock resetting after each subsequent
  strike.

- **Code Black (Tornado):** Activated upon an NWS Wakefield (AKQ)
  tornado warning for the area. Requires immediate sheltering and
  activation of the community warning siren.

- **Code Blue (Severe Weather):** For a severe weather watch or warning,
  requiring staff to monitor conditions and take necessary precautions.
