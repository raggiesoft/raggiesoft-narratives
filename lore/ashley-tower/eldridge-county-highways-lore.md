# Eldridge County & Meridian City Highway Infrastructure

## The Scale of Meridian City
In this universe, Skagit County is replaced by Eldridge County, home to **Meridian City**. Meridian is not a sleepy suburban outpost—it is a **MASSIVE Tier 1 metro** situated north of Seattle and south of Vancouver, BC. It has a dense, towering, futuristic skyline and deals with the severe, large-scale traffic gridlock typical of a major North American city.

## Interstate 96 (I-96)
I-96 is the major east-west federal highway. Unlike traditional highways that start in a downtown grid, I-96 originates directly at the water at the **Anacortes Ferry Terminal**. It acts as the physical, concrete beginning of the federal highway, designed specifically to pull heavy international (Sidney, BC) and island (San Juan Islands) maritime vehicle traffic directly out of the port. It shoots east, cutting through Meridian City, before intersecting with I-5 (located geographically where I-5 meets SR-20 in Burlington in the real world). 

## Interstate 296 (I-296)
I-296 is a classic tale of ambitious, unfinished American highway infrastructure. 

It was originally designed to be a massive, continuous circuit loop around Meridian City and Eldridge County to alleviate I-5 and I-96 congestion. However, only a fraction of it was actually completed.

**The Completed Segment:**
*   **The Southwest Spur:** The only built segment of I-296 runs from **I-5 at Conway, WA** (Exit 221) heading northwest until it terminates at **I-96 at Whitney** (near the Swinomish Channel Bridge). 

**The Unbuilt Segments (The Missing Loop):**
1.  **The Coastal Extension:** The highway was supposed to continue north from the I-96 Whitney interchange, running up along the coast before swinging east to reconnect with I-5 at Exit 236 (Bow Hill area).
2.  **The Eastern Arc:** On the eastern side, a massive arc was planned from I-5 at Conway, sweeping northeast toward Sedro-Woolley. It was designed to cross *over* I-96, and then swing northwest to reconnect with I-5 at Exit 236, forming the completed loop. 

### The Gridlock Reality
Because the I-296 loop was never finished, traffic cannot easily bypass the Meridian City core. Commuters and travelers heading north from Seattle (or Federal Way) must face the absolute nightmare of I-5 traffic as it plunges straight through the massive, dense urban center of Meridian City just to reach the I-96 interchange.
