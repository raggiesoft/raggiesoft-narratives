## 1. High-Concept Premise

On **Tuesday, June 20, 2000**—the day they turn eighteen—triplets **Luke, Tessa, and Sadie Bridger** (fraternal brother Luke and his identical twin sisters Tessa and Sadie) finally hit the breaking point of a years-long cold war with their parents. All their lives, the three of them genuinely loved doing everything together; all their lives, their parents screamed at them to "act like normal siblings, spread their wings, and go their separate ways." When they turn eighteen and choose to stick together instead, their parents throw them out on the spot.

Their childhood house backs directly up to **Colville National Forest** in remote Northeast Washington (`Kettle River Range`, Ferry County—**100% non-volcanic**). Shouldering three canvas packs, a weathered blue-and-white dome tent, `$412` in saved chore-and-yardwork cash, hand tools, and their late maternal grandfather **Owen Sawyer's** 1974 **Clearwater** steel-string acoustic guitar—and no swimwear—they step past the back boundary fence and wander miles deep into the pines just to clear their heads and think.

Then comes their first night together in the deep woods without anyone screaming through the walls at them: **pure, unbroken bliss.**

Beside a cold, clear granite-bed mountain stream they christen **Cedar Run**, a single night away to think turns into a daring realization: *why ever walk back out to be split apart?* With roughly a hundred days of warm golden mountain summer ahead of them, Luke, Tessa, and Sadie set out to build a makeshift timber-and-stone shelter by hand and expand it piece by piece before October snow closes the Kettle Crest—writing warm 1970s-style mountain-meadow acoustic songs by firelight that will eventually reach far beyond the trees.

## 2. The Bridger Triplets (`Born June 20, 1982`)

### A. Luke Bridger (`pov: "Luke"`, Fraternal Triplet Brother — The Builder & Anchor)
- **Appearance:** `5'11"`, lean athletic build from summers hauling lumber and mowing acreage; tousled sandy-brown / dirty-blond hair that bleaches lighter in July sun, warm hazel-brown eyes, calloused carpenter's hands. Wears heather-gray crew-neck tees, faded olive flannel overshirts, denim jeans, and weathered leather work boots.
- **Role & Skills:** Steady structural thinker and protective center of the trio. Learned framing carpentry, mortise-and-peg joinery, splitting cedar shakes with a froe and wooden mallet, and dry-stone masonry working summers for Grandpa Owen before Owen passed in 1998.
- **Voice (`"I"` Narrator):** Grounded, observant, quietly protective, tactile—notices how wood grain splits, how cold creek water feels on split knuckles, and the exact micro-differences between Tessa's and Sadie's footsteps before he even turns around.
- **Singing Role:** Warm baritone-tenor anchor; holds the low harmony and rhythm guitar foundation when Sadie or Tessa takes the lead melody.

### B. Tessa Bridger (Identical Twin Sister #1 — The Trail Mechanic & Hearth-Keeper)
- **Appearance:** `5'6"`, identical twin facial bone structure, sun-kissed freckles, gray-hazel eyes, and long warm chestnut-brown hair (almost always worn **pulled half-back or braided** to keep it clear of saw blades, snare cordage, and campfire smoke). Wears modest cream/henley cotton crew-neck shirts, a tan canvas utility vest (`Ironclad` workwear), classic blue jeans, and a **braided brown leather cord bracelet** on her left wrist.
- **Role & Skills:** Fearless hands-on problem solver, trail mechanic, knot-and-rigging specialist, and camp cook/forager. Can tension a ridge-tarp in a downpour without a wrinkle, identify huckleberry, thimbleberry, morels, and wild rosehip, rig a bear-safe counterbalance hang fifty feet up a western larch, and repair anything mechanical from a bicycle derailleur to a seized Coleman-style dual-fuel camp stove (`TrailFlame` camp stove).
- **Personality:** Direct, kinetic, quick with dry humor when morale dips; processes stress by *building or fixing something physical right now*.
- **Singing Role:** Clear, earthy alto-mezzo; sings high/middle twin harmony and taps steady percussive rhythm on a salvaged wooden crate / tambourine ring or second thrift-barn parlor guitar.

### C. Sadie Bridger (Identical Twin Sister #2 — The Strategist, Medic & Songwriter)
- **Appearance:** `5'6"`, exact identical twin mirror to Tessa in face, gray-hazel eyes, and long chestnut-brown hair (usually worn **loose over her shoulders**), distinguished at a glance by her modest **navy-and-white striped crew-neck cotton tee**, soft burgundy overshirt/cardigan, and a **woven burgundy-and-navy thread bracelet** on her left wrist.
- **Role & Skills:** Quiet strategist, map-reader, budget accountant, camp medic, and primary lyricist. Guards their laminated USGS `1:24,000` quadrangle maps (`Republic` / `Sherman Pass` / `Kettle Falls`), tracks every dollar in a waterproof pocket ledger, knows wilderness first aid, and carries **Grandpa Owen's 1974 Clearwater D-28** steel-string dreadnought guitar in its duct-taped hardshell case.
- **Personality:** Thoughtful, perceptive, steel-spined beneath a gentle exterior; spots legal, social, and logistical traps three moves ahead (including the USFS 14-day dispersed-camping rule and how to avoid leaving paper trails their father could trace).
- **Singing Role:** Pure, crystalline 1970s mountain-folk soprano-mezzo lead and fingerstyle acoustic guitarist; hears three-part vocal harmonies in her head like architecture.

### D. Why Their Parents Threw Them Out (`Richard & Evelyn Bridger`) & The Back-Fence Departure
- **Years of Screaming Tension Over Sticking Together:** Richard and Evelyn Bridger live on a five-acre rural parcel outside Kettle Ford whose back fence borders **Colville National Forest**. For years, home was a pressure cooker of yelling and resentment for one central reason: Luke, Tessa, and Sadie genuinely preferred each other's company—working, studying, playing Grandpa Owen's guitar, and curling up together—while their parents relentlessly demanded they stop acting like a single unit, "be normal siblings," spread their wings, and go separate ways after graduation.
- **The 18th Birthday Eviction (`June 20, 2000`):** When the triplets turned eighteen and told their parents plainly that they were staying together wherever they went next, another shouting match erupted and Richard and Evelyn threw all three out of the house.
- **Wandering Into the Forest -> Night One of Pure Bliss:** Grabbing three backpacks, their birth certificates, `$412` in saved cash, the old blue-and-white dome tent, Grandpa Owen's tool roll, and his 1974 Clearwater guitar (and zero swimwear), they didn't walk down the driveway toward the highway—they stepped through the back timberline gate and hiked miles up past the old washed-out logging spur deep into **Colville National Forest** simply to think. That first night under the ponderosa pines—zero shouting, zero doors slamming, just creek water and all three of them piled together—felt like pure bliss, planting the seed to stay and build their own shelter.
- **Natural Creek Dips & Visual Framing Rule (*1998 The Parent Trap* PG Blocking):** Having left without swimwear—and knowing wet cotton jeans and flannels turn freezing after sundown in mountain country—the eighteen-year-old triplets stack their clothes dry on sun-warmed granite boulders to wash off sweat and cedar pitch in Cedar Run's deep snowmelt pool. In prose, this is written with clean, non-sexualized, matter-of-fact literary naturalism: they have shared every space since before birth, never feel awkward or look away from one another, and cuddle together naturally in sunbeams and blankets for warmth. For illustrations, always use classic **1998 *The Parent Trap* PG framing**: neatly folded jeans and flannel shirts in the foreground on a warm creekside boulder, with only heads and collarbones/shoulders visible above dark-emerald reflective water (or wrapped afterward in dry flannels/blankets by the keyhole fire).

## 3. Geography & Ecology: Colville National Forest (`Zero Volcanoes`)

- **Strict Geological Rule:** **100% Non-Volcanic.** Colville National Forest sits in **Ferry, Pend Oreille, and Stevens Counties** in remote Northeast Washington (`Kettle River Range` and `Selkirk Mountains`), formed of ancient granite batholiths, quartzite ridges, and glacial valleys—**hundreds of miles away from the Cascade volcanic arc**, with **zero volcanoes** anywhere on the horizon.
- **Fictional Micro-Geography Inside Real Public Land:**
  - **Cedar Run Creek:** A fictional cold, spring-fed granite-and-gravel tributary tucked off a washed-out, decommissioned 1960s USFS logging spur (` Spur 470-B`) high above the Kettle River corridor off `State Route 20` (`Sherman Pass Scenic Byway`).
  - **Kettle Ford, WA:** Our fictional unincorporated highway crossroads (`pop. 640`) along `WA-20` between real-world **Republic** and **Kettle Falls**—home to the **Kettle Ford General Store & Post Office** (with an outdoor coin payphone booth), **Sawtooth Lumber & Hardware**, the **Kettle Valley Grange Hall**, a tiny Carnegie-style **County Library Branch**, and a twice-daily rural **Northeast Tri-County Transit (`NETT`)** flag-stop cutaway van (`$1.00` cash fare).
- **Sheltered Valley Microclimate, Soothing Rain & Periodic Quiet Snow (`1,900 ft` Elevation):**
  - Rather than sitting on an exposed `5,500-foot` alpine pass, **Cedar Run** winds through a low, wind-sheltered `1,900-foot` granite-and-cedar bench valley where Pacific moisture fronts bring **frequent, gentle mountain rainstorms** from late spring through autumn—and **mild, survivable winters** punctuated by **periodic quiet snowfalls** (`6–14 inches` of hushed powder blanketing the cedar boughs for a week at a time before melting back into the moss, rather than lethal sub-zero blizzards).
  - **Why the Triplets Love the Rain:** After eighteen years in a house where silence always felt brittle right before the next shouting match, **Luke, Tessa, and Sadie genuinely love the rain**. Standing out under warm summer rain showers splashing cool across their faces, or curling up dry inside their shelter listening to the steady, rhythmic **white noise of rainwater pattering against split-cedar shakes, bark shingles, or stone eaves**, is their ultimate sensory sanctuary.
- **Bortle Class 1 Night Skies & The Milky Way Overhead:**
  - Deep inside Colville National Forest—over eighty miles from any major city light dome—Cedar Run sits beneath **pristine Bortle Class 1 dark skies**. Whenever a night rainstorm clears out before midnight and washes every speck of dust and pine pollen from the air, the **Milky Way** blazes from horizon to horizon between the black ponderosa crowns, bright enough to cast a faint silver shimmer across the dark creek pools while the three of them lie on warm granite or lean off their porch looking up in wonder at what's out there among the stars.

## 4. Progressive Homestead Engineering (`How the Shelter Grows`)

1. **Stage 1 — Night-One Dome Tent & Sloped Ridge-Fly (`Late June 2000`)**
   - Pitching their weathered blue-and-white two-person dome tent on a high, dry pine-needle bench twenty yards above Cedar Run's flood mark, rigging a sloped heavy poly-canvas tarp fly overhead for rain runoff, digging perimeter drainage channels, hanging food bags on a `50-foot` larch counterbalance line, and building a keyhole stone hearth for smokeless dry pine/larch cooking fires.
2. **Stage 2 — Raised Deadfall Cedar Platform & A-Frame Shelter (`July 2000`)**
   - Harvesting *exclusively fallen deadfall* **western red cedar** and **Douglas fir** (honoring National Forest live-tree rules and leaving zero fresh chainsaw/axe stumps): splitting straight-grained cedar bolts with Grandpa Owen's steel froe and a hardwood maul into rot-proof floorboards and overlapping roof shakes, raising a three-person sleeping platform `18 inches` off the cold ground.
3. **Stage 3 — Enclosed Split-Cedar Wall, Moss Chinking & Covered Guitar Porch (`August–September 2000`)**
   - Framing vertical cedar-plank walls chinked with dry clay-and-moss mortar, laying a river-stone heat-retaining fireplace & chimney draft hood, building a three-stage gravel-sand-charcoal water filter fed by an uphill rock spring, and extending the front eaves four feet over a split-log bench—**the Cedar Run Porch**—where Sadie, Tessa, and Luke play acoustic music every evening at dusk.
4. **Stage 4 — Winterized Creek Homestead (`October 2000+`, Shaped by Crossroads Route)**
   - Banking four full cords of seasoned tamarack and fir under dry bark eaves, digging a frost-proof creek-bank root cellar lined with flat slate stones, and securing heavy wool blankets, bulk oats/beans/flour/rice, and winter boots before the Kettle Crest snowline drops.
