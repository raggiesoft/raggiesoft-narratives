# Chunk 24: The Grievance (Seattle Union Hall)

The fluorescent lights of the IBEW Local 77 union hall buzzed with a dull, irritating hum.

Barrett sat at a scuffed folding table, his arms crossed tightly over his chest. He had officially filed a grievance under the Collective Bargaining Agreement, citing "unjust reassignment" and "management overreach." He expected the union to march into Marcus Cole’s office and demand his winter deployment—and the hazard pay that came with it—be immediately reinstated.

Sitting across from him were two men.

Dawson, the official Union Representative, was a pragmatist with a thick file folder open in front of him. Beside him was Miller, the Shop Steward. Miller was a heavily scarred, fifty-something veteran lineman who had spent over a decade working the Skagit River Hydroelectric Project before his knees gave out. He knew the ice, and he knew exactly who operated above it.

Dawson closed the file folder, folding his hands on top of it.

"We're not advancing the grievance, Barrett," Dawson stated plainly. "The union is withdrawing the complaint. Your reassignment to the downtown transit yard stands."

Barrett bolted upright, slamming his hand on the plastic table. "What? Why? Marcus Cole openly admitted he pulled me off the dam because a civilian hotel owner told him to! I'm a dues-paying member. You are supposed to protect my job from outside contractors dictating our deployments!"

"We _are_ protecting a dues-paying member," Miller growled, his voice deep and entirely devoid of sympathy. He leaned forward, resting his heavy forearms on the table. "We're protecting Henderson."

Barrett faltered, his mouth opening and closing.

"I read the incident report," Dawson continued, tapping the file. "Henderson is a thirty-year union brother. He was pinned under a metric ton of Cascades concrete. And instead of stabilizing the catwalk and administering trauma care, you panicked. You tried to make a cell phone call from a dead zone, and then you screamed into the VHF for a helicopter that physically could not launch."

"I was following lowland emergency protocols!" Barrett argued, his face flushing red.

"You weren't in the lowlands," Miller interrupted, his tone laced with absolute disgust. "You were behind the steel. Milepost 134 isn't just a jurisdiction line, Barrett; it is a different planet. When that gate locks, you are in the Air-Gap. The state can't save you. The city can't save you. You either survive by the physics of the gorge, or you die out there."

"So you're just going to let Victoria Vance run Seattle City Light?" Barrett demanded, grasping at straws. "You guys are scared of a woman who runs a bed and breakfast?"

Miller let out a short, harsh laugh. He looked at Dawson, shaking his head, before locking his eyes back on the rookie.

"You really don't get it," Miller said softly. "Nobody in this hall is afraid of Victoria Vance. We don't bow to her because we're scared. We bow to her because she is _right_. She is the Matriarch of Ruby Mountain. Her family maintains the only heavy extraction capability, the only medical firewall, and the only localized telemetry grid capable of keeping our men alive when the sky falls. She doesn't manage a hotel; she commands a sovereign survival perimeter."

Dawson slid a copy of the reassignment paperwork across the table.

"Victoria Vance called Marcus Cole because you actively endangered the life of a union brother, and you proved that your threat-detection processor is catastrophically broken," Dawson explained, his tone completely absolute. "If you go back up that mountain, your lowland ego is going to get someone killed. Victoria knows it, Marcus knows it, and the union knows it."

Barrett stared at the paper, the reality finally crushing the last remnants of his arrogance. He wasn't just being punished by management; his own brotherhood was exiling him.

"The Vanguard dug a trench through a whiteout to save one of our own," Miller said, standing up from the table and looking down at the disgraced engineer. "The union honors that debt. We don't disrespect the women of the Keep, and we sure as hell don't send liabilities back into their territory. Sign the paper, Barrett. Enjoy the transit yard."
