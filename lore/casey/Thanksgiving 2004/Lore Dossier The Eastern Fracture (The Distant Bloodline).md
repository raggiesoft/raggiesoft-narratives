# Lore Dossier: The Eastern Fracture (The Distant Bloodline)

**The Divergence of the Fortune** The Vance family fortune originates with Laurent Vance, a rugged Québécois prospector who traveled north to the Yukon for the Klondike Gold Rush in 1897 and struck a staggering vein of gold. While Laurent brought his vast fortune south in 1903 to build The Prospector's Keep in the absolute isolation of the North Cascades, not all of his children inherited his obsession with the alpine wilderness.

Laurent had two sons. The eldest remained in Washington State, anchoring the bloodline to the mountain—the line that eventually produced Victoria Vance and the exiled aunts and uncles.

The younger son, however, despised the freezing winters and the claustrophobic isolation of the Cascades. In the late 1920s, he demanded a one-time, flat-cash buyout of his inheritance, legally severing his ties to the mountain estate forever. He moved to the East Coast, settling in Virginia to pursue a vastly different, disconnected life.

**The Ignorance of Generations** Because that eastern branch of the family was formally severed from the estate long before Victoria Vance seized control and established the modern Vance-Whitaker Trust in 1999, there are no shared legal records.

Tom Vance (the neglectful father of Cassandra, David, and Casey) is the direct grandson of that exiled eastern son. Tom was entirely disinterested in his own family history; he never bothered to research his lineage, and he certainly never told his children about a theoretical gold-rush ancestor.

## The Current Reality (Spring 2005)

Cassandra Vance operates in the executive suite of Building 33 in Redmond, fiercely protecting her siblings, entirely oblivious to the fact that she shares a great-grandfather with the Matriarch of Ruby Mountain.

Meanwhile, Victoria Vance—the 42-year-old Sole Trustee and legal guardian of the Vanguard pack—operates her sovereign fortress under the assumption that she legally exiled the only remaining threats to her bloodline in 1999. She has absolutely no idea that three highly competent, fiercely loyal third-cousins are living just hours away in King County.

# Chunk 19.5: The Vanguard Briefing (April 8, 2005)

The morning light streaming through the massive frosted windowpanes of the Great Hall was sharp and clear, reflecting off the remaining snowbanks outside. The Keep had been physically sealed off from the world for months, but today, the perimeter was officially opening.

Victoria Vance stood in front of the roaring river-rock fireplace, holding a single manila folder.

Arrayed in front of her, entirely abandoning their morning maintenance and culinary prep, were the adult members of the Vanguard. Morgan, Megan, Diana, and Lauren stood in a loose, formidable line. Clara and Maya flanked them. Sarah and Ashleigh sat on the edge of a leather Chesterfield sofa, while Amanda stood securely behind Johnny’s manual wheelchair.

"I have authorized a commercial breach of our perimeter," Victoria announced, her voice echoing with cold, absolute authority in the 1890s timber-framed lobby. "We have a party of four arriving at the Lower Motorpool at approximately 16:00 hours."

Morgan crossed her arms, her brow furrowing. "We just got the WSDOT gates open four days ago. Why are we letting civilians up the mountain before the spring thaw is even finished?"

"Because they are not civilians," Victoria stated, opening the manila folder. "The primary billing contact is a woman named Cassandra Vance. She is a Vice President of Executive Operations at Quantum, and she just paid the absolute maximum public rate to buy out the entire mountain for a weekend later this summer."

The room went dead silent. The name _Vance_ hung in the air like a dropped pin.

Johnny’s fingers moved over his Quantum XN keyboard, his text-to-speech engine cutting through the quiet. "I ran the forensic trace. She is Laurent Vance's great-granddaughter. She is blood."

A ripple of shock went through the older cousins.

"The eastern branch," Sarah Foster breathed out, her legal mind instantly connecting the historical dots. "The son who took the buyout in the twenties."

"Exactly," Victoria nodded. "However, after the events of 2003, I do not believe in coincidences. Vanessa Hayes utilized fake credentials and a geographical bait-and-switch to infiltrate our perimeter and target Johnny. Until I look this Quantum executive in the eye and verify her intentions, we treat this arrival as a potential hostile incursion."

Victoria closed the folder and turned her sharp, sovereign gaze toward the seventeen-year-old Perimeter Architect.

"Emma," Victoria commanded.

Emma Bennett straightened her posture, resting her hand on the heavy radio clipped to her chest harness. "Yes, Victoria."

"You will be operating the Lower Motorpool when they arrive," Victoria instructed. "You will greet them. You will verify their identity. You will not disclose our genetic connection, and you will not offer them standard hospitality pleasantries. You will cross-load them into the Ridgeback and you will personally drive them up Prospector Road."

"Understood," Emma nodded firmly. "I'll radio Command the second their tires hit the bunker."

Victoria turned her attention to the rest of the pack.

"When Emma's shuttle reaches the summit portico, I want this entire floor assembled," the Matriarch ordered, her eyes sweeping over the heavy operations mechanics and the logisticians. "I want a physical wall between the front doors and Johnny. I want them to walk into this lobby and instantly understand that they are entirely out of their depth. If Cassandra Vance is here to play games with our bloodline, we are going to end it before she even unloads her wheelchair."

"We've got the line, Vic," Morgan promised, her tone dropping into a lethal, protective register.

Victoria gave a single, satisfied nod.

"Good," Victoria said. "Let the executives come to the mountain. We will be waiting."

# Chunk 20: The Bloodline Summit (April 2005)

The drive from Redmond to the North Cascades was a quiet, comfortable cruise.

Liz was behind the wheel of the brand-new minivan she had recently purchased. Since she and I took the bus for almost all of our daily commuting and grocery runs, the minivan was our first major, shared vehicular investment. It was primarily chosen for its cavernous interior space, allowing us to easily and securely stow Cass’s manual travel wheelchair without having to break it down.

It was Friday afternoon, April 8th. We had driven straight to Casey’s high school, pulling her directly out of her final period class before hitting Interstate 5 North. The Washington State Department of Transportation had officially opened the highway gates just four days prior, meaning we were some of the very first civilians to access the high country for the 2005 season.

Two hours later, Liz banked the minivan off State Route 20, plunging down the steep concrete ramp into a sprawling, subterranean bunker.

The climate-controlled garage was an astonishing piece of infrastructure. As Liz brought the minivan to a halt in the arrival lane, a young woman wearing a heavy black peacoat, high-visibility gear, and a radio harness walked up to the driver's side window.

"Welcome to The Prospector's Keep," the young woman said, her voice a smooth, practiced hospitality baseline. She held a heavy clipboard. "Reservation name?"

"Cassandra Vance," Cass answered from the passenger seat. "We booked the weekend to tour the property."

The young woman's pen hovered over the clipboard. She didn't gasp, but her posture instantly locked into a state of absolute, militarized focus. Her dark eyes swept over Cass, darted to me, tracked Liz, and finally landed on Casey.

She reached for the heavy radio clipped to her chest harness.

"Command, this is Perimeter," she broadcasted, her voice dropping all pretense of customer service, shifting into a cold, tactical register. "The Vance party has arrived. I am transferring Motorpool command to Beta-Shift. I am personally driving them to the summit. Have the floor assemble."

"Copy that, Perimeter," a static-laced voice replied instantly. "We're waiting."

The young woman unclipped her radio and looked back at Liz. "Leave the keys in the ignition, please. We will transfer your luggage and the wheelchair into the Ridgeback."

She didn't explain the radio call. She efficiently helped us cross-load into one of the massive, chained 4x4 transit vans. She climbed into the driver's seat and put the heavy vehicle into gear, leaving a subordinate to manage the bunker as we began the brutal, five-mile serpentine ascent up Prospector Road.

We broke through the timberline at 7,000 feet, and the true, towering majesty of The Prospector's Keep loomed against the jagged skyline.

The driver parked the Ridgeback under the cobblestone portico, deployed the hydraulic ramp for Cass, and pushed the heavy oak front doors open for us.

We stepped into the Great Hall.

The 1890s timber-framed lobby was a masterpiece of crimson velvet and roaring walk-in fireplaces. But the opulence of the room was entirely overshadowed by the sheer, terrifying human wall waiting for us.

There were no bellhops. There were no concierges smiling behind the mahogany reception desk. Instead, forming a sweeping, defensive semicircle in the center of the lobby was a massive group of women.

They looked less like hotel staff and more like a paramilitary unit. Two women were wearing heavy canvas coveralls stained with diesel grease. Another woman stood in a pristine, white double-breasted chef's coat. Flanking the center were a pair of women radiating an intense, lethal protectiveness. And sitting dead center behind the mahogany desk, wearing a tailored charcoal blazer, was an imposing woman in her early forties. Parked directly beside her was a young man in a manual wheelchair, his hands resting on a heavy Quantum XN laptop mounted to his chair's frame. Standing immediately behind him, her hands fiercely gripping his shoulders, was a young woman with a long braid.

Cassandra wheeled herself forward, her executive processor instantly recognizing the overwhelming, highly unusual show of force. This wasn't a standard, European-style hospitality greeting. She hesitated for a microsecond, clearly thrown completely off her game, before shifting flawlessly into her Building 33 executive baseline.

"I am Cassandra Vance," Cass stated smoothly, projecting her voice across the quiet, echoing timber. "I assume from the welcoming committee that our booking is in order?"

The woman behind the desk did not look at a computer screen. She stared directly at Cassandra.

"Your booking is fully funded, Ms. Vance," the woman said, her voice a cold, resonant blade that carried the sovereign authority of the mountain. "However, I do not believe in coincidences. Not when it involves the security of my family."

Cass frowned slightly, adjusting her glasses. "I don't understand. Are there issues with the liability waivers?"

"There are no issues with the paperwork," she countered, stepping out from behind the mahogany desk. "But when a Quantum executive sharing my exact surname buys out my entire operational grid, I run a forensic trace."

The imposing woman paced slowly across the Persian rug, her sharp eyes dissecting all four of us.

"Laurent Vance, the man who built this fortress with Klondike gold in 1903, had two sons," she explained, her voice echoing in the absolute silence of the Great Hall. "My grandfather stayed on this mountain to maintain the empire. The younger son despised the cold. He took a cash buyout in the 1920s, permanently severing his ties to this estate, and moved to Virginia."

She stopped dead in front of Cassandra’s wheelchair.

"You are not a client," she stated, the paranoia finally bleeding out of her posture, replaced by a profound, calculating realization. "I am Victoria Vance. And you are Laurent Vance’s great-granddaughter. You are blood."

My processor completely stalled.

Cassandra froze, her hands gripping the wheels of her chair. She looked at Victoria, then at the sprawling army of highly competent, fiercely protective women standing behind the desk.

"We're... related?" Casey gasped, her dark eyes wide as she looked at the sheer scale of the family we never knew we had.

"We are," Victoria confirmed, a rare, razor-thin smile touching her lips. "I legally exiled the rest of my generation from this mountain in 1999 to protect my cousins from their greed. I assumed my pack was all that was left. I had no idea a highly competent, entirely separate branch of the family was operating in the executive suites of Redmond."

"This is impossible," Cass breathed out, entirely stunned. "My father never said a word about Washington State. Or gold."

"Your father likely didn't know," one of the sharp-eyed twins standing near the desk chimed in. "The eastern branch was severed eighty years ago. You just accidentally rented your own ancestral fortress."

The young man in the wheelchair moved his stiff fingers over his mechanical keyboard. The flat, digitized voice of his text-to-speech software suddenly chimed into the room.

"Data correlation," his laptop announced. "Your booking profile lists your brother, David Vance, as a primary guest. The birthdate registered on the liability waiver is April 8, 1985."

I blinked, taking a step forward. "Yeah. Today is my birthday. I'm turning twenty."

The young woman standing behind the wheelchair let out a short, breathless laugh. She looked at me, her protective stance melting entirely into pure shock. She squeezed the young man's shoulders.

"David," she said, her voice echoing into the quiet room. "Today is April 8th. Johnny and I were born on April 8, 1985. We are turning twenty today, too."

The air in the Great Hall seemed to physically shift.

I looked at the young man—Johnny. He looked back at me. We were exactly the same age, born on the exact same day on opposite sides of the country, both carrying the exact same bloodline, entirely insulated by incredibly powerful older sisters who would burn the world down to keep us safe.

Liz tightened her grip on my hand, looking around the room at the staggering display of unity and protection. She recognized the frequency instantly. Victoria’s pack wasn't just a group of hotel operators; they were the exact same fiercely loyal, deeply structured family architecture that Cass had built for us.

Victoria Vance looked at Cassandra, the final remnants of the Matriarch's icy defensive wall dissolving completely.

"Well, Cassandra," Victoria smiled, gesturing to the roaring fireplaces and the sprawling estate. "It appears we have three twentieth birthdays to celebrate today. Welcome home."
