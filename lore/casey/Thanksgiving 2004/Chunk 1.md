# Chunk 1: The Arrival and The Mask (Wednesday)

The drive down Interstate 64 to Norfolk International Airport was buzzing with anticipation. I had both hands gripping the steering wheel of my mother’s car, my leg bouncing lightly against the floorboard. It had been months since I had seen Cassandra and Casey, and my processor was eagerly counting down the minutes until their flight from Seattle landed.

Sitting in the passenger seat, Kristine watched the highway markers pass by.

"Your mom was really nice to let us borrow the car for this," Kristine noted, checking her reflection in the passenger-side sun visor mirror and smoothing her hair.

"Yeah, it was her idea," I smiled, navigating the exit toward the ORF arrivals terminal. "She knew it would be easier for us to just grab them at the curb and drive straight back to her place in Virginia Beach so they can see her and Peter."

Kristine offered a polite nod, snapping the visor shut. "I just hope they aren't too exhausted from the flight. We have a busy schedule."

I parked the car in the short-term lot, and we walked into the terminal. The moment I spotted my sisters coming down the escalator toward baggage claim, the ambient noise of the airport completely vanished.

"Bug!" I called out.

Casey dropped her carry-on bag instantly. She broke into a run, her face lighting up with absolute joy. I met her halfway, wrapping my arms around her and lifting her slightly off the floor in a massive, deep-pressure bear hug. She buried her face in my neck, letting out a soft, happy hum that immediately regulated my own nervous system.

I set her down, keeping one arm wrapped securely around her shoulders, and turned to catch Cassandra in a tight, one-armed embrace as she wheeled up to us.

"It is so good to see you, David," Cassandra smiled, squeezing my shoulder before adjusting her glasses. She looked past me, offering a polite, practiced smile. "Hi, Kristine. Thank you guys for picking us up."

"Of course! Welcome to Virginia," Kristine beamed. Her voice was bright, sugary, and perfectly pitched for a welcoming host.

But as I pulled my arm back from Casey, I felt Kristine step immediately into my space. She didn't say anything hostile. She just slid her hand smoothly down my arm, lacing her fingers tightly through mine, and pressed her side flush against my ribs. To anyone else, it looked like the affectionate, natural posture of a loving girlfriend. But the grip was just a fraction too tight, a silent, physical reassertion of territory right in front of my sisters.

My brain didn't log it as a threat. I just assumed she was a little nervous meeting my family, so I squeezed her hand back to reassure her.

We gathered their luggage and loaded up the trunk. The drive to Virginia Beach was filled with easy, rapid-fire catching up between me, Cassandra, and Casey. Kristine chimed in politely from the passenger seat, asking standard, friendly questions about the flight and the Seattle weather.

When we arrived at my mother’s house, the environment grew a little more crowded. Wendy and Peter welcomed everyone warmly. We sat in their living room, eating snacks and talking about the upcoming Thanksgiving plans.

Throughout the visit, I sat on the sofa with Casey tucked right against my side, our shoulders resting comfortably against each other as we listened to Mom talk. It was our standard, lifelong baseline for physical proximity.

Out of the corner of my eye, I caught Kristine watching us from an armchair across the room. Her smile was still perfectly fixed in place, but her eyes were tracking the way Casey leaned against me. She took a slow sip of her drink, completely masking the tension brewing behind her eyes. She didn't say a word to interrupt the sibling cuddles, playing the role of the supportive partner flawlessly, but a vague, unplaceable need to manage her mood began to quietly hum in the back of my mind. I quickly convinced myself it was just standard adult relationship maintenance.

Since we needed to return Wendy's car to her, my mother grabbed her keys once the visit wrapped up. She drove the four of us out of Virginia Beach, crossing the water and dropping us off directly at the curb of Kristine’s apartment building in Portsmouth before waving goodbye and driving off into the night.
