# Lore: The Sanctuary Wet Room (Bathing Logistics)

**Tags:** `#the_estate` `#accessibility` `#sanctuary_room` `#the_triplets` `#worldbuilding`

## Overview

When the family officially moved from the Big Island cabin into their completed Palo Alto residential estate in late 2009, they brought their deeply ingrained communal habits with them. Because the triplets essentially function as a single person and despise being separated, the concept of privacy among the four of them is entirely non-existent. The architects of Sanctuary 2.0—the massive second-floor master suite—were instructed to design the en-suite bathroom to accommodate all four adults simultaneously.

## The Wet Room Architecture

To eliminate the physical barriers and dangers of a traditional bathroom, the attached master bath was engineered as a massive, fully waterproofed "wet room."

- **The Seamless Floor:** There are no curbs, glass doors, or step-ups. The entire floor is a seamless expanse of radiant-heated, non-slip stone that gently slopes toward high-capacity commercial trench drains.
    
- **The Waterproofing:** Water can spray or spill anywhere in the room without causing structural damage, allowing wheelchairs to roll freely in and out of the bathing zones without navigating tight thresholds.
    

## The Four-Person Fixtures

The room features two primary, custom-built bathing installations, both scaled massively to accommodate Valerie, Lyra, Orion, and Celeste at the exact same time.

- **The Custom Shower:** A sprawling, open-concept roll-in shower area features multiple independent thermostatic showerheads and handheld wands at dual heights. It is wide enough that Lyra and Orion's waterproof transit chairs can be parked directly under the water simultaneously, with plenty of room left over for Valerie and Celeste.
    
- **The Walk-In Tub:** Adjacent to the shower is an enormous, custom-built walk-in hydrotherapy tub. It features a watertight, extra-wide mechanical door for easy lateral transfers and is physically large enough for all four of them to sit comfortably submerged in the warm water together.
    

## Valerie's Pragmatic Routine

In the Big Island cabin, bathing the triplets was a grueling, sequential chore. In the Palo Alto estate, Valerie leaned entirely into her signature pragmatism.

- Because the triplets find absolute safety in their shared physical presence, keeping them together during the bathing routine keeps the sensory environment completely calm.
    
- Valerie quickly realized that since she was already in the wet room getting soaked while assisting them, and since they all share the giant custom Alaska King bed anyway, there was zero logical reason to take a separate shower.
    
- The daily bathing routine simply became a synchronized, four-person event. They shower or soak in the tub together, turning what used to be an exhausting medical necessity into an efficient, deeply bonded extension of their daily family life.
