# Lore: The Cassini Center for Space Exploration

## Overview

What began as a desire to share their pristine dark skies evolved into **The Cassini Center for Space Exploration**. Situated on a dedicated public entrance branching off Cowpasture Road N, the center is a massive, fully operational public museum and research hub. It physically separates the thousands of annual tourists, students, and researchers from the family’s heavily guarded private residential estate.

The complex is divided into three primary facilities: the world-class observatory, the 8K planetarium dome, and the sprawling Cassini educational museum.

## The Museum & Exhibits

The Cassini educational wing is a massive, multi-level museum that masterfully blends hard astrophysics with immersive science fiction.

- **The Deep Space Galleries:** The core of the museum features interactive, high-budget exhibits detailing actual astronomical science. Guests can study the life cycles of stars, interact with gravitational wave simulators, and view hyper-detailed, physical scale models of real-world rovers and probes.
    
- **The _Cassini_ Experience:** Integrated seamlessly alongside the real-world science is the physical realization of the triplets' sci-fi universe. Built out in stunning **Dark Aero** and **Frutiger Aero** aesthetics—featuring curved glass, glossy surfaces, and cyan lighting—guests walk through a recreation of the luxury deep-space cruise liner, _Cassini_. Interactive terminals allow students to navigate fictional star systems while learning the real-world physics of theoretical artificial gravity and faster-than-light travel.
    
- **The VIP "Bridge":** Suspended above the busiest exhibit halls is "The Bridge"—a restricted, heavily soundproofed observation deck with one-way tinted glass. From this climate-controlled, sensory-safe vantage point, Lyra, Orion, and Celeste can watch the daily chaos of school field trips interacting with their creations without ever being physically overwhelmed.
    

## Operations & Memberships

The Cassini Center operates as a premier, public-facing institution. While the family's Texas Tea easily funds the center, it is run as a self-sustaining entity to ensure its longevity and to fund regional STEM grants.

- **Admission:** The museum, planetarium shows, and specific public observatory nights require purchased tickets.
    
- **Memberships:** Locals and frequent visitors can purchase annual memberships, granting them priority access to highly coveted stargazing events and discounted planetarium tickets.
    
- **The ASTI Affiliation:** The Cassini Center is a proud, accredited member of the **Alliance of Science and Technology Institutions (ASTI)**. Because of this, they participate in the **ASTI Discovery Passport** program. This reciprocal agreement allows families holding memberships at other participating science museums across the country to gain free or heavily discounted general admission to the Cassini Center, driving massive tourism to Highland County.
    

## The Observatory (Research & Public Access)

Sitting at the apex of the Starlight Wing, the observatory serves a dual purpose.

- **The Time-Share Leases:** NASA, the Department of Defense, and elite research universities (such as MIT) hold lucrative time-share leases, utilizing the multi-meter Ritchey-Chrétien telescope during strictly scheduled research blocks.
    
- **The Founder's Override:** The family retains the ultimate trump card. A contractual override allows Orion to pause any government or university feed if he specifically requests to view a celestial event.
    
- **Public Nights:** On specific weekends, the research feeds are disconnected, and the primary telescope (along with a fleet of smaller, highly advanced auxiliary scopes on the observation deck) is opened directly to the public and school groups for guided stargazing.
