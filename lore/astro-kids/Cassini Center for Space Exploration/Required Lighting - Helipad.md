# Lore: The Helipad & The Dark-Sky Exceptions

**Tags:** `#infrastructure` `#dark_skies` `#medical` `#the_estate` `#aviation`

## Overview

Aside from the mandatory, shielded red FAA beacon on the peak of the Starlight Wing observatory, the Cassini Center and the private residential compound produce zero upward light pollution. However, the remote geography of Highland County created a life-or-death logistical problem for the family: if Orion or the sisters suffered a critical medical emergency, a ground ambulance ride down winding mountain roads to the nearest major hospital in Roanoke would take far too long.

To solve this, Valerie had a commercial-grade emergency helipad constructed on the valley floor of the private estate.

## The Aeronautical Lighting Requirements

To legally permit a helipad for night-time MedEvac operations, the FAA and aeronautical safety boards require specific lighting that cannot be shielded from the sky.

- **The Heliport Beacon:** A rotating beacon that flashes alternating white and green lights, visible for miles from the air.
    
- **Perimeter Lights:** An array of bright green omnidirectional lights outlining the touchdown and lift-off area (TLOF).
    
- **The Wind Cone:** A brightly illuminated aviation windsock, requiring upward or lateral spotlights so incoming pilots can gauge wind direction and speed.
    

## The Pilot-Controlled Lighting (PCL) Solution

Having bright, flashing white and green lights on the valley floor would completely ruin the observatory's deep-space imaging and disrupt the dark-sky aesthetic of the entire compound.

To maintain the darkness, the family’s engineers implemented a **Pilot-Controlled Lighting (PCL)** system—a standard technology used at remote, un-towered airstrips.

- **The Default State:** 99.9% of the year, the helipad is completely dead and perfectly dark. The perimeter lights, the wind cone, and the beacon are powered off, preserving the absolute blackness of the valley.
    
- **The Emergency Activation:** If a MedEvac helicopter from Roanoke or Charlottesville is dispatched to the estate, the pilot tunes their onboard VHF radio to the estate's private aeronautical frequency. By clicking their radio transmission button a specific number of times (usually three, five, or seven clicks within five seconds), the pilot remotely triggers the helipad's system.
    
- **The Arrival:** Instantly, the valley floor lights up with the required green perimeter outline, the illuminated windsock, and the rotating beacon, guiding the flight crew safely in. The moment the helicopter departs and clears the airspace, the system runs on a timer and automatically shuts down, plunging the estate back into pristine, starlit darkness so the observatory can resume its work.
