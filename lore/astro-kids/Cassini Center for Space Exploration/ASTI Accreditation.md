# Lore: ASTI Accreditation & The Discovery Passport

## Overview

The **Alliance of Science and Technology Institutions (ASTI)** is the premier global consortium for science museums, planetariums, and research centers. For the Cassini Center to participate in the highly coveted **ASTI Discovery Passport** program—which drives massive interstate tourism by offering reciprocal admission—they had to pass a rigorous, multi-step accreditation process.

ASTI does not grant membership to just any roadside attraction. To maintain the prestige of the network, an institution must prove it is a dedicated, accessible, and scientifically rigorous facility.

## Core Accreditation Requirements

To join ASTI and enroll in the Passport program, a facility must legally and operationally meet the following strict criteria:

- **The Scientific Mandate:** The institution's primary mission must be dedicated to science, technology, engineering, or mathematics (STEM). A general history or art museum that happens to have a single science exhibit does not qualify. The Cassini Center easily surpassed this with its uncompromising 100% space exploration focus.
    
- **Interactive Education:** ASTI requires that the facility be heavily interactive. It cannot simply be a static gallery of artifacts behind glass. There must be dedicated educational outreach programs, hands-on learning kiosks, and staff educators (which the Cassini Center fulfills via its massive school field trip programs and interactive starliner kiosks).
    
- **Public Operation:** The facility must be open to the general public on a regular, predictable schedule for a minimum number of days per year. Private corporate showrooms or university labs that do not allow public admission are excluded.
    
- **Non-Profit or Educational Status:** The institution must operate as a non-profit (501(c)(3)) or be directly tied to a recognized educational/governmental entity. While the Starling family is astronomically wealthy, the Cassini Center itself is legally structured as a self-sustaining non-profit educational foundation.
    

## The Discovery Passport Program Rules

Once an institution is accredited by ASTI, it can opt into the **Discovery Passport** program. This reciprocal agreement is a massive draw for families, but it operates under a strict set of administrative rules to protect local museum revenues.

### The 90-Mile Exclusion Zone

This is the most critical rule of the Passport program. The reciprocal free or discounted admission _only_ applies to visitors who travel from outside the facility's local radius.

- **How it Works:** If a family holds an ASTI membership at a science museum in Richmond, VA, they can use it to get free general admission to the Cassini Center in Highland County, because it is more than 90 miles away.
    
- **The Revenue Protection:** However, a family living in Highland County cannot buy a cheap membership to a small ASTI museum three states away and use it to get free access to the Cassini Center every weekend. If the visitor's home address or their home museum is within a 90-mile radius of the Cassini Center, the Passport benefits are void.
    

### General Admission Only

The ASTI Passport strictly covers _general admission_. It explicitly does not force the host museum to give away its premium experiences for free.

- At the Cassini Center, an out-of-state ASTI Passport holder gets free entry into the main deep-space galleries and the _Cassini_ starliner walkthrough.
    
- However, they must still pay full price for the 8K planetarium shows, specialized guided tours, or ticketed access to the Starlight Wing's public observatory nights.
    

## The Starling Amendment (Universal Design)

When the Starlings brought the Cassini Center into the ASTI network in 2012, they used their immense financial leverage to lobby the governing board for a new requirement. Starting in 2014, ASTI adopted what the industry unofficially calls "The Starling Amendment."

To maintain ASTI accreditation, a facility must now undergo a strict, third-party accessibility audit. They must prove that their core interactive exhibits, theaters, and primary galleries meet elevated universal design standards, ensuring that science education is physically and sensorily accessible to all patrons, regardless of mobility or neurodivergence.
