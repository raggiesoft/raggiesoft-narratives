# Lore: The Academic Schism (Public School Dynamics)

**Tags:** `#the_triplets` `#academics` `#accessibility` `#timeline_2001_2003` `#worldbuilding`

## The Separation Mandate

For Lyra, Orion, and Celeste, existence has always been a collective experience. They operate as a single, indivisible system, relying on each other for physical support, emotional regulation, and academic problem-solving.

This inherent bond is exactly why they despised their time in the public school system. Like most public districts, Bedford County High School enforced a strict administrative policy of placing siblings—especially multiples—into separate classrooms. The district's IEP team believed this forced separation would foster individual independence. For the triplets, it felt like having their primary sensory and emotional anchors ripped away for six hours a day.

They hated the isolation, enduring the bulk of their core classes entirely alone, surviving only on the promise of the few periods a day where the school's scheduling limitations accidentally brought them back together.

## The Shared Sanctuaries

Despite the district's best efforts to keep them separated, logistical realities forced the administration to reluctantly group them together for three specific periods.

### 1. The Lunch Period

This was their baseline daily reunion. Because the school only ran a limited number of lunch blocks, the triplets were finally able to reconvene in the cafeteria. They used this time to decompress, share notes, and emotionally regulate each other before being forced back into separate afternoon classes.

### 2. The Latin Exception

The administration attempted to place them in different foreign language classes, but the triplets had all specifically chosen Latin.

- **The Logistical Bottleneck:** Bedford County only employed a single Latin teacher (_Magistra_). The school had no choice but to schedule all three of them into the exact same period.
    
- **The Academic Shift:** _Magistra_ was one of the few educators who truly understood their dynamic. She quickly noticed that when they were placed at a table together, their academic output skyrocketed. Separated, they were anxious and withdrawn; together, they thrived, naturally dividing the translation work and collaborating seamlessly.
    

### 3. The Percussion Section (Band)

Music was the one arena where their synchronization was actively rewarded rather than discouraged. All three triplets were percussionists, and they participated in the school band program for a total of seven years (three years in middle school and all four years of high school).

- **The Ensemble:** Because high school band periods are strictly segregated by instrument sections, the administration was forced to keep them together.
    
- **The Synergy:** The band director quickly realized that Lyra, Orion, and Celeste functioned flawlessly as a single rhythmic unit. Orion provided the heavy, resonant bass tempo, while Lyra and Celeste handled the complex, melodic auxiliary rhythms. They didn't even need to look at each other to stay on the exact same beat.
    
- **The Marching Band Accommodation:** To meet their accessibility needs during the fall marching season, the director placed all three of them permanently in the "Pit" (the stationary Front Ensemble positioned on the sideline). This allowed them to remain seated in their wheelchairs, playing the marimbas, timpani, and auxiliary percussion without having to navigate the physical impossibility of marching in formation on a grassy football field.
