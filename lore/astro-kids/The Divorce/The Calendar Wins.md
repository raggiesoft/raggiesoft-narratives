# Lore: The Calendar Victory (The Moot Court)

**Tags:** `#the_triplets` `#divorce` `#virginia_beach` `#timeline_2003` `#worldbuilding`

## The Blind War

The Virginia Beach divorce proceedings had devolved into a masterclass in mutually assured destruction. For nearly two years, the parents' attorneys billed thousands of hours, filing endless motions, objections, and continuances. They weaponized the custody of the triplets simply to deny the other parent a win.

Because the parents viewed the children purely as assets to be divided—and because they hadn't actually seen Lyra, Orion, and Celeste since handing them over to Valerie in 2001—everyone involved in the litigation developed severe tunnel vision. The attorneys were so consumed by the legal mechanics of the fight that they forgot the most fundamental piece of information buried in the first page of the case file: time was still passing.

## The Exhausted Judge

In the autumn of 2003, yet another custody hearing was convened in the Virginia Beach family court.

The father's attorney stood up, confidently requesting sole custody. The mother's attorney immediately rose to object, demanding the mother be recognized as the appropriate custodial parent.

The presiding judge, who had been listening to variations of this exact argument for over two years, opened the massive, bloated case file. He stared at the paperwork. He paused. He turned to the first page. He looked down at the birth dates, looked over his glasses at the two highly paid lawyers, and did the math.

The courtroom went dead silent when the judge finally interrupted the bickering attorneys to ask a very simple question:

_"Counselors, how old are these individuals today?"_

## The Evaporation

The lawyers froze. The horrifying realization washed over the courtroom as they desperately did the mental math. The triplets had turned 18 weeks prior. The calendar had defeated them.

The judge wasn't angry; he was profoundly, existentially tired. He was staring at a legal question that no longer existed.

He closed the file and spoke into the record:

> _"The Court notes that the individuals in question have reached the age of majority. Accordingly, this Court lacks any statutory basis to determine the custody of adult persons. All pending custody motions are therefore dismissed as moot."_

The bitter, two-year war didn't end with a dramatic ruling or a triumphant parent. It evaporated into thin air because the judge realized he was being asked to decide where three legal adults had to live. And under the law, the answer was beautifully simple: wherever they wanted.

## The Final Printout

Hundreds of miles away in Bedford County, Lyra, Orion, and Celeste were not in a courtroom. They were sitting at the heavy wooden table in the cabin, doing their homework, completely unaware that years of litigation had just vanished.

Then, the landline rang. It wasn't a 757 area code calling directly. The Caller ID showed the local Virginia 711 Relay Service. Lyra toggled the printer _ON_, and the TTY machine began to spool out the final chapter of their parents' divorce.

```
VALERIE:
GOOD NEWS
GA
```

The triplets stopped what they were doing and watched the paper feed upward.

```
VALERIE:
JUDGE DISMISSED CUSTODY
YOU ARE 18
NOBODY GETS YOU
GA
```

And finally, the ultimate declaration of their freedom:

```
VALERIE:
COMING HOME
PIZZA TONIGHT
GA
SK
```
