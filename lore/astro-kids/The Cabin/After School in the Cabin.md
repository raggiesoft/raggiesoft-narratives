# Lore: The Latchkey Protocol (After-School Autonomy)

## The IEP Standoff

When the triplets enrolled in Bedford County public schools for their Junior year, transportation immediately became a logistical battlefield. The school district's transportation department initially refused to do a "curb drop-off" at an empty house for three disabled minors, citing district liability policies.

Valerie fought the IEP team and won. She argued that they were 16 years old and cognitively brilliant. More importantly, she demonstrated that Lyra, as an ambulatory wheelchair user, had the physical capability to unlock the cabin door and hold it open for Orion and Celeste. Valerie signed an ironclad liability waiver, legally releasing the county from any responsibility the moment the bus driver watched the three of them safely cross the threshold of the cabin.

## The Schedule Logistics

Because the Piedmont Central Branch operates from 10:00 AM to 6:00 PM, Valerie is effectively scheduled for a full-day block.

- **The Shift:** She arrives at 9:45 AM to unlock the doors and prep the tills, and finishes her closing duties to leave the building by 6:15 PM.
    
- **The Commute:** The drive from downtown Lynchburg back up into the Blue Ridge Mountains takes roughly 35 to 40 minutes in her beat-up van.
    
- **The Gap:** The accessible school bus drops the triplets off at the Big Island cabin at around 3:30 PM. Valerie does not pull into the dirt driveway until roughly 7:00 PM.
    

This creates a daily, three-and-a-half-hour window where Lyra, Orion, and Celeste are entirely alone.

## The Cabin Independence

Rather than a period of vulnerability, these afternoon hours become a crucial incubator for the triplets' independence and shared responsibility.

- **The Routine:** The bus driver lowers the hydraulic lift. Lyra walks up the wooden ramp, unlocks the deadbolt, and props the door. Orion and Celeste roll inside.
    
- **The Safety Net:** They have a strict protocol. They immediately move to the main living area where the landline is located. Orion’s steady, rhythmic humming serves as an acoustic anchor, keeping the environment calm and predictable while they wait for Valerie.
    
- **The Academic Protocol:** Because their IEPs placed them in different class schedules to force them to spread their wings, their only shared period during the school day is lunch. They hate being separated, but they understand the district's goal of fostering individual independence. To make up for it, they insist on doing all of their homework together at the cabin table.
    
- **The Honor Code:** They are fiercely protective of their academic honesty. While they rely on in-school tutoring during their respective study periods, the bulk of their problem-solving happens together in the afternoons. They never copy each other's work, but if one of them is struggling with a concept, the other two will actively guide them toward the answer. If a problem completely stumps all three of them, it gets tabled until 7:00 PM so they can ask Valerie for help.
    
- **The Analog Worldbuilding:** Once the homework is finished, with the smell of the mountain woods drifting through the screened windows, the triplets dive into their hyper-fixations. Unable to speak or use computers yet, they spend the remainder of the afternoon furiously scribbling on legal pads—Lyra charting stars, Orion sketching ship drives, and Celeste mapping acoustics.
    

## The Saturday Sanctuary

Because the triplets are safe as latchkey kids during the week, the downtown library is no longer a desperate childcare workaround. Instead, it becomes a dedicated weekend excursion.

When Valerie works her Saturday shift (9:00 AM to 5:00 PM), she loads the teenagers into the van and brings them with her. The heavy wooden table near the non-fiction section becomes their weekend base of operations, allowing the reference librarians to feed their growing intellects with towering stacks of discarded encyclopedias and structural engineering textbooks.
