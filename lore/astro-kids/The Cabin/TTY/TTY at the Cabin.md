## The Reality of Residential TTY (2001)

In the early 2000s—before the widespread adoption of text messaging, smartphones, or video relay services—the **TTY (Teletypewriter)**, also known as a TDD (Telecommunications Device for the Deaf), was the absolute gold standard for accessibility.

While you wouldn't find one in a typical household, they were universally present in homes where a resident was deaf, hard of hearing, or speech-impaired.

- **The Procurement:** Valerie did not have to pay out of pocket for the expensive machine. Because the triplets had documented disabilities, she applied through the Virginia Department for the Deaf and Hard of Hearing (VDDHH) Technology Assistance Program, which provided a bulky, heavy-duty TTY machine to the cabin entirely for free.
    

## The Hardware Setup (The Shared Line)

A TTY does not require a dedicated, separate phone line. It operates over standard analog copper phone lines (POTS) by converting typed letters into audio tones.

- **The Splitter:** Valerie simply plugged a cheap, plastic RJ11 splitter into the cabin's main telephone wall jack in the living area.
    
- **The Dual System:** She plugged the TTY into one side of the splitter, and a standard, cheap cordless telephone into the other.
    
- **Seamless Operation:** When the TTY was turned off, the phone line functioned completely normally. Valerie could pick up her cordless handset to call the power company or talk to her library supervisor without any interference. The TTY only seized the line when the triplets physically turned it on to dial out.
    

## The 711 Protocol: Hearing Carry Over (HCO)

In 2001, the FCC mandated the nationwide rollout of **711**, the universal three-digit dialing code to reach the Telecommunications Relay Service (TRS). Because Lyra, Orion, and Celeste are non-verbal but have perfect hearing, they used a highly specific relay mode called **Hearing Carry Over (HCO)**.

If there was an emergency at the cabin, or if they simply needed to ask Valerie a question while she was working at the Piedmont Central Branch, the protocol worked like this:

1. **The Connection:** Lyra would power on the TTY, place the telephone handset onto the machine's acoustic couplers, and dial 711.
    
2. **The Operator:** A state relay operator would answer. Lyra would type "HCO PLS" (Hearing Carry Over Please) and give the operator the number for the library's circulation desk.
    
3. **The Call:** The operator dialed the library. When Valerie answered, she would hear the operator announce: _"This is the Virginia Relay Center. I have an HCO call. Go ahead."_
    
4. **The Conversation:**
    
    - Lyra typed her message on the TTY keyboard.
        
    - The relay operator read Lyra's typed words out loud to Valerie.
        
    - Because it was an HCO call, the operator did not type Valerie's response. Instead, Valerie spoke normally into the phone, and Lyra simply listened to Valerie's actual voice through the TTY's connected handset.
        

## The Physical Toll

While the TTY was an incredible safety net, using it was not effortless.

- **The Keyboard:** TTY keyboards were highly mechanical. Because of their cerebral palsy, striking the keys required deliberate, concentrated physical effort from Lyra or Celeste (Orion's motor control made typing on the small keys nearly impossible).
    
- **The Shorthand:** To minimize the physical exhaustion of typing, the triplets learned standard TTY shorthand (using "GA" for Go Ahead, "SK" for Stop Keying/Goodbye, and skipping unnecessary punctuation).
    

Despite the slow, clunky technology, the TTY machine sitting on the cabin table guaranteed that during those three-and-a-half hours they were home alone, they were never truly cut off from Valerie.

### Lore: The Relay Transcripts (HCO in Action)

#### Overview

The Hearing Carry Over (HCO) protocol is the primary communication bridge between the Big Island cabin and the Piedmont Central Branch. Because the Virginia Relay Center operators (referred to as Communications Assistants, or CAs) strictly act as transparent conduits, they do not know the contents of a call or who it is for until the TTY user types it.

Here are the operational transcripts of how these calls actually functioned in the early 2000s, utilizing standard TTY shorthand like **GA** ("Go Ahead") and **SK** ("Stop Keying").

#### Call 1: The Triplets Call the Library

When Lyra dials 711 on the cabin's TTY machine to reach the library, she connects to a Relay Operator. She types "HCO PLS," provides the library's local number, and waits for the connection.

Because Valerie is often busy at the circulation desk, one of her coworkers, Marcus, answers the main line. Marcus is already familiar with the Relay protocol from previous calls.

- **Marcus (Library):** "Seven Hills Public Library, Piedmont Central Branch. This is Marcus."
    
- **Relay Operator (Voice):** "Hello, a person is calling through the relay service. This is operator 8492. The caller will type their conversation, and I will read it to you. Please speak directly to the caller and say 'Go Ahead' when you are finished. One moment for your call to begin."
    
- _(Marcus waits patiently, as the operator does not know who the caller wants until Lyra types it)._
    
- **Lyra (Typing / Operator Reading out loud):** "HI MARCUS THIS IS LYRA IS VALERIE THERE Q GA"
    
- **Marcus (Speaking directly to Lyra):** "Hey Lyra, hold on one second, let me go grab her. Go ahead."
    
- _(Marcus flags Valerie down at the desk and hands her the cordless phone)._
    
- **Valerie (Speaking directly to Lyra):** "Hey Lyra, I'm here. Go ahead."
    
- **Lyra (Typing / Operator Reading):** "PHARMACY CALLED MEDS READY CAN YOU PICK UP ON WAY HOME Q GA"
    
- **Valerie (Speaking):** "Yes, I'll swing by and grab them on the way. I should be home at the normal time. Go ahead."
    
- **Lyra (Typing / Operator Reading):** "OKAY THANKS SEE YOU LATER SK SK"
    
- **Valerie (Speaking):** "See you guys tonight. Bye. SK SK." _(Valerie hangs up)._

#### Call 3: Valerie Calls the Cabin

If Valerie needs to initiate a call to the Big Island cabin, she simply dials 711 from her desk at the library. She informs the answering Relay Operator that she is calling a TTY user who utilizes Hearing Carry Over.

The operator dials the cabin. Because Lyra, Orion, and Celeste have perfect hearing, they can hear the standard ring of the telephone in the living room. Celeste powers on the TTY, places the handset into the acoustic cups, and begins typing.

- **Celeste (Typing / Operator Reading out loud to Valerie):** "HELLO THIS IS THE CABIN GA"
    
- **Valerie (Speaking directly to Celeste):** "Hey Celeste, it's Valerie. I just wanted to let you guys know that closing procedures are taking a bit longer tonight. I am going to be about thirty minutes late coming home. Go ahead."
    
- **Celeste (Hearing Valerie's real voice / Typing back / Operator Reading):** "OKAY WE WILL START THE HOMEWORK AND WAIT FOR YOU GA"
    
- **Valerie (Speaking):** "Sounds good. Don't tackle the math without me if you get stuck. See you soon. SK SK."
    
- **Celeste (Typing / Operator Reading):** "WE WONT SEE YOU SOON SK SK" _(Celeste turns off the TTY)._
    

**The Emotional Impact:** By utilizing the HCO protocol, the triplets never have to listen to the robotic or unfamiliar voice of a Relay Operator reading Valerie's words. They get to hear Valerie's actual, comforting voice over the phone line, while the operator only reads the triplets' typed responses to Valerie.
