# Lore: The Estranged Branches (Handling Legitimate Rivals)

**Tags:** `#worldbuilding` `#legal_strategy` `#texas_tea` `#valerie` `#timeline_2004`

## Overview

While the Dallas law firm easily swatted away the outright fraudsters and grifters, a secondary threat emerged during the probate process: actual, legitimate blood relatives. The discovery of the $2.4 billion SovTex Energy escrow alerted estranged second and third cousins scattered across the country. Because the great-grandfather had died intestate (without a will), several of these distant relatives technically possessed a valid, fractional legal claim to the estate under Texas intestacy laws.

## Valerie's Verdict

When the Dallas senior partners presented these legitimate claims to Valerie, she did not hesitate.

During the darkest years at the Big Island cabin, Valerie was an 18-to-20-year-old girl working an entry-level municipal library job for **$8.50 an hour**, taking home **$483 bi-weekly**, drowning in Medicaid paperwork, and carrying the entire physical and financial weight of three severely disabled teenagers on her back. The extended family—most of whom lived comfortable, middle-class lives—knew exactly what she, Lyra, Orion, and Celeste were going through. They never called to check in, never sent a care package, and never offered so much as $50 to help keep the mountain cabin heated through the winter.

Furthermore, none of these relatives were even actively looking out for the family line; they only discovered the estate when specialized "Heir Hunter" probate firms cold-called their landlines to tip them off for a cut of the payout.

Now that billions of dollars were on the line, these same people suddenly hired cheap local attorneys to demand their "rightful" percentage. Valerie gave the Dallas firm a cold, absolute mandate: **Not one single dime.**

## The Strategy of Legal Attrition

Even if a claimant has a legitimate blood tie, successfully fighting a contested probate battle in Texas requires immense capital. This is where Valerie's decision to hire apex corporate predators paid off.

- **The Resource Disparity:** The estranged relatives were middle-class individuals paying out-of-pocket or on small retainers for low-tier local lawyers. The Dallas firm, defending a $48 million contingency fee, possessed practically infinite resources and an army of junior associates.
    
- **The Bureaucratic Siege:** The Dallas lawyers refused to negotiate or entertain settlement offers. Instead, they buried the relatives' attorneys in an avalanche of procedural warfare. They filed endless motions for discovery, demanded pristine, decades-old certified genealogical proof that was exorbitantly expensive to pull, and scheduled depositions in Dallas that required the claimants to pay for their own travel.
    
- **The Starve-Out:** The strategy was simple financial attrition. The Dallas firm intentionally drove up the cost of litigation until the relatives' small-town attorneys were forced to demand massive retainer top-ups that the relatives simply could not afford.
    

## The Aftermath

Unable to match the overwhelming legal firepower and financial endurance of the Dallas firm, the legitimate relatives were effectively starved out of the Texas court system. Their legal funds dried up, their attorneys withdrew from the cases, and their claims were dismissed due to procedural exhaustion.

The Texas probate judge ultimately ruled exclusively in favor of Valerie, Lyra, Orion, and Celeste, ensuring that the family members who abandoned the triplets in poverty were permanently and legally locked out of the empire.
