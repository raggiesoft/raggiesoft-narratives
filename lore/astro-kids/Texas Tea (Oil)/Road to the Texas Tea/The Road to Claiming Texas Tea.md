# Lore: The Cold Call & The Verification (Securing the Claim)

**Tags:** `#timeline_2004` `#dallas_trip` `#legal_strategy` `#texas_tea` `#worldbuilding`

## Overview

The transition from impoverished caregivers to multi-billionaires did not happen overnight. After discovering the $2,418,903,114.67 escrow balance on MissingMoney.com, Valerie knew she needed elite legal representation. The initial friction of claiming the fortune came from simply convincing a room full of high-powered Dallas attorneys that a 22-year-old living in a rural Virginia cabin wasn't completely delusional.

## Week 1: The Prank Call

Armed only with printouts from the Glasgow public library, Valerie used the Big Island cabin's landline to cold-call one of the most ruthless, prestigious Estate and Probate corporate law firms in Dallas, Texas.

- **The Reception:** When Valerie stated she was calling regarding a multi-billion-dollar unclaimed property claim, the receptionist almost hung up. She was eventually transferred to a junior partner who handled intake and contingency vetting.
    
- **The Disbelief:** To the Dallas lawyer, the story sounded like an elaborate prank or a scam. A girl from an Appalachian backwater claiming she was the sole heir to a $2.4 billion SovTex Energy escrow—left behind by a great-grandfather she had never met—sounded completely absurd. The lawyer was polite, but highly dismissive, assuming she was a crank caller.
    
- **The Hook:** Valerie didn't get emotional or defensive. She simply read the lawyer the exact SovTex Energy property ID from the website, the great-grandfather's full legal name, and the dates of death.
    
- **The Realization:** To humor her and end the call, the lawyer typed the property ID into the Texas Comptroller’s portal while they were on the phone. When the database loaded and the lawyer saw the nine-figure balance sitting in state escrow, the tone of the conversation shifted instantly. The firm realized that even if it was a long shot, a 2% contingency fee ($48 million) was worth looking into.
    

## Weeks 2–3: The Ground Game Verification

Despite seeing the number in the database, the law firm did not immediately take Valerie's word that she was the rightful heir. They needed absolute proof before they fronted any legal costs or chartered planes.

- **The Investigators:** The Dallas firm immediately dispatched a paralegal and a private investigator to Virginia. They didn't just meet with Valerie; they went straight to the county clerk's offices in Virginia Beach and Bedford County.
    
- **The Paper Trail:** The investigators pulled the parents' death certificates, the triplets' birth certificates, and dug into the grandfather's estate papers. They traced the genealogical line backward, verifying the unbroken chain from the Virginia Starlings directly to the Texas great-grandfather who died intestate.
    
- **The Green Light:** By the end of Week 3, the investigators called the senior partners in Dallas. The claim was entirely legitimate. Valerie and the triplets were the sole surviving direct descendants. The $2.4 billion was legally theirs for the taking.
    

## Week 4: Securing the Assets (The Flight)

The moment the law firm verified the claim, their mindset shifted from skepticism to outright panic. They realized Valerie was holding a winning multi-billion-dollar lottery ticket, and if she called a rival law firm, they would lose out on the $48 million payday.

- **The Pivot:** The senior partners took over the account. They called Valerie back at the cabin, treating her with the utmost deference and respect.
    
- **The Invitation:** The firm insisted on formalizing the contingency contract and initiating the "Application to Determine Heirship" immediately. Because the state of Texas requires heirs to physically appear before a probate judge to establish identity, the firm offered to handle all logistics.
    
- **The Charter:** Knowing the complexities of transporting three wheelchair users, the firm bypassed commercial airlines completely. They paid out of pocket to charter the private, accessible jet out of Lynchburg Regional Airport (LYH) directly into Addison Airport (ADS), eager to get the Starlings into their boardroom to sign the paperwork before anyone else could.
