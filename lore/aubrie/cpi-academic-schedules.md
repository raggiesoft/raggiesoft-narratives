## 1. Foreign Language Backstory & The Sophomore Japanese Arc (`JAPN 101` / `JAPN 102`)

### A. High School Foreign Language Backgrounds (Pre-CPI)
1. **Jordan (`Aubrey Jordan Fuller` $\rightarrow$ `Jordan Shawn Kelly`) — Zero Prior Foreign Language:**
   - **Sophomore Year (`Fall 2015`) is Jordan's first time EVER taking any foreign language class.**
   - In Virginia Beach public schools, Aubrie Sinclair used Virginia Department of Education (`VDOE`) communicative/speech disability waiver provisions to exempt him from high school foreign language requirements entirely (*"Why on earth would a boy who can't even speak English take Spanish?"*).
   - When she sabotaged his Freshman Fall (`2014`) CPI course registration expecting him to flunk out by Thanksgiving, she bypassed CPI's university foreign language requirement altogether—leaving it unfulfilled on his College of Science degree audit until Sophomore Year (`2015–2016`).
2. **Zoë Wren Brooks — Tested Out of High School Foreign Language (Fluent in Japanese, Non-Native Speaker):**
   - Having lived in **Osaka, Japan for four years** during childhood with her parents (**Daniel and Laura Brooks**) and older brother (**Caleb Brooks**), Zoë speaks, reads, and writes Japanese (`日本語` — both standard *Hyōjungo* and Osaka *Kansai-ben*) with effortless fluency, though as a **British-American citizen**, she is not a native heritage speaker.
   - When the Brooks family relocated from Osaka to Christiansburg, Virginia, Zoë took the official **VDOE World Language Credit-by-Exam proficiency assessment** in Japanese, scored at the maximum threshold, and **tested out of high school foreign language completely**.
3. **Lauren Nicole Barnes — Tested Out of High School Foreign Language (Fluent in Québécois French):**
   - Having spent significant time in Québec (`e003/c001/s010.md`), Lauren possesses rapid, native-level fluency in **Québécois French** and **tested out of standard high school French classes** by proficiency exam back home in Gander, Newfoundland.
4. **Aubrie Kelly, Madison Scott, Hannah Morales, Mark Cooper, Sarah Braithwaite, & Jessica Gallagher:**
   - All six completed traditional high school foreign language classes (standard high school Spanish or French) before arriving at CPI, and had never studied Japanese prior to Sophomore Year.

### B. The Inspiration on the *Ferry Hoshizora* (`Summer 2015 World Trek — Leg 4`) & Academic Honesty Rule
- **The Spark in Japan (`e003` — *Hoshizora no Uta* on the Seto Inland Sea):** During **Leg 4 of the Summer 2015 World Trek** (Tokyo, Osaka, and the Seto Inland Sea), Zoë acts as the primary translator for the 18-person expedition. Aboard the overnight **`Ferry Hoshizora`** sailing out of Osaka port, the ship's signature theme song, ***Hoshizora no Uta (Song of the Starry Sky / 星空のうた)***, plays over the cabin PA system—triggering Jordan's **two-stage meltdown of pure relief**:
  1. **First Meltdown (Zero-Threat Sensory Shock):** Even without understanding a single word of Japanese, the warm, breezy melody makes Jordan feel—for the first time in his life—that the world is asking him to feel comfort and safety on the Seto Inland Sea, dropping his threat level to zero and snapping 18 years of hyper-vigilance as Aubrie and Zoë hold him in deep bilateral compression.
  2. **Second Explosive Meltdown (When Zoë Translates the Lyrics):** When Jordan types (<aac>I do not know what they are singing. But it feels incredibly kind.</aac>) and Zoë translates the chorus he just heard (*"Leave your heavy shadows on the shore. The gentle waves will carry you to tomorrow. Rest your eyes under the starry sky, for you are safe upon the sea"*), the cognitive realization that the song is a literal promise of safety shatters his emotional dam into a second, even more massive meltdown of pure relief.
- **Zoë's Offer:** Seeing how deeply the language and *Hoshizora no Uta* touched Jordan—and knowing that Jordan still needs his two-semester CPI Foreign Language sequence to graduate (having never taken a foreign language in K–12) while the rest of the group has Humanities / Global Study or Free Elective credits to fill—Zoë gets the inspiration right there in Japan: **she offers to teach them the language and serve as their personal tutor when they take Japanese at CPI in Sophomore Year (`2015–2016`)**, so that Jordan will never need a translator to read or understand *Hoshizora no Uta* again.
- **Why All 9 Enroll Together (`JAPN 101` & `JAPN 102`):**
  - Because every member of the circle earned **CPI Priority Course Registration** as Jordan's official Educational Accessibility peer note-takers in Freshman Year (`e001/c006/s007.md` & `c007/s002.md`), **all nine (`Jordan, Aubrie, Madison, Zoë, Lauren, Hannah, Mark, Sarah, and Jessica`)** lock in seats together in the exact same section of **`JAPN 101: Elementary Japanese I` (`Fall 2015`)** and **`JAPN 102: Elementary Japanese II` (`Spring 2016`)**.
  - **Why Zoë Enrolls Too:** Even though Zoë is already fluent in Japanese, CPI allows her to enroll in `JAPN 101–102` as a free elective and as **Jordan's official Educational Accessibility peer note-taker** because she is a British-American citizen who graduated from a U.S. high school (not a native Japanese national). She takes the class because she wants to be right beside her found family.
  - **Strict Academic Honesty Rule:** Outside the classroom, Zoë runs evening Japanese coaching and drill sessions in Bennett Hall / Geneva Street—teaching *Hiragana/Katakana/Kanji* stroke order, grammar logic, and pronunciation—while **strictly obeying the CPI Undergraduate Honor Code** (she coaches and tutors them rigorously, but never completes their homework, compositions, or exams for them).
  - **Jordan's Japanese AAC Stack:** Zoë configures a Japanese Input Method Editor (`IME`) directly inside Jordan's **`Aura Glade`** Linux environment and **`NeoEdit`** terminal so he can type phonetic *rōmaji* and convert it live into glowing neon-green *Hiragana*, *Katakana*, and *Kanji*, paired with a calibrated Japanese vocal module in **`EchoType`** (approved through **Ms. Higgins** at Educational Accessibility for oral classroom drills).
