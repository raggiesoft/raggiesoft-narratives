# Chunk 1: The Arrival and The Mask (Wednesday)

The drive down Interstate 64 to Norfolk International Airport was buzzing with anticipation. I had both hands gripping the steering wheel of my mother’s car, my leg bouncing lightly against the floorboard. It had been months since I had seen Jessica and Rachel, and my processor was eagerly counting down the minutes until their flight from Seattle landed.

Sitting in the passenger seat, Delores watched the highway markers pass by.

"Your mom was really nice to let us borrow the car for this," Delores noted, checking her reflection in the passenger-side sun visor mirror and smoothing her hair.

"Yeah, it was her idea," I smiled, navigating the exit toward the ORF arrivals terminal. "She knew it would be easier for us to just grab them at the curb and drive straight back to her place in Virginia Beach so they can see her and Gregg."

Delores offered a polite nod, snapping the visor shut. "I just hope they aren't too exhausted from the flight. We have a busy schedule."

I parked the car in the short-term lot, and we walked into the terminal. The moment I spotted my sisters coming down the escalator toward baggage claim, the ambient noise of the airport completely vanished.

"Bug!" I called out.

Rachel dropped her carry-on bag instantly. She broke into a run, her face lighting up with absolute joy. I met her halfway, wrapping my arms around her and lifting her slightly off the floor in a massive, deep-pressure bear hug. She buried her face in my neck, letting out a soft, happy hum that immediately regulated my own nervous system.

I set her down, keeping one arm wrapped securely around her shoulders, and turned to catch Jessica in a tight, one-armed embrace as she wheeled up to us.

"It is so good to see you, Michael," Jessica smiled, squeezing my shoulder before adjusting her glasses. She looked past me, offering a polite, practiced smile. "Hi, Delores. Thank you guys for picking us up."

"Of course! Welcome to Virginia," Delores beamed. Her voice was bright, sugary, and perfectly pitched for a welcoming host.

But as I pulled my arm back from Rachel, I felt Delores step immediately into my space. She didn't say anything hostile. She just slid her hand smoothly down my arm, lacing her fingers tightly through mine, and pressed her side flush against my ribs. To anyone else, it looked like the affectionate, natural posture of a loving girlfriend. But the grip was just a fraction too tight, a silent, physical reassertion of territory right in front of my sisters.

My brain didn't log it as a threat. I just assumed she was a little nervous meeting my family, so I squeezed her hand back to reassure her.

We gathered their luggage and loaded up the trunk. The drive to Virginia Beach was filled with easy, rapid-fire catching up between me, Jessica, and Rachel. Delores chimed in politely from the passenger seat, asking standard, friendly questions about the flight and the Seattle weather.

When we arrived at my mother’s house, the environment grew a little more crowded. Ellen and Gregg welcomed everyone warmly. We sat in their living room, eating snacks and talking about the upcoming Thanksgiving plans.

Throughout the visit, I sat on the sofa with Rachel tucked right against my side, our shoulders resting comfortably against each other as we listened to Mom talk. It was our standard, lifelong baseline for physical proximity.

Out of the corner of my eye, I caught Delores watching us from an armchair across the room. Her smile was still perfectly fixed in place, but her eyes were tracking the way Rachel leaned against me. She took a slow sip of her drink, completely masking the tension brewing behind her eyes. She didn't say a word to interrupt the sibling cuddles, playing the role of the supportive partner flawlessly, but a vague, unplaceable need to manage her mood began to quietly hum in the back of my mind. I quickly convinced myself it was just standard adult relationship maintenance.

Since we needed to return Ellen's car to her, my mother grabbed her keys once the visit wrapped up. She drove the four of us out of Virginia Beach, crossing the water and dropping us off directly at the curb of Delores’s apartment building in Portsmouth before waving goodbye and driving off into the night.

# Chunk 2: Establishing the Perimeter (Wednesday Night)

We hauled the luggage up the stairs and into the three-bedroom apartment. Delores's mother, Robyn, and her boyfriend, Tom, were already gone for the holiday visiting his family, leaving the space entirely to us. The living room was modest, anchored by two sofas positioned perpendicular to each other with a small wooden end table wedged in the corner between them.

"We can easily grab a hotel room down the road for the three of us," Jessica offered casually, setting her heavy messenger bag on the floor. "That way we aren't crowding your space, Delores, and Michael can just ride back here with us in the morning."

"Oh, no, that’s absolutely not a problem at all!" Delores answered.

The response was instant. There wasn't a microsecond of polite consideration. I didn't think much of it—I just assumed she was being a highly accommodating host—but Jessica’s eyes tracked the sheer, unblinking speed of the rejection.

"If you're sure," Jessica replied smoothly. She reached into her canvas duffel bag and pulled out a tightly folded, heavy-duty air mattress, along with a stack of pillows and blankets. Because she was constantly deployed by Quantum to different corporate sites, she never left her physical sleeping arrangements to chance.

"Rachel and I can take the air mattress and the sofas," Jessica continued, pivoting her logistics flawlessly. "Michael can just grab a blanket and camp out in the living room with us. It'll be just like when we were kids."

My chest instantly lightened. The idea of spending the night camped out in the living room, surrounded by my pack, sounded perfect. Rachel didn't care that a sofa was meant for one person; she was fully prepared to figure it out, needing the deep pressure and proximity to regulate her own system after the long flight.

"Actually," Delores chimed in, her voice dropping into a soft, pouty register as she stepped into my space and wrapped her arms around my waist. "I was really hoping Michael would sleep in my room with me tonight. We've been running around all day, and I just want to spend some time with my boyfriend."

She looked up at me with a sweet, expectant smile. It was a flawless social trap. If I said no and chose my sisters, I would look like a neglectful, childish boyfriend rejecting his partner's affection.

I hesitated, my eyes darting to Jessica. I desperately wanted to stay in the living room.

Rachel opened her mouth, her protective instincts flaring as she prepared to argue for our sibling time. But before Rachel could utter a single syllable, Jessica caught her eye. Jessica gave a sharp, almost imperceptible downward motion with her hand—a silent, absolute command for Rachel to hold her tongue. Jessica’s analytical brain had just logged the territorial blockade, but she recognized that fighting it right now in Delores's apartment would only arouse suspicion and cause unnecessary friction.

"Have a good night, Michael," Jessica said politely, unpacking her blankets.

"Night, guys," I muttered, feeling a heavy, confusing knot form in my stomach as I grabbed my bag.

I followed Delores down the hall to her bedroom. The door clicked shut behind us, isolating me from the rest of my family. I was exhausted from the driving and the travel logistics, and my internal clock was begging for sleep. But sleep wasn't the immediate priority on Delores's agenda. I went along with it, forcing myself to be present and compliant, deeply convinced that this quiet, coercive transaction was simply the required maintenance of a normal adult relationship.

# Chunk 3: The Morning Anchor (Thursday Morning)

I woke up early on Thursday morning, long before the gray daylight fully filtered through the bedroom blinds. Beside me, Delores was still fast asleep, her breathing slow and heavy.

My internal processor was already humming with anticipation. I quietly slipped out from under the covers, gathered my clothes, and dressed as silently as possible in the dim light. I didn't want to wake her, but my body was craving the familiar, grounding presence of my pack.

I eased the bedroom door open, slipping down the short hallway into the living room.

Jessica and Rachel were just beginning to stir. Because Delores had swiftly shot down the air mattress idea the night before, my sisters had simply claimed the two perpendicular sofas. Jessica was sitting up on the other couch, adjusting her glasses, while Rachel was buried under a pile of blankets on the adjacent sofa.

I didn't say a word. I just got down on my knees next to Rachel's sofa, reached under the heavy blankets, and pulled a groggy Rachel into a tight, deep-pressure hug.

Rachel blinked her dark eyes open. The disorientation of waking up in a strange apartment vanished the absolute second she realized she was looking at my face. A massive, sleep-heavy smile broke across her lips, and she wrapped her arms fiercely around my neck, humming a quiet, happy note.

Once I was satisfied that my Bug was regulated and smiling, I stood up and moved over to the other sofa. I leaned down and wrapped my arms around Jessica, pulling my older sister into a solid embrace.

"Happy Thanksgiving, Michael," Jessica smiled warmly, squeezing my shoulders. "It's so good to actually have you here."

"I missed my two favorite women," I whispered.

Rachel beamed from her pile of blankets, and Jessica’s smile widened affectionately. But almost immediately, a subtle, calculating crease appeared on Jessica’s forehead. Her dark eyes darted briefly toward the dark hallway leading to Delores's bedroom.

"We missed you too, Michael," Jessica said, her voice dropping into a very quiet, careful whisper. "But do me a favor. Maybe don't use the phrase 'favorite women' when Delores is in earshot."

I blinked, genuinely confused. "Why? You ladies are my sisters."

"I know that, and you know that," Jessica replied gently, giving my arm a reassuring pat. "But new girlfriends don't always understand our pack dynamic. Some people can be a little sensitive about where they rank. Just keep it in mind to keep the peace, okay?"

I nodded, accepting her logistical advice. My analytical brain logged it as standard relationship maintenance, entirely unaware that Jessica’s pragmatic processor was already quietly flagging Delores's highly territorial behavior as a severe structural threat.

Rachel abandoned her loveseat, dragging her blanket with her, and climbed up onto Jessica's couch. She patted the space right between them. I happily obliged, slotting myself dead center on the cushions. The seating geometry was a tight, logistically perfect squeeze. Rachel pressed her side flush against my ribs, throwing her arm over my chest, while Jessica leaned against my other shoulder, wrapping her arm around my back.

Jessica's lightweight, manual travel wheelchair was folded neatly out of the way against the far wall, leaving us completely enclosed in our own perimeter. I was perfectly sandwiched between them, fully enveloped in the deep, restrictive pressure I had been starved of for months. We just sat there for a while, holding each other tightly, desperate to feel like a complete family again.

Jessica reached for the television remote on the wooden end table, navigating the channels until she found the live broadcast.

"Perfect," Jessica announced as the brightly colored marching bands and massive balloons filled the screen. "We are right on time for _The Hudson’s Thanksgiving Day Parade_."

We settled in to watch the broadcast, the comfortable, easy banter of my childhood rushing back into my system. We were pointing out the massive character balloons and critiquing the marching bands when the hallway floorboards creaked.

I looked up. Delores was standing at the edge of the living room, wearing her robe.

Her eyes immediately locked onto the sofa. I saw her gaze track the way Rachel’s arm was draped across my chest, and the way my head was resting near Jessica’s shoulder. For a fraction of a second, a sharp, undeniable frown pulled at the corners of Delores's mouth. Her posture went rigid.

But just as quickly as the micro-expression appeared, it vanished. She smoothed her features into a polite, accommodating smile, effortlessly masking the sudden shift in her baseline.

"Good morning, everyone," Delores said, walking over to the small kitchenette to grab bowls from the cabinet. "Happy Thanksgiving."

"Morning," I smiled back, completely miscategorizing her brief frown as morning grogginess. I gently untangled myself from my sisters just enough to sit up straight, giving Delores her space as the host.

Delores brought a large box of generic cereal into the living room, setting it on the end table along with four bowls and spoons.

Rachel immediately started to unspool herself from my side. "I can grab the milk from the fridge," she offered eagerly, ready to help with the breakfast logistics.

"I can reach it if you want to stay seated, Rachel," Jessica chimed in, leaning forward. "My travel chair is right there against the wall."

"Oh, we don't have any," Delores replied casually, pouring the dry cereal into her bowl. "I'm lactose intolerant. We just eat it dry."

"Ah. Okay, cool. Dry cereal it is," Jessica nodded politely, accepting her bowl without a second thought.

I took my own bowl, crunching on the dry squares. My analytical brain logged the dietary parameter instantly. It made perfect, logical sense; if the primary resident couldn't process dairy, there was no reason to waste money stocking it. I accepted the rule at absolute face value, entirely unaware that the absence of a simple gallon of milk was the quiet, invisible beginning of my dietary control.

Delores sat down in the armchair across from the sofas, eating her dry cereal while we turned our attention back to the television.

"Look at the scale of that float," Rachel rasped happily, pointing at a massive, glittering pirate ship rolling down the streets of Empire City. "The structural engineering required to balance that on a single chassis is incredible."

"I just want to know how they navigate the streetlights with the giant balloons," I added, laughing. "You know somebody has to be doing the wind-shear math."

"Delores, have you ever been up to the city to see the parade in person?" Jessica asked, making a deliberate, polite effort to pull my girlfriend into the pack's conversation.

Delores took a slow bite of her cereal, keeping her eyes fixed on the television screen. "No. Too crowded."

"Yeah, the crowds would probably be a nightmare," I agreed, trying to bridge the gap. "But the spectacle must be amazing."

Delores just offered a terse, non-committal shrug, refusing to engage with the topic any further.

I leaned back against the sofa cushions, feeling a vague, confusing friction settling over the room. We tried a few more times to pull her into our commentary about the parade performances, but her answers remained clipped and distinctly bothered. I couldn't figure out the social math. My sisters were being perfectly friendly, and I was doing my best to be an attentive partner, but Delores was radiating a quiet, simmering annoyance.

# Chunk 4: The Kitchen Ambush (Thursday Afternoon)

The comfortable sanctuary of the living room held steady into the early afternoon. By two o'clock, the parade broadcast had ended, but the three of us remained anchored to the sofa, happily chatting and enjoying the rare, uninterrupted proximity of our pack.

The layout of the Portsmouth apartment was compact and highly compartmentalized. If you were walking down the main hallway from the bedrooms, the small kitchen sat immediately to the right, sectioned off by a mid-height divider wall. The back of our long sofa ran directly parallel to that walkway, acting as a physical barricade that defined the living room space to the left.

The floorboards in the hallway creaked.

I assumed Delores was finally coming out to start prepping the massive Thanksgiving meal. I didn't turn my head immediately, but I felt Jessica suddenly go completely rigid beside me.

Jessica had the optimal angle. She looked up, her dark eyes tracking Delores’s movement from the hallway into the kitchen space. I saw Jessica’s jaw clench, her expression instantly dropping into a mask of pure, clinical disbelief mixed with a flash of pragmatic disgust.

I turned my head to see what had triggered my older sister’s firewall.

Delores was standing in the kitchen. Because of the height of the divider wall and the back of our sofa, her lower half was entirely obscured from our sightline. But from the waist up, she was completely, undeniably naked.

My processor violently stalled.

She hadn't just forgotten a shirt. She was parading around the kitchen space in nothing but her skin. She reached over, twisting the dials on the stove to preheat the oven, completely ignoring the fact that my two sisters were sitting ten feet away staring at her bare chest.

"It gets suffocatingly hot in this tiny kitchen when the oven is running all afternoon," Delores announced casually, pulling a heavy roasting pan onto the counter. "This way I don't overheat while I cook."

I sat frozen on the cushions. My analytical brain desperately searched for a social script to handle the situation, but the parameters were entirely missing. I knew adults engaged in physical intimacy behind closed doors, but parading completely naked in front of your boyfriend’s siblings while preparing a holiday meal shattered every established neurotypical boundary I had ever logged. I felt my face flush hot with nervous, scrambling confusion. I didn't know if I was supposed to look away, say something, or pretend it was normal.

Rachel reacted instantly to the sudden, chaotic boundary violation. She didn't say a word, but she shrank back into the sofa, pulling her knees up and wrapping both of her arms fiercely around my bicep. I could feel her trembling. The unpredictable, highly inappropriate environment was frightening her, pushing her nervous system toward an overload.

Jessica didn't yell. She didn't call Delores out on the blatant, coercive territorial display. She bypassed the psychological warfare entirely and attacked the action with cold, executive logic.

"Delores, are you sure that's a good idea?" Jessica asked, her voice tight and impeccably polite. "You're going to be cooking a massive turkey. If hot oil spits from the pan, you have absolutely zero thermal protection. You're risking severe burns."

Delores paused, her hands resting on the edge of the counter. She had expected shock, embarrassment, or an argument she could win. She hadn't calculated that the Quantum TPM would simply critique her safety protocols.

"I know how to cook in my own kitchen, Jessica," Delores snapped, the sugary host facade slipping to reveal a sharp, defensive edge.

"I'm just looking out for your safety," Jessica replied smoothly, holding her gaze without blinking.

The kitchen descended into a tense, radioactive silence. Delores banged a few pots and pans around, attempting to hold her ground, but the complete lack of the desired emotional reaction from our side of the divider was clearly ruining her power play.

After five agonizing minutes, Delores let out a loud, frustrated huff of air. She turned her back, marching out of the kitchen and down the hallway toward her bedroom.

"It's my house," Delores grumbled loudly enough for us to hear. "I should be able to be comfortable in my own home."

She returned a minute later, having shoved a robe on, her posture radiating a dark, simmering hostility.

Rachel squeezed my arm tighter, burying her face into my shoulder to block out the tension. Jessica reached across my chest, resting her hand reassuringly on Rachel's knee. I sat entirely still in the middle of the sofa, a heavy, confusing knot twisting in my stomach. I had no idea what social protocol I was supposed to be running. I just wanted my girlfriend to be happy and my sisters to feel welcome, but the atmosphere in the apartment had grown thick and suffocating, and I had absolutely no idea how to fix it.

# Chunk 5: The Tactical Extraction (Thursday Evening)

The Thanksgiving meal was an exercise in excruciating, mechanical endurance.

We gathered around the small dining table. The food Delores had prepared was sitting in front of us, but my appetite had completely vanished. The air in the apartment was incredibly heavy. Delores sat across from me, her jaw tight, offering clipped, one-word answers whenever Jessica or I tried to initiate standard dinner conversation.

Rachel barely spoke at all. She ate her food quickly, keeping her chair pulled as close to mine as physically possible without overlapping the legs of the furniture.

Jessica, however, was a portrait of absolute corporate composure. She ate her turkey, complimented the seasoning, and navigated the tense silence with the frictionless diplomacy of a senior executive managing a failing project. But behind her tinted glasses, I could tell her pragmatic brain was rapidly calculating logistics.

As soon as the last fork was set down, Jessica reached into her pocket and pulled out her silver cell phone.

"Thank you so much for the meal, Delores," Jessica said, her voice perfectly polite but laced with an undeniable, unyielding authority. She dialed a number, holding the phone to her ear. "Yes, I need a taxi dispatched to Portsmouth, please."

Delores blinked, her defensive posture suddenly faltering. "A taxi? Where are you going?"

"I've booked a hotel down the road for the night," Jessica explained smoothly, snapping her phone shut. "We've been crowding your apartment all day, and with Rachel and me taking up the living room, it's just too cramped. We are going to give you your space back. Michael, go grab your bag."

I froze in my chair. "Wait, I'm going with you?"

"Of course," Jessica smiled, standing up and reaching for her messenger bag. "We want to spend some time with our brother on Thanksgiving night."

Delores’s face flushed a deep, frantic red. The realization that Jessica wasn't just retreating to a hotel, but was actively taking me with her for the night, caused her defensive hostility to instantly collapse into sheer panic.

She lunged across the small space between the table and the hallway, grabbing my arm before I could even reach the bedroom to get my bag.

"Michael, please," Delores gasped. The anger was completely gone, instantly replaced by massive, welling crocodile tears. "Please don't leave. I didn't mean to make everyone uncomfortable."

My processor stalled. The sudden shift from cold silence to weeping desperation was entirely overwhelming.

"I was just so hot," Delores sobbed, burying her face against my chest, her tears soaking through my shirt. "The oven was running for hours, and the apartment is so small, I felt like I was suffocating. I just wanted to take my clothes off to cool down in my own kitchen, and now your sisters hate me. They think I'm a terrible person, but I was just hot!"

"Delores, no one hates you," I stammered, awkwardly patting her back. My threat-detection system, which had been buzzing with anxiety all afternoon, suddenly pivoted to deep, guilt-ridden empathy. Her logic made literal sense—ovens generate heat, and it _was_ her apartment.

"Then stay," she pleaded, looking up at me with wet, desperate eyes. "Please stay here with me."

"The taxi is waiting outside," Jessica announced from the front door. She didn't raise her voice, but she didn't offer a single inch of compromise. She had the door propped open, and Rachel was already standing out in the hallway, clutching her duffel bag.

I looked at Delores, my chest tight. "I'll be back first thing in the morning," I promised, gently untangling her hands from my shirt. "It's just for the night. They flew a long way."

Delores let out a dramatic, heartbroken sniffle, wrapping her robe tighter around herself as I grabbed my heavy nylon backpack. She stood in the doorway, playing the role of the abandoned, misunderstood girlfriend to absolute perfection as I walked out and followed my sisters down to the waiting cab.

# Chunk 6: The Golden Carrot (Thursday Night)

The hotel room was expansive, quiet, and completely neutral. The second the heavy door clicked shut behind us, the suffocating, atmospheric pressure of Delores's apartment vanished.

Rachel immediately dropped her bag and collapsed face-first onto one of the two queen beds, letting out a long, ragged exhale. I set my heavy backpack down near the desk, feeling the exhausting whiplash of the day settling deep into my bones.  

Jessica wheeled her travel chair over to the small table by the window, adjusting her glasses. She didn't yell, and she didn't aggressively attack Delores's character, because she knew my nineteen-year-old brain was already actively defending my girlfriend.  

"I'm sorry we had to leave so abruptly, Michael," Jessica said calmly. "But Rachel is seventeen. I am acting as her guardian on this trip, and as her guardian, I cannot allow her to stay in an environment where an adult is walking around without clothes on."  

The words hit my processor, finally cutting through the confusing fog of Delores's crocodile tears. Seventeen. Rachel was seventeen. She was legally a minor. The mathematical reality of what had just happened suddenly clicked into place. Even if Delores was genuinely hot from the oven, parading around completely naked in front of a kid was an absolute, inexcusable boundary violation.

"You're right," I muttered, the guilt suddenly weighing heavier than the exhaustion. "She shouldn't have been subjected to that. I'll... I'll talk to Delores about it tomorrow."

As soon as the words left my mouth, they felt hollow. I knew, with terrifying certainty, that bringing this up to Delores would spark a massive, screaming argument, and my voice wavered slightly at the thought.

Jessica caught the hesitation instantly. She tilted her head, giving me a sharp, penetrating look through her tinted glasses.

"You need to do right by Rachel," Jessica stated, her tone dropping into a firm, unyielding command.

I swallowed hard, my chest tightening as my intense fear of conflict battled my protective instincts. "I will," I agreed reluctantly, shifting my weight and looking down at the carpet.

Jessica’s right eyebrow arched upward. Her analytical brain clearly registered the sheer, unnatural terror hiding behind my reluctance. But she didn't push. She didn't interrogate me about why I was so afraid to talk to my own girlfriend. She just let the silence hold for a second, filing the quiet, alarming data point away, before leaving the confrontation alone.

Instead, Jessica looked at me, her dark eyes filled with a profound, aching empathy. She recognized exactly what was happening on a logistical level. She knew that I was terrified of being alone, and that I was clinging to the milestone of having a local, live-in girlfriend.  

"Michael, I want you to listen to me very carefully," Jessica said, her voice dropping into a gentle, absolute promise. "You do not have to live like this. If you are ever unhappy here, or if things ever get too overwhelming... the door to Washington State is always open."  

I looked up at her, my processor halting.  

"You can pack your bags tomorrow, or you can pack them a year from now," Jessica continued, holding my gaze. "But if you ever want to leave, you can come live with me and Rachel. We have the space. We will always have the space for you."  

Rachel rolled over on the bed, sitting up to look at me. "You can sleep in my bed as much as you want, Bear," she added softly, her raspy voice completely genuine. "You'd never have to be alone."  

The offer was incredible. It was a massive, unconditional safety net. But as my analytical brain ran the variables, a cold spike of pure, geographic terror hit my chest. Washington State was 2,800 miles away. It was a complete leap into the void. When Ashley had offered me an apartment just a few hours west in Dayton, I had panicked. The thought of abandoning the grid I knew in Hampton Roads felt mathematically impossible.  

And deeper than the geography was the quiet fear in the back of my head: If I leave her, I lose my relationship. I lose the milestone.  

"I appreciate it, Jess," I whispered, looking back down at my sneakers. "I really do. But I'm okay here. Delores is just stressed about the holiday. Everything is going to be fine."  

Jessica didn't push. She didn't force the issue or demand that I pack my bags. She just nodded slowly, accepting my decision.  

"The offer stands," Jessica promised quietly. "Forever."
