# Lore Bible: The Wingman Briefing

**Timestamp:** Thursday, May 13, 1999 (Two days before the Brewster Gardens Spring Sound Showcase) **Location:** The quiet hallway outside the Morrison Middle School Band Room **Characters:** Ashley Raybourn and Bonnie Reed

**The Context:** For the past seven months, Bonnie has been fully integrated into the physical baseline of the percussion section's perimeter. She has shared deep-pressure pack hugs, leaned into heavy, grounding pack cuddles, and held hands with Michael in crowded hallways. To Michael, this was purely an extension of his survival algorithm—adding a new trusted person to his physical tether. But to Bonnie, who has developed a massive crush on him, the constant platonic physical contact is agonizingly confusing, especially because he does the exact same things with Ashley.

## The Scene:

The dismissal bell hadn't rung yet, but Ms. Pitizer had given the percussion section the last ten minutes of class to pack up their gear. The back of the room was a chaotic mess of snare drums being zipped into cases and Sloane loudly bragging about something irrelevant.

Ashley slammed her locker shut, grabbed her backpack, and looked over at Bonnie. Bonnie was watching Michael from across the room. Michael was currently untangling a mess of xylophone mallets, completely oblivious to the world around him.

Ashley recognized the exact, pining, hesitant look on Bonnie's face. It was time to intervene.

"Hey, Bonnie," Ashley said, grabbing the sleeve of Bonnie's t-shirt. "Walk with me for a second. Let the boys finish the mallets."

Bonnie blinked, surprised, but nodded. She followed Ashley out the heavy double doors of the band room and into the quiet, empty tile hallway.

The second the doors swung shut, cutting off the noise of the snare drums, Ashley dropped her backpack onto the floor and crossed her arms. She didn't believe in middle-school games or beating around the bush. She possessed a tactical, terrifying level of emotional intelligence, and she deployed it instantly.

"So," Ashley said, leaning against the cinderblock wall. "When are you going to make a move?"

Bonnie froze. Her face instantly flushed a brilliant, panicked crimson. She looked frantically down the hallway, terrified someone might have heard. "What? Make a move on who?"

"Don't do that," Ashley said, her tone entirely casual but deeply observant. "I see the way you look at him, Bonnie. And I see the way you hold back. Every time we do a group cuddle on the bus, or every time he grabs your hand to pull you through the hallway, you tense up. You like him."

Bonnie swallowed hard, looking down at her sneakers. The jig was completely up. She wrung her hands together, the anxiety of stepping on a landmine completely overwhelming her.

"Ashley, I swear I'm not trying to cause problems," Bonnie stammered, her voice dropping to a nervous whisper. "I know how close you guys are. I see the way he holds you. I see the way you guys protect each other. I would never try to get in the middle of whatever you two have going on—"

"Stop right there," Ashley interrupted, holding up a hand. Her expression shifted from casual to fiercely serious. Her face physically contorted in disgust. "Ew. Bonnie, no. Gross."

Bonnie blinked, completely thrown off balance. "Gross?"

"Michael is my brother," Ashley stated with absolute, iron-clad certainty. "He is my best friend in the entire world, and he is my brother. Thinking about him any other way makes me want to throw up."

Bonnie let out a slow, shaky breath, her shoulders instantly dropping a fraction of an inch. "But... the hugs? The hand-holding? You guys are always touching."

"Because that's how his brain works," Ashley explained softly, shifting into her role as the pack's sentinel. "Michael operates on physical boundaries. The world is really loud and really chaotic for him, and physical touch—heavy, grounding touch—is how he resets his nervous system. He holds my hand so he knows where I am. He hugs me because the pressure calms him down. It’s a survival mechanism, Bonnie. It's not romantic."

Ashley paused, letting the information sink in before delivering the most important piece of data.

"And for the record," Ashley added, a genuine, warm smile breaking across her face, "he does the exact same thing with Rachel."

Bonnie tilted her head, confused. "Who is Rachel?"

"His little sister," Ashley said. "His actual, biological sister. She's in sixth grade, so she's not on our band schedule, but she's the center of his universe. The three of us—me, Michael, and Rachel—we're the Trio. That is his core pack. I am his honorary sister, and I am telling you right now: the perimeter is safe. I'm not your competition, Bonnie. I'm your wingman."

Bonnie stared at Ashley, a profound wave of relief washing over her. The terrifying, confusing social geometry of the percussion section suddenly made perfect sense.

"He likes you, Bonnie," Ashley said gently, dropping the final piece into place. "I see the way he looks at you, too. He just doesn't know what to do with it. His brain doesn't have the vocabulary for romance. To him, you're just a new, really great person in his pack."

"So... what do I do?" Bonnie asked, her voice steadying.

"You upgrade the math," Ashley grinned, her eyes sparkling with excitement for the upcoming weekend. "We have the Brewster Gardens trip on Saturday. When he hugs you? Don't just give him the heavy, dead-weight pack squeeze. Hold on a little longer. When he grabs your hand? Interlace your fingers. He needs you to change the physical data so his brain realizes what's happening. And when he panics because he doesn't know what the feeling is?"

Ashley picked her backpack up off the floor and slung it over her shoulder.

"I'll be sitting right there to translate it for him. You have my absolute blessing, Reed. Go get my brother."
