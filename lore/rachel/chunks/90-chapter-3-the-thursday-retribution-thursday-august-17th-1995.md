SPLIT SCREEN / SAME TIME
---

The windshield wipers of Megan’s compact car squeaked frantically, pushing away the muggy, late-summer Virginia drizzle.

Her hands were gripping the steering wheel so tightly her knuckles were stark white, but she couldn't stop them from shaking.

She was driving down Volvo Parkway, but her mind was completely disconnected from the road. The tears were flowing hot and fast down her cheeks, blurring the streetlights into jagged streaks of red and yellow.

She had locked every attendance roster, liability waiver, and employment file inside the heavy steel office safe just as Master Ray Shackford had ordered her to do on the phone, and she had handed the keys to Tidewater Tumblers directly to Sergeant Gable so the police could seal the crime scene. Even though Master Ray had been furious at Curtis Maddox and had stood behind her 100%, she knew she could never step foot inside that building again.

A choked, jagged sob tore out of her throat.

She thought about the little boy's stomach. The sheer, horrific geometry of that footprint. And she thought about the two older kids—Michael and Rachel—limping out into the parking lot, completely broken, while she sat at the front desk and smiled at their parents.

*How could I not know?* she agonized, hitting her steering wheel with the palm of her hand. *How could I sit ten feet away and not hear them?*

But the darkest, most nauseating thought was how fast a sanctuary could be corrupted.

Megan wiped her eyes roughly, her breath catching as a flood of childhood memories collided with tonight's horror.

Ten years ago, she had been a Tidewater Tumblers kid.

She remembered the smell of the chalk dust, the bright blue spring floors, and the upbeat pop music playing over the speakers while she practiced her back handsprings and vault routines. She remembered feeling so safe, so strong, and so proud in that building under Master Ray Shackford and Master April.

And Curtis Maddox had started out as an adult student here, taught by April, Ray, and the other instructors—only recently applying for and receiving a junior teaching role as part of getting ready to test for his 3rd Dan.

A fresh wave of nausea rolled over her, forcing her to roll down the driver's side window to let the cold, wet air hit her face.

In just a matter of weeks on the Thursday rotation, while Ray was away from the building, Curtis had turned the back room behind the closed blinds into his own sadistic proving ground—taking his rage at failing his 3rd Dan test out on defenseless children.

The absolute, profound violation of her childhood sanctuary shattered her.

Megan pulled her car into the driveway of her apartment complex, slammed the gear shift into park, and dropped her head onto the steering wheel, weeping uncontrollably into the dark.


The relentless, strobing flashes of Rossi’s camera finally stopped. The crime scene technician lowered his rig, the whine of the capacitor recharging cutting through the dead silence of the martial arts room.

He had photographed every microscopic ridge in the floor wax, mapped the exact trajectory of the "Quick Steps," and taken macro-lensed, hyper-detailed shots of the two anchored heel prints where Curtis had lingered to crush Michael and Rachel. Rossi followed up by using sterile cotton swabs to collect the dried sweat pooled near the toe impressions for DNA typing.

"I've got it all," Rossi said, snapping his hard-shell Pelican case shut. "The physical footprint of the assault is fully documented."

Sergeant Gable stood near the wooden grab bars, staring at the floor. He had been on the Chesapeake police force for nearly two decades. He had worked homicide, vice, and domestic violence. He had seen the absolute worst of what human beings were capable of doing to one another in the heat of a drunken rage or a drug deal gone bad.

But this room was different. This wasn't a crime of passion. It was a cold, calculated, architectural execution.

Gable let out a heavy, ragged exhale. The seasoned professionalism he wore like armor suddenly cracked, giving way to the raw, exhausted humanity of a father who just couldn't make the math work.

"Rossi," Gable murmured, his voice thick with a profound, simmering disgust. He pointed his flashlight at the floor, illuminating the space between the massive footprints where the children had been lying. "What the hell does a ten-year-old boy do to deserve this?"

Rossi stood up, his own face grim in the shadows. He didn't have an answer.

"And an eight-year-old girl," Gable continued, his voice rising, the anger bleeding through the cracks. "He anchored his weight on a little girl. And the other kid in the emergency room right now... he’s six. A kindergartener, Rossi. Just walked right over his internal organs."

Gable ran a heavy hand over his face, shaking his head. He looked around the perimeter of the room, imagining the line of adult students standing at attention.

"But you know what makes me the sickest?" Gable asked, his jaw clenching so tight his teeth ground together. "The adults. The mother who brought the six-year-old to the ER said the lobby was full. Megan said there were grown men walking out of this room laughing when the class ended. A dozen adults stood on this floor, watched a two-hundred-pound man systematically crush the breath out of three children, and *nobody did a damn thing*."

"Cult mentality, Sarge," Rossi said quietly, hauling his heavy case toward the door. "They're brainwashed. To them, the guy at the front of the room is God."

"Well, God is about to get booked into the city jail," Gable spat.

He walked over to the doorway and picked up the heavy brass keys that Megan had thrown onto the linoleum. He didn't bag them for evidence; he needed them for logistics.

"Let's pack it up," Gable ordered. "We're done here."

The officers moved out of the Dojang, hitting the switches and plunging the room back into darkness. They did a final, tactical sweep of the massive gymnastics floor, checking the locker rooms, the bathrooms, and the administrative office to ensure the building was entirely vacant.

Once the team was out in the muggy August night, standing under the awning, Gable pulled the heavy glass double doors shut.

He inserted Megan’s brass key into the lock and threw the deadbolt. The heavy metallic *click* echoed across the empty plaza.

But a simple lock wasn't enough. Tidewater Tumblers was an active crime scene, and Curtis Maddox—who still carried an instructor's key on his keyring—could not be allowed to sneak back inside overnight to tamper with the floor wax or sanitize the Dojang before the detectives executed their arrest and search warrants in the morning.

Gable walked over to the trunk of his police cruiser. He pulled out a thick roll of red and white, tamper-evident tape printed with the words: **CRIME SCENE - DO NOT CROSS - POLICE SEAL**.

He walked back to the entrance and stretched the heavy adhesive tape horizontally across the seam of the double glass doors, pressing it firmly against the handles and the glass. He ran a second strip vertically, creating a massive, highly visible cross that physically and legally sealed the threshold.

If Curtis Maddox, or any of his adult "Iron Shirt" disciples, tried to put a key in that door and break the tape, they would be immediately arrested for tampering with a crime scene.

Gable stepped back, looking up at the cheerful, brightly colored "Tidewater Tumblers & Martial Arts" sign glowing above the strip mall unit.

"Alright, building is secured and sealed," Gable announced to his team, tossing the brass keys into the passenger seat of his cruiser. "Let's head back to the precinct and check on the status of that arrest warrant. Curtis's reign is officially over."
