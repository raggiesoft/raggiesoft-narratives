# Lore Bible: The Diplomatic Credential (The OFM ID Card)

## I. The Front of the Card (The Blue Border)

The OFM Diplomatic ID is the size of a standard credit card but carries the visual weight of the federal government.

- **The Header:** Across the very top, in bold, undeniable federal lettering, it reads: **UNITED STATES DEPARTMENT OF STATE**.

- **The Seal:** Faded into the background of the card is a highly detailed watermark of the Great Seal of the United States (the eagle holding the olive branch and arrows).

- **The Color Code (The Blue Band):** This is the most critical feature. The OFM color-codes its IDs based on the level of immunity. Consular employees get green; administrative staff get red. April’s card features a **bright, solid blue border**, which is recognized instantly by federal law enforcement as the highest level: full, unquestionable criminal and civil immunity.

- **The Photograph:** A crisp, color photograph of April. She is not smiling. Her expression displays the vast, unbothered stillness of the *Doju*-in-training.

- **The Text Fields:**

  - **Name:** LEE HAE-JIN

  - **Title:** State Emissary / Cultural Attaché

  - **Mission:** REPUBLIC OF KOREA

  - **DOB:** \[Her Birth Date\]

  - **PID (Personal Identification Number):** A unique alphanumeric federal tracking code.

## II. The Back of the Card (The Guillotine)

If a local police officer doesn't immediately understand the gravity of the blue border on the front, the back of the card leaves absolutely zero room for interpretation.

- The back is printed in dense, black, authoritative text. It is a direct legal threat to any law enforcement officer who attempts to interfere with her.

- **The Warning Text:** \> *"The bearer of this card is registered with the U.S. Department of State and is entitled to full diplomatic immunity in accordance with the Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations. The bearer shall not be liable to any form of arrest or detention. The bearer's person, residence, papers, and property are inviolable."*

- **The DSS Hotline:** Directly beneath the warning is a 24-hour toll-free federal hotline number specifically for law enforcement, instructing them to contact the Diplomatic Security Service (DSS) immediately to verify her status before taking any action.

## III. The Interaction on Tuesday

When Curtis proudly brings the Chesapeake Police into the back office, he expects the cops to ask for her Virginia driver's license.

Instead, April calmly retrieves this heavily watermarked, blue-banded federal credential and hands it over. The local officer flips it around, reads the explicit, federally mandated warning on the back telling him that touching her is an international crime, and immediately realizes Curtis has just tricked him into harassing a protected foreign diplomat.

The aesthetic of the card itself—completely devoid of local Chesapeake authority and dripping with federal and international power—is what instantly shatters Curtis’s plan.
