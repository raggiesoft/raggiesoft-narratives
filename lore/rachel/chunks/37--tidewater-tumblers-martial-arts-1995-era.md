# 🥋 Tidewater Tumblers & Martial Arts (1995 Era)

## Facility & Affiliation

- **Tidewater Tumblers & Martial Arts:** The dual-purpose gymnastics and Dojang facility at 912 Professional Place in Chesapeake, Virginia (equivalent to the original Ocean Tumblers facility). Originally opened as a gymnastics center in the late 1970s (featuring late-1970s wood panel backing on the walls of its second-floor observation deck bleachers overlooking the main gymnastics spring floor), the facility received its license to operate as a franchised branch of Pinnacle Mastery School of Karate in 1982.

- **The Regional Testing Center:** Because Master Ray signed the very first franchise agreement with Pinnacle in 1982, Tidewater Tumblers serves as the official regional testing hub for all Pinnacle branches in the Hampton Roads area. Quarterly belt tests are held on the main gymnastics spring floor while parents and observers sit in the second-floor observation deck bleachers above. Testing is conducted strictly by belt class in rank order—every color belt class and belt-with-stripe class (White Belts, White Belts with stripe, Yellow Belts, Purple Belts, Green Belts, Green Belts with stripe, etc.) tests as a class, followed by individual *Poom* candidates, and finally individual *Dan* candidates.

- **Professional Place:** The street location of the facility in Chesapeake, Virginia.

- **April's Garage:** A fully sanctioned, licensed, and permitted off-site facility where Master April holds sensory-friendly Open Gyms on Fridays on colorful puzzle mats.

He outranks everyone and answers only to the 9th Dan Grandmasters (like Master Lee Dong-won) in South Korea. He travels to the site exclusively to administer quarterly belt testing and grades students on their actual biomechanical effort rather than rigid traditionalism.

She is trauma-informed, deeply protective of her students, and an absolute scholar of the *Art's* true biomechanics. She actively despises Curtis's reliance on blunt-force conditioning.

He sacrificed his own rank progression to build the business. He is older than April but respectfully and willingly executes a formal bow to her due to her superior rank. For the two years Michael and Rachel train at the Chesapeake Tidewater Tumblers (1993–1995), Ray is a warm, honorable headmaster—which is why the siblings happily remain at the school. Because Curtis Maddox had been a known student at Tidewater Tumblers since 1988, had passed a clean Virginia State Police criminal background check when entering the Junior Instructor program just before his 2nd Dan test in 1993, and had served as the Junior Instructor at Tidewater Tumblers' Virginia Beach location without incident (since his offsite *Iron Shirt Underground* consisted of consenting adults who never filed police reports), Ray trusted Curtis completely when rotating him to the Chesapeake location on Thursdays ahead of his August 1995 3rd Dan exam. The moment Megan gets Ray on the phone with Drew's mother Claire on Friday morning and Ray learns what Curtis did to the children, Ray is furious with Curtis: he immediately offers to pay 100% of Drew's emergency room costs, orders Megan to lock every record in the safe for the police, explicitly agrees that breaking children in half and stepping on them has zero place in martial arts, and immediately calls Master April off-camera to report everything Megan and Claire told him. ## The Dobok (Uniform) & Rank Rules

- **The Pinnacle Mastery Patch:** *Every* student and instructor, regardless of rank, must wear the official Pinnacle Mastery School of Karate patch on their uniform. While a student can purchase a standard white *dobok* anywhere (through the Dojang or a third-party sporting goods store), the proprietary patch must be ordered and purchased directly through their specific franchise owner (e.g., Master Ray at Tidewater Tumblers). Once it arrives, it is affixed to the uniform.

- **Under-18 Black Belts (Poom Rank):** Students under 18 who earn a black belt are classified as Junior Black Belts (*Poom*). They wear a half-red, half-black collar and belt. At age 18, this automatically converts to an adult Dan rank.

- **Color Belts (White through Red):** Students wear a solid white cotton *dobok* with a plain white V-neck collar. Ranks (stripes) are denoted cheaply and efficiently by wrapping black electrical tape around one end of the belt.

- **Adult Black Belts (1st Dan to 3rd Dan):** Practitioners (including Master Ray and Master Curtis) wear a solid white *dobok* with a midnight blue V-neck collar. Their belts feature permanent gold embroidery—one gold bar per degree across the right end, and their name/school embroidered in gold Hangul on the left.

- **Master / Sabomnim (4th Dan to 6th Dan):** Upon reaching 4th Dan, practitioners (like Master April) earn the right to wear the solid midnight blue *dobok* with gold embroidery. Their belts drop the individual bars in favor of heavy gold Hangul script denoting their Master title.

- **Regional Director (7th Dan+):** Master Scott wears a highly distinguished solid yellow *dobok* with a midnight blue V-neck collar, visually separating him as the ultimate authority in the room.

## Curriculum & Techniques

- **Basic Form IV:** The pattern students must demonstrate mastery over (along with a specific set of punches, kicks, and blocks) to graduate from Green tti with a stripe and officially earn their Brown tti.

- **Basic Form V:** The complex, motor-chaining form that students learn and drill after acquiring their Brown belt.

- **Front Kick-Jump Front Kick (I-dan Ap Chagi):** The physically demanding technique introduced at the Green tti level that Michael’s CP prevents him from executing.

- **Seondu: Tradition and Form in Korean Karate:** The ultimate foundational text written by 9th (honorary 10th) Dan Grandmaster Lee Dong-won. It emphasizes true mechanics, safety, and leading by example. April studies it religiously; Curtis dismisses and hates it.
